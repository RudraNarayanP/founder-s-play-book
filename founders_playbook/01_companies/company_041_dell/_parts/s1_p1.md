### Which person owns which window — the registrant question, stated once and used everywhere below

## C. ORIGINAL PROBLEM

STATUS: WRITTEN

### C.1 The problem statement that exists in-window (three years late)

The earliest articulation of the problem this firm claimed to solve is printed in its own April 1987 advertisement, and it is a **distribution-and-price** problem, not a technology problem: *"PC's Limited has revolutionized the way America buys personal computers by manufacturing demonstrably better systems and then selling them directly to end users at remarkably low prices"* (87-AD@l22204-22207). Two features of that sentence carry the whole original frame: the object of attack is **the way computers are bought**, and the claimed solution pairs *making* with *selling direct*, i.e. the firm positions itself as a manufacturer that cut out an intermediary rather than as a retailer that added a factory. The October 1988 ad states the same mechanism as arithmetic the buyer can check: *"Because you buy it direct from us. Eliminating the mark-ups and margins of computer stores"* (88-AD@l42083-42086), with the price gap framed against the incumbent architecture of the day — *"The first 286 computer with a clock speed of 20 MHz… Yet it sells for much less than you may pay for a 386 computer"* (88-AD@l42076-42081).

Second component, the performance-per-dollar axis: *"Because PC's Limited gives you more speed with 8 and 12 MHz machines. Plus a megabyte of high-speed RAM"* (87-AD@l22214). In 1987 print, 1 MB of RAM and 12 MHz are the argument; there is no claim about software, services breadth, or enterprise relationships yet.

Third component, **risk removal** as part of the problem: a buyer who cannot inspect a machine in a store is offered substitutes — *"an unconditional 30-Day Money-Back Guarantee, and a full One-Year Limited Warranty"* and *"industry-leading free 800-line technical support"* (87-AD@l22220-22221), with the guarantee's operating terms printed in full (87-AD@l22463-22469). Direct selling does not merely remove a margin; it must remove the **inspection and recourse** the store used to supply, and the 1987 ad is already busy doing that.

Class for C.1: **CONTEMPORANEOUS OBSERVATION, company self-report**, Medium (TLS cap).

### C.2 The problem as the registrant later explained it (RESTATED, and where the firewall bites)

The 1994 filing supplies the polished version of the same claim and extends it backwards over the whole history: *"Since incorporating in 1984, the Company has grown rapidly because of the effectiveness and efficiency of its approach… its approach is driven by its direct interaction with the customer, thus avoiding high inventory and occupancy costs of physical stores… generally avoids the inefficiency and mark-ups associated with the typical industry model, where products move through a multi-step process involving manufacturers, distributors, retail stores and a variety of value-added service providers"* (94-10-K@l150-160). The 1996 filing turns it into a founding principle: *"The Company was founded on the principle that delivering computers custom-built to specific customer orders is the best business model for providing solutions that are truly relevant to end-user needs"* (96-10K405@l196-198).

Those are **RESTATED / (PB)** sentences about a 1984 intention, and the firewall rule (§2) applies to them directly: the 1994 language is written by the fifth-largest vendor in the world with $2.87 billion of sales, and cannot be used as evidence that a 1984 actor chose the model for the reasons given. Their evidential value here is narrower and real: they show that by 1994-04-01 the registrant was, under liability, describing its own continuity of approach back to the 1984 incorporation.

### D.2 The 1987 advertisement read as an experiment, not as decoration

Treated forensically, the April 1987 page is the earliest *testable* artifact this firm has left, and it is itself an experiment in a repeatable acquisition loop:

* **The stimulus is purchasable media.** A full-page advertisement in BYTE (circulation: the enthusiast/technical buyer), dated by its own footer to April 1987, page 81 (87-AD@l22496), carrying a **reader-service number**: *"Inquiry 214"* (l.22492). Reader-service cards give a direct-response advertiser a countable response channel — the mechanism by which the ad could be evaluated, and the reason the page is evidence of a *test* and not merely of a *presence*.
* **The conversion path is telephone, and it is segmented.** One number to buy (*"call us directly at . . . 1-800-426-5150"*), one for in-state callers (*"Calls inside Texas, 1-800-252-8336"*), one for technical support (*1-800-624-9896*), one for customer service (*1-800-624-9897*), a local sales line *(512) 339-6962*, a **company BBS** *(512) 339-4127*, MCI Mail, telex and fax (l.22478-22488), with published opening hours (l.22482). Segregating order-taking from support from service is an operational choice that only matters if call volume is being managed — an in-window signal of a running process, not of a hobby.
* **The risk-control device is procedural.** Returns require a *"PC's Limited Credit Return Authorization (CRA) on the shipping label"* and items *"as-new, not modified or damaged, with all warranty cards, manuals, and packaging intact"* (l.22463-22465). A CRA number is an internal control: it exists to track returns per customer and per unit, which is precisely the data a direct seller needs to keep a 30-day unconditional promise affordable.
