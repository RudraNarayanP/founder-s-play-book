   a decade it did not document. **Class: UNKNOWN for every Stage-1 failure mode**, with the route named in
   `## Untried` (U3 IPO-week 1988 trade print, U6 Austin business press).
2. **The company's own advertising tells on its brand deficit.** April 1987: "If you are just getting
   acquainted with us, there's never been a better time to find out what you've been missing"
   (Dell-PCSL-87A@l22222-22224). That sentence is only worth printing by a firm whose name is not known to its
   readers, three years in. **CONTEMPORANEOUS OBSERVATION (self-published), Medium** — it is the clearest
   in-window signal of low brand recognition, and it is the counterweight to any "the direct model won instantly"
   reading. No customer count, order count, or awareness measurement behind it.
3. **The 1988 offer was priced as an underdog claim, not a premium one.** "It's totally MS-DOS and MS OS/2
   compatible. Yet it sells for much less than you may pay for a 386 computer" (Dell-DELL-88B@l42078-42081) and
   "at less than half the price— more power for the money than any other system" (Dell-DELL-88B@l42305-42310).
   BYTE's reviewer puts it the same way from the outside: performance "comes at a price that's well below that
   of comparable systems" (Dell-BYTE-REV88B@l48990-48992). **Read as a negative signal, not a boast**: by
   October 1988 a firm with a federal supply contract, a preferred-stock investor and a national brand was still
   competing primarily on being cheaper than the 386 field, against Compaq, IBM and ALR, which are the machines
   BYTE names as its comparators (@l49058-49066, @l49246-49252). Margin implication: **UNKNOWN** — no cost data
   exists in-window.
4. **A documented capability limit, from the independent reviewer.** On the System 310's memory scheme: "if
   you install a proprietary 32-bit memory-expansion card, you must add another megabyte to the standard 1
   megabyte to achieve the full benefits of interleaved memory", and adding proprietary RAM can cost the
   full-length expansion-card position ("you won't have enough room to add a full-length expansion card in the
   8-bit slot") (Dell-BYTE-REV88B@l49180-49192). Also "This feature may be incompatible with future releases of
   DOS or OS/2, however, so Dell made it an option in the setup program" (@l49219-49223), and "While my review
   machine had an 8-bit VGA board, Dell says that the System 310 will be shipping with 16-bit boards by the time
   you read this. This is likely to change the video- and graphics-oriented benchmarks" (@l49256-49262).
   **CONTEMPORANEOUS OBSERVATION, High that the reviewer said it** — an upgrade-path and compatibility liability
   printed in the same issue as the advertisement, and a caution against treating the published benchmark result
   as a stable product property.
5. **The service promise was partly not Dell's to make.** "And, in the rare case, that your question can't be
   answered by an on-line technician, we'll send a Honeywell Bull technician by the next business day" and "A
   full year of on-site Honeywell Bull service is included within the purchase price of your Dell system"
   (Dell-DELL-88B@l42108-42115), and a second copy of the claim at @l42193-42196. **Two problems**: (i) the
   attribution is unsafe, because the same OCR column run carries other advertisers' copy — the probe's C-4,
   re-confirmed and widened on this pass (see U.110); (ii) if the sentence *is* Dell's, then the flagship
   "industry-leading support" claim rested on a third party's field-service network, i.e. a dependency the
   direct-model story normally omits. Either way the support apparatus cannot be claimed as wholly
   self-supplied: **UNKNOWN (who performed the on-site service)**.
6. **Financing was not available at the point of sale when it was advertised.** The lease offer in the October
   1988 advertisement is footnoted "Available January 1, 1989" and "Payments based on a 36-month open-end lease",
   with "In Canada, leasing is not currently available" (Dell-DELL-88B@l42813-42816; the December 1988 copy
   repeats it, Dell-DELL-88C@l34724). **FACT (as printed)** — the monthly-payment channel that made a $2,299-
   $7,699 catalogue feel affordable to small buyers was *prospective* in-window, one to two quarters ahead, and
   geographically patchy. The lessor is never named: **UNKNOWN**, and the credit risk-holder is therefore
   unknown too.
7. **The record's own negative space, stated so it cannot be mistaken for a finding.** Re-measured on this
   pass over all held text with spacing-tolerant patterns (OCR double-spaces the magazine and report layers, so
   single-space greps under-report): `"Dell Computer"` = **0** in the FY1994-10-K-era *founding* sense — the
   phrase occurs 168 times in the FY1994 filing but every occurrence is the 1994 registrant, not the window;
   `"PC's Limited"` = **0 in all nine SEC accessions (2,317,629 B)**; `"Michael Dell"` = **0** in all four BYTE
   issues held; `"Michael S. Dell"` appears in filings only from 1994. The founding decade therefore has **no
   self-description by its founder anywhere in this corpus**, and no third-party account of him.
8. **Four issues sampled out of a 159-item run is not a corpus.** Two of the four issues the probe held
   (March 1988, December 1988) were recorded as clean nulls; on re-measurement the **December 1988 issue is not
   a null** — it carries Dell copy at @l34765, @l34805, @l34815-34818, @l34856, @l34877 and a BYTE advertiser
   index entry "884 DELL COMPUTER 193" (@l108751), plus "DELL COMPUTERS (INT'L)" and "(N. AMER.)" entries
   (@l109329, @l109331). One of the two recorded nulls was a matcher artefact, not a fact (U.103), which bounds
   how much weight *any* §M silence in family (c) can carry.
9. **Eleven periodical items mined by the scripted harvest returned nothing, and three of the "hits" are
   anti-evidence.** `sources/periodicals/` also holds ten DTIC microfilm reports and three unrelated books whose
   only match on "Dell" is the English word: "LITTLE DELL LAKE" (Dell-ADA298471@l4, @l44, @l92;
   Dell-ADA425243@l1, @l61, @l104), "Susan J. Dell, Ph.D" (Dell-MICRO@l51, @l210) and "FOWLER DELL BROOKS"
   (Dell-CU3192@l103, @l129, @l154). `research/A4_harvest_mine.md` classes these `BARE_WORD_MATCH`, 0 entity
   hits. **This is the failure mode §14 rule 8 warns about, in periodical form**: a name-search over this shelf
   produces a lake in Salt Lake City, a surname and a 1921 author, and any Dell "periodical coverage" count that
   includes them is void.
10. **Every quantity in this part that is a company self-report is uncross-checked.** "tens of thousands of
    computers to date" (§L) has no counterpart in any held document, in filings or in press; the price grids
    have one partial check (BYTE's review reproduces two System 310 endpoints) and none for the PC's Limited
    grid. Independence (§3) therefore fails for the volume claim, and it is recorded at **Low** rather than
    being dropped.
11. **What the corpus cannot say about friction at all.** Nothing held speaks to component supply (Intel
    80386/80286 sourcing is only visible as a *product specification*, e.g. Dell-BYTE-REV88B@l49080-49084),
    cash conversion, inventory, credit, or the price of the guarantee (returns are priced nowhere). Those are
    not observed failures; they are **unobserved** ones, and the difference is the deliverable.
