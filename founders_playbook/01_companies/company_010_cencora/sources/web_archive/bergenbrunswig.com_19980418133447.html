History
Bergen Brunswig's First 100+ Years
-- A Capsule History and Beyond --

Bergen Brunswig, now in its 109th year of business, has come through a long and colorful history to take its place as the nation’s largest distributor of both pharmaceuticals and medical-surgical supplies.

The firm began in Los Angeles in 1888 when Lucien Brunswig, a French immigrant and pharmacist, and his partner, F.W. Braun, opened a wholesale apothecary in a double storefront downtown to serve a growing community of scarcely more than 30,000 people. The company flourished along with the city, expanding into San Diego, another fledgling community, a scant two years later.

In May of 1907, Braun sold his interest to Lucien Brunswig, and the firm became Brunswig Drug Company, a corporation, which expanded again to service several Southwest states. After Brunswig’s death in 1943, new president Roy V. Schwab moved the company into a 225,000 square-foot building in Vernon, California, considered to be the most advanced wholesale drug operation in the United States. Among many innovations developed in the new headquarters was a punch card tabulating system, the first to be used in the wholesale drug industry and the forerunner to one of the country’s most sophisticated distribution systems.

In 1949, with 14 full-line divisions operating in the Southwest, Brunswig Drug Company expanded into Central and Northern California through a merger with Coffin-Redington Company of San Francisco, a well-respected company founded during the gold rush years.

Meanwhile, in Bergen County, New Jersey, a successful pharmacist by the name of Emil P. Martini left the retail business to establish Bergen Drug Company. A wholesaler serving pharmacists in the local area, Bergen began operations in a converted garage in Hackensack. But by the time of the senior Martini’s death eight years later, the new company was serving retailers in three states from a 125,000 square-foot warehouse.

<Emil P. Martini, Jr., who had joined Bergen in 1952 after receiving a degree in pharmacy from Purdue University, was elected president of the company in 1956. That same year his brother, Robert E. Martini, came into the company after receiving his degree in pharmacy from Ohio State University. Carrying out their father’s plans for expansion nationwide, Emil Jr. and Robert took the company forward with the acquisition of several other wholesale concerns, including the 1969 purchase of Brunswig Drug Company in Los Angeles.

The new company, Bergen Brunswig Corporation, automated and streamlined its operations. It developed state-of-the-art electronic order entry devices and supportive merchandising systems for its customers. Using an expansion strategy that combined acquisition with internal growth, the company quickly moved into a leadership position within the industry.

With the passing of Emil Jr. in 1992, the company’s then president and chief operating officer, Robert Martini, took the helm as chairman of the board and chief executive officer.  

In the same year, Bergen made a major move into the growing area of medical-surgical supply distribution by acquiring Durr-Fillauer Medical, Inc. of Alabama. In the next three years, it acquired such outstanding companies as Southwestern Hospital Supply Corporation, Professional Medical Supply Company, Colonial Healthcare Supply and Biddle & Crowther Company. By 1995, the company’s medical-surgical supply subsidiary, now called Bergen Brunswig Medical Corporation, had become the fourth largest wholesaler of its kind in the United States.  

In January of 1997, Bergen Brunswig Corporation promoted its dynamic president, Donald R. Roden, to chief executive officer. It is a post to which he brings more than 25 years of experience in the healthcare industry, with proven skills in the areas of strategic planning, alliance negotiation and long-term growth initiatives. Robert Martini retains his position as chairman of the board, and his son, Brent Martini, continues the Martini tradition within the company as president of Bergen Brunswig Drug Company. 

Today, combining innovative management techniques with a philosophy of “Doing Today What Others Will Imitate Tomorrow,” Bergen Brunswig Corporation has evolved into a $9.9 billion enterprise, employing more than 4,700 people and operating 59 distribution centers in 29 states.

The company has four subsidiaries: Bergen Brunswig Drug Company (BBDC), Bergen Brunswig Medical Corporation (BBMC), IntePlex and Alternate Site Distributors (ASD).
