## A — Executive state summary

STATUS: WRITTEN 2026-10-07

At the open of Stage 1 the registrant is **a Delaware corporation that has done nothing**: formed
2001-03-16 as AABB Corporation, renamed AmeriSource-Bergen Corporation on the face of its own charter
exhibits, and describing itself in its own first financial statement as having *"not conducted any

listed on the NYSE and filed a second S-4 to exchange the notes.**

report of CIK 1140859 is on this shelf. The registrant's scale in Stage 1 is therefore **borrowed,
declared and unverified** — in that order.

## C — Original problem

STATUS: WRITTEN 2026-10-07

The problem the combination was assembled to solve, in the parties' own in-period words, is **scale
against purchasing power and duplicated logistics**, not a product gap. Ex-99.2 (2001-07-31) states the
merger "will enable us to significantly enhance our competitive position with: enhanced scale of

## D — First experiment

STATUS: WRITTEN 2026-10-07

**The first real-world experiment of this registrant was a financing performed by an entity with no
operations, and the record preserves it as an experiment with a visible result.** On 2001-07-31 the two
*predecessors* jointly announced "plans for AmerisourceBergen Corporation to issue $400 million Senior
Notes due 2008 … in a private placement … expected to be resold by the initial purchasers to qualified
institutional buyers under Rule 144A" (Ex-99.1). The named issuer did not yet own a business. Nineteen
days later the registrant's own closing release states the size changed: *"issuing $500 million of 8.125
percent senior notes and retiring the tendered bonds of its subsidiary, PharMerica"*, plus *"a new $1.3
billion credit facility, comprised of a $300 million term loan and a $1 billion revolving credit
facility"*. The exchange-offer S-4 of 2001-10-19 registers **$500,000,000 of 8 1/8% Senior Notes due
2008** with the operating subsidiaries as joint and several guarantors (`ds4.txt:97` and its fee table).
**What it demonstrated:** the capital market priced the *planned* combined entity above its own
announced size — a 25% upsizing, the only in-window outcome on this shelf that needs no later document.
**What it did not demonstrate:** that the merged operations would work; the notes were placed on pro
forma statements whose own carrier warns they *"do not reflect any adjustments to reflect any cost
savings or other synergies anticipated as a result of the merger"* (S-4 l.1558-1564). The second
experiment — the operating one, 51→~30 centers — has **no held result** (§L.3, FR-5).
