purchase year) cannot be used: no document in this evidence set mentions 1950, Bentonville's 1950
store, a purchase price, a rent, or a lease. A Stage 1 beginning in 1962 cannot be used either —
1962 is inside the founder-state period and the record's own 1945/1955 anchors would be cut off.
1945 is proposed **as the limit of what the registrant itself ever claimed for its origin**, not as
a documented event.

silent; between 1955 and 1962 it is silent; between 1962 and 1968 it is silent (the earliest

## Narrative notes


Section-tagged prose for the dossier body. Each note names the records it rests on and its
knowability status; nothing here may be lifted into the narrative without its class tag.

**§A/§B — the shape the record actually permits (KNOWABLE for 1968-1980; NOT KNOWABLE for
1945-1967).** The register's usable spine begins at a 1972 document looking back to a fiscal year
that closed on 31 January 1968, when 24 stores rang up $12,618,754 (W-01, W-25). Everything
"earlier" is company memory printed in 1973, 1976 and 1980 (W-27, W-49, W-50, W-63). The honest
sentence for §B is: *the corporate print names one pre-1962 business — Walton's Family Store,
Bentonville, with an office above it from 1955 — and one pre-1962 origin — a Ben Franklin franchised
store at Newport, Arkansas, from 1945 — and both are claims made in the 1970s about twenty-five and
fifty years earlier.* The right-hand column of the Pre-1962 ledger is all the rest, and it is memoir.

**§D — the first experiment, and what "first" can mean here.** The only 1962 in this corpus is
asserted, not witnessed: an FY1980 paragraph (W-63) and a museum catalogue entry for an advertisement
dated 1962 (W-77). The advert's slogan "Wal-Mart Lowers Living Cost" is the single piece of 1962
wording that survives — and it survives **as the company's own transcription on a page last modified
in 2026**, not as a document anyone read in 1962. No price, no item, no crowd, no opening-day sales
figure from 1962 exists in these files. Any §D that reads like a first day is being written from the
1992 memoir, and must be labelled that way.

**§E — format as filed, which is richer than the folklore.** The registrant describes a
multi-format operator from its first audited year: Discount Cities of 30,000-60,000 sq ft averaging
45,000 with 36 full-line departments and "over 35,000 items" (W-65), run alongside Ben Franklin
variety stores, "variety and family center stores" (W-37), Family Center conversions (W-35), two
Sav-Co Home Improvement Centers that under-performed and were all closed within five years (W-71),
leased shoe/jewelry/pharmacy departments whose lessees' sales the company reported (W-16, W-42, W-47,
W-72), and an experimental Pine Bluff "Project '79" store explicitly "planned, not as a prototype,
but to be an experiment" (W-71). This is the counter-hagiography material: the company's own print
documents formats it tried and stopped.

**§G — supply side, quantified for the first time in these files.** 80% of store purchases moved
through Bentonville in FY1976 and the balance shipped direct from suppliers (W-57); the DC shipped
$42,786,831 at cost in FY1973 against $124.9m of sales (W-36) — which bounds, in both directions, any
claim about how quickly company distribution displaced the wholesaler model the secondary literature
describes. Estate growth is contracted **ahead of opening** at every date the register can see: 6
future-store leases in FY1972, 8 in FY1973, 24 in FY1976, 38 in FY1980 (W-15, W-42, W-53, W-73).

**§I/§L — validation signals the company selected, and the ones it did not.** Selected: same-store
gains of 17% (FY1972, W-07), 11% (FY1973, W-32), 15% (FY1976, W-48) and 15.3% (FY1980, W-70); "sales
per square foot … well above the national average" against the untraced Cornell study (W-38); the
Forbes first-place ranking (W-76). Not selected, and printed anyway: the FY1973 same-store figure
**excludes** a store the company had cannibalised (W-32); three "older and most profitable stores"
lost or damaged by fire and tornado in the same year (W-35); FY1975 net income cut $2,347,000 by the
LIFO switch (W-51); FY1980's own SFAS 33 note saying constant-dollar earnings were $27.1m not $41.2m
(W-75); and earnings held near the Wage-and-Price ceiling (W-31).

**§K — money, as against the legend.** The 1970 offering is 200,000 shares on 8 October 1970 for
$3,030,467 net (W-03); the 1972 follow-on is 400,000 shares at $23.75 before expenses, about
$9,250,000 by the letter's rounding and $8,913,504 posted to paid-in (W-29, W-30); the stock did not
trade on the NYSE before 25 August 1972 (W-28); and expansion between FY1972 and FY1976 was funded by
retained earnings and bank borrowing until a single $15m convertible issue in May 1975 (W-48).
Covenants restricted $5,660,237 of retained earnings in 1973, $22,235,000 in 1976 and $50,021,000 in
1980 (W-41, W-53, W-73) — the dividend the company initiated only in fiscal 1975 (W-54) was, on its
own filing, the residue of a restricted-capital business.

**§M — negative signals.** Loss prevention as a formal division with interrogation and prosecution
functions by FY1976, and an "aggressive campaign against shrinkage" by FY1980 (W-60, W-71, W-76); a
related-party stockholder receivable in FY1971 (W-20); IRS reassessment of the pre-IPO group for 1969
and 1970 (W-06, W-40); a store network whose operator excluded a cannibalised unit from its own
metric (W-32); the Sav-Co failure (W-71); and off-balance-sheet lease commitments of $106,501,000
against $17,531,000 of recorded long-term debt in FY1976 (W-52).

**§N — decisions the print evidences, with their in-period rationale.** The FY1971-72 decision to
build an internal Real Estate and Construction Division and monetise buildings through Wal-Mart

FY1976 in the company's own framing, "In these days of loss of credibility by corporate citizens"

shoe-licensee acquisition and the 1980 jewelry-licensee plan (W-72). Alternatives considered and
rejected options: **NOT KNOWABLE** — nothing in this corpus records them and no minute book exists in
it.

**§O — counterfactuals, honestly bounded.** The record shows the variety-store arm run down over a
decade (14 → 9 → last trace closed January 1976) while the discount arm grew, but it cannot show that

distribution complex inside a 300-400 mile radius (W-57, W-65) without ever stating what was declined.
**NOT KNOWABLE**; §O must record that rather than invent a road not taken.

**§R — end-of-stage snapshot, Stage 1 as bounded (to 1970-10-08).** 32 stores, of which 18 were

## Contradictions

U-A2/1 — **Which net income is "the" FY1972-FY1976 figure.**
CLAIM A: FY1976 report as published — FY1972 $2,907k · FY1973 $4,591k · FY1974 $6,159k · FY1975
$6,353k · FY1976 $11,506k (W-45, W-46). CLAIM B: FY1980 ten-year table — $2,806k · $4,439k · $5,954k ·

in the FY1980 analysis for FY1978 and implied for earlier rows; behind it, the LIFO change effective
31 January 1975. EVIDENCE WEIGHT: both Tier-1, same lineage, same auditors; B is the later
presentation, A is what investors were told at the time. BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: quote A for
any statement about what was known when, B for the series' final audited shape, and label every figure
as-published or restated. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: the FY1971-FY1974 restated values are read off an
OCR-scrambled row; whether pre-tax income was restated for FY1971-FY1974 is not shown. CONFIDENCE:
High that a restatement occurred; Low on the individual restated pre-1976 numbers.

U-A2/2 — **The 1970 offering's terms.**
CLAIM A: company timeline — "The first stock is sold at $16.50 per share", undated, un-sized (W-05).
CLAIM B: FY1972 audited note — 200,000 shares sold 8 October 1970, net proceeds $3,030,467, no price

lineage; A is an uncited current-state page. BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: date, size and proceeds =
**1970-10-08, 200,000 shares, $3,030,467 net**; price UNKNOWN at Tier 1, with $16.50 *consistent* with
