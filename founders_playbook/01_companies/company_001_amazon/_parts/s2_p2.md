**Coda (§F).** Repeat behaviour is the load-bearing Stage-2 signal in one sense only: it is the sole claim the
company filed **twice** that customers returned, and the hinge of its own causal account of growth. *Mechanism* of a
genuinely rising repeat share, if real: the e-mail loop (confirmation and shipment notice at negligible incremental
cost, Eyes and Editors subscriptions, review posting) is the only filed apparatus able to produce return visits
without paid media — but **no document in the window attributes any order volume to it**, so the mechanism is
asserted, not evidenced, and no label of "viral" is applied anywhere in this file. *Alternative explanations the
record cannot exclude:* (i) that the definition changed; (ii) that flattering figures went to reporters and a
soberer one to the SEC, or the reverse; (iii) that the share held while the denominator exploded — ~180,000 accounts
at 1996-12-31 against ~340,000 at 1997-03-31 means an ever-larger fraction of the base was **new**, so a stable 40%
of orders from repeaters is compatible with **falling** per-customer loyalty; (iv) that the 40% is a floor
("over") chosen for a registration statement and the 58% a different window entirely. The five figures are therefore
printed as five measurements of unknown relation, and the dossier's own ban stands: "**Do not treat 58% as evidence
of a retention flywheel**" (S2C-35). Absence of a channel from the four filings is recorded as a **documented null**
and never as proof it did not operate; equally, **no** channel has an attributable order, revenue or conversion
measurement anywhere in the window (S2C-G3).

### G.3 One order, end to end (as filed)

[S2D-25]. Exception handling is proved by a filed price-approval procedure [S2E-39]. **Whether distributor

described, the routing rule never.
