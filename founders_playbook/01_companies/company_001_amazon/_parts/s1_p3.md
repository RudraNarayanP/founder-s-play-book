## M. Negative signals / failures

1. **A full year of no revenue.** $0 sales in the 179-day 1994 stub on $52,000 of expense, and $0 "from
   inception through July 1995" — about twelve months of paying for nothing (B-128, E-03). That calendar
   contradicts the founding rhetoric "when it's growing 2,300% a year, weeks are important" (G-03).
2. **Losses were the whole record, and every order lost money by construction.** $107,000 of cumulative losses
   ended the S-election; op. loss $(304,000); cumulative $(355,000) DERIVED. A 19.96% margin against a 39%
   marketing ratio gives $(0.19) contribution per revenue dollar, and **no average order value makes a Stage-1
   order profitable** (margin would have needed ≥ ~79%). Marketing $200,000 exceeded gross profit $102,000.
   Evidenced arithmetic, not hindsight.
3. **Supply capped the core claim.** Only **up to 400,000** of >2.5M listed titles were distributor-supplied;
   distributors combined "carry up to 350,000 of the best-selling titles" (E-18, E-57). Firewall: 2.5M is 1997;
   Stage-1 advertising was **>1,000,000** (E-71), so the advertised-versus-obtainable gap was real from month
   one. Catalogue data was **licensed from unnamed third parties** (E-42; licensor UNKNOWN).
4. **Unfilled demand was disclosed.** "Four to six weeks" for the in-print remainder, "two to six months"
   out-of-print, both with "some titles may not be available at all" (E-41); no fill, back-order or unfillable
   rate was ever quantified (G-06) — against the founder's own slogan "If it's in print, it's in stock"
   (D-07, G-14).
5. **Card acceptance was obtainable only on a personal guarantee** — Seafirst from **Nov 1994, before the
   company could sell anything**, Wells Fargo Jul-95, cards Apr-95; amounts UNKNOWN (E-25). The guarantee stood
   in for a balance sheet: $0 borrowings, $83,000 of payables growth.
6. **Manual at every choke point.** Transaction systems "not integrated with the remainder of the Company's
   accounting and financial systems", needing "a significant amount of manual effort" (E-22); card details taken
   "either by e-mail or by telephone"; nine e-mail mailboxes as the service desk; price requotes needing
   customer approval (E-43); the wholesalers' ten-book minimum gamed by ordering one wanted title plus nine
   copies of an out-of-stock lichen book (B-20).
7. **No supplier protection, and the supplier armed rivals.** No contract guaranteeing availability, terms or
   credit limits (E-17); Ingram disclosed as helping others "establish and operate online sites for
   booksellers" (E-80). Ingram's **1995** share UNKNOWN — only FY1996's 59% exists (G-05).
8. **No 1995 evidence of repeat purchase exists anywhere.** ">40%" is a March-1997 statement about 1997 orders
   (I-36); 60% (May-96) and 44% (Jan-97) are founder claims with unpublished definitions (D-30). Stage-1 demand
   demonstrates *first purchase only*: retention, cohorts and acquisition cost are absent.
9. **Rejected / failed / unverifiable experiments** (D, G): radio and TV — no 1995 order effect (D-44); customer
   gifts — no date, cost or result (D-72); paid print advertising — earliest confirmed Jan-1997, Stage 2
   (D-34); Associates — Jul-1996 (D-37); "traffic came from search engines" — not evidenced (G-33); a
   "Washington Post credibility" mechanism — rejected, documented null (D-57); the Slate 37-minute order test —
   unusable, Stephen Glass's fabrication record (D-66); the Bulgarian floppy-disk order — no corroboration
   (D-61). Referral share and word of mouth were never measured in Stage 1.
10. **The most-cited origin artifacts cannot be traced.** The 1994 "roadmap" / 91-page notebook / "Future of
    Computer Stores": no artifact, no quoted word, no page count, no title — the ladder tops out at rung 3,
    founder description, and the S-1 never mentions a plan (K-05, K-28–K-31). "July 16, 1995 first sale": no
    primary support (D-04). "$12,000 / $14,000 first weeks": Tier-3, undated, unreconcilable to a ship-basis
    audited year (E-77). "Shipped our first book in August 1995": absent from all five SEC documents searched —
    **documented null** (D-04).
11. **Fragility and scale.** Periodic interruptions, no redundancy, no disaster-recovery plan, insufficient
    business-interruption insurance, all hardware at one Seattle site (E-52; 1995 state INFERENCE). $511,000 ≈
    0.16% of all 1995 Web purchases DERIVED (511,000/318,000,000, same filing, IDC estimate); Bezos put Internet
    books at "well under 1 percent" of US sales (E-56, D-70).

## O. Counterfactual opportunities

**No counterfactual below is claimed to have succeeded.** "Considered" = evidence someone looked at it.

**O.1 Enterprise / consumer software.** *Visible:* his career was financial software — an 11-person post-trade
clearing firm, then Bankers Trust's BT World (B-21, B-23); DESCO's Juno and FarSight were employer-owned
software ventures (E-69); sellable-online in 1994 as he named it: ISPs, Netscape, HotWired, Yahoo (B-08). *For:*
deepest demonstrated competence, already funded. *Against:* the employer owned the output — his stated reason
for leaving (E-69). *Considered:* yes, software is one of four named items on the twenty-category list (A-16).
*Others:* yes, including DESCO. Medium (RETRO).

**O.2 CD-ROM / interactive multimedia.** *Visible:* the first technical hire came from Kaleida Labs, the
Apple/IBM multimedia JV (F-28); HotWired as "first content company" (B-08). *Against:* **the retrieved dossiers
contain no CD-ROM evidence at all** — no market size, no title price, no place on the category list.
*Considered:* UNKNOWN. *Others:* UNKNOWN on this evidence. Recorded as **UNKNOWN, not as a rejected
alternative**, and must not be written as one.

**O.3 Acquired by, or partnering with, a distributor or incumbent.** *Visible:* the S-1's risk factor discloses
Ingram "help[ing] establish and operate online sites for booksellers" (E-80); Ingram reported supplying Crown
Books and cbs.sportsline.com (B-80); Book Stacks was CUC-owned (E-79). *For:* a supplier-funded route removes the
capital and contract problems the filings concede (E-17). *Against:* it removes the control he left DESCO to
obtain (B-88) and hands pricing to the supplier. *Considered:* no document shows Bezos pursuing it; a 1996 B&N
co-operation meeting is Tier-4 quoting Tier-3, an open research item. *Others:* yes — Crown Books,
cbs.sportsline.com, Book Stacks/CUC. Medium (mechanism); **UNKNOWN (founder consideration)**.

**O.4 Direct-mail / catalogue hybrid.** *Visible:* mail-order was the established mechanism for buying
sight-unseen (I-43, I-44 — structure solid, **quantities UNKNOWN or disallowed**); Amazon's launch language
adopted the catalogue form (I-41). *For:* it sidesteps the barrier the S-1 names, "the secure transmission of
confidential information over public networks" (I-30). *Against:* the founder's stated reason for the Web at all
was that a book catalogue cannot be mailed, an argument the S-1 repeats on printing-and-mailing cost (B-10,
F-54). *Considered:* yes, as the incumbent format being displaced. *Others:* yes — B&N was the largest
direct-mail book supplier. Medium.

**O.5 Launch as a niche retailer.** *Visible:* Future Fantasy, WordsWorth, Book Stacks Unlimited
(425,000–450,000 titles) and Britain's Internet Book Shop (912,000–915,000) pre-date or coincide (B-102, D-69,
E-79); NCSA's Aug-1995 "What's New" listed several commercial book sites. *For:* a niche does not promise
availability it cannot deliver, and 1996 demand was niche-shaped (D-40, D-49). *Against:* breadth was the whole
stated proposition. *Considered:* he placed himself late by his own account — online bookselling "started 17
years ago… way too early" (B-9). *Others:* demonstrably yes. Medium.

**O.6 Staying at D. E. Shaw.** *Visible:* SVP since 1992, a live mid-1994 bonus, an employer funding internet
ventures (B-63, B-06, B-86); Shaw tried to keep him, citing the good job and warning DESCO could compete
(B-04, B-98); his mother counseled running the company nights and weekends, which he refused (B-100); 1994–95
credit was expensive (I-39). *For:* salary, bonus, in-house capital. *Against:* ownership economics (E-69).
*Considered:* yes, the part-time version, and refused. *Others:* DESCO peers. Medium-High on facts, all
retrospective; the forfeited amount remains UNKNOWN.

**O.7 Boundary note (not a counterfactual).** Bezos placed himself late, not first: the S-1 named Book Stacks
Unlimited among competitors and claimed only "first mover advantages" (E-79, G-12); period press stated Amazon
"isn't the first or necessarily the cheapest online book merchant" (D-69, with Wired and the Seattle Times). The
Stage-1 differentiator the evidence supports is aggregation and discoverability — directory placement, licensed
catalogue data, e-mail notification, tiered discounting — not chronology (D narrative; G-33).
