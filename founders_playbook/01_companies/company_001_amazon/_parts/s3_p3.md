### K.2 The $(27,590)k vs $(31,020)k FY1997 net-loss difference: a pooling recast, not an error

**Finding, stated plainly.** The FY1997 annual report — the document a 1998 reader opened — printed a **$27,590k
net loss** for 1997. The FY1998 10-K re-prints 1997 as **$(31,020)k**, and the FY1999 10-K agrees with the
FY1998 10-K on $(31,020). The difference is **not a transcription conflict and not a mistake**: the FY1998
10-K's Selected Financial Data is footnoted "**Reflects restatement for pooling of interests. See Notes 1 and
2**" (L1269-1270). PlanetAll (via Sage Enterprises) was merged on **1998-08-27** under **pooling-of-interests**
accounting, which under GAAP then in force required **retrospective restatement of every period presented**. So

number is labelled **RESTATED** in this dataset, with the **as-filed** version kept beside it. **No restated
value in this part reads as filed, and no as-filed value is silently overwritten by a restated one.**

### K.3 Why FY1998's +$31,035k is not trading cash: the decomposition, in both directions

The claim this section exists to police is: *"1998 was the first year Amazon generated positive operating cash
flow."* Both halves are wrong, and the corrections run in opposite directions, which is why the row is worth
its own subsection.

1. **It is not the first year.** On the level, FY1997 is already positive: **+$3,522k as filed**, **+$687k as
   restated**. FY1998's $31,035k is the year the figure becomes *material*, not the year it becomes
   *positive*. (ST3A-35's formulation; the FY1997 numbers belong to Stage 2 §K row 3 and M.2.)
2. **It is not trading cash.** The audited statement prints the subtotal, and it is **negative in every year of
   the window**: **(26,160)** 1997, **(41,433)** 1998, **(320,987)** 1999 (FY1999 10-K L2831-2832). FY1996's
   total operating cash was **$(2,010)k**. The positive result is produced entirely below that subtotal, by
   **+72,468k** of working-capital change in 1998, of which payables are **78,674k** — i.e. one line is
   **larger than the whole reported inflow by 2.5×**, and the float lines together (payables +78,674, other
   liabilities and accrued expenses +21,448/+21,615, accrued advertising +9,617) are **3.5×** it.
3. **The mechanism, tested rather than asserted.** "Supplier float did it" invites the counter-question: did
   the company stretch its vendors, or did it simply buy more? DERIVED DPO (AP ÷ cost of sales × 365, inputs
   at L2561 and L2608): **101.3 days (1997) → 86.8 days (1998) → 125.2 days (1999)**. In 1998 the terms
   **shortened by ~15 days** — the $78.7m was purchased by growth in purchases, not by leaning on vendors. In

## M. NEGATIVE SIGNALS AND FAILURES (STAGE 3)

**Mandatory, and not optimised for inspiration (method §7).** This section is the load-bearing one: Stage 3 is
otherwise a growth story told by the survivor's own filings, which is exactly the reading §2 forbids. Every
item below is stated with its evidence, its mechanism, an alternative explanation where one exists, and its
confidence; where the mechanism cannot be named, the mechanism is printed UNKNOWN. **Nothing here is scored,
and nothing here is offered as a reason the stage failed — the stage did not fail; the record shows a company
whose revenue grew 169% in its last stage year while its operating loss grew 455%.**

**M.1 Fulfilment economics moved the wrong way, and the filings now prove it in dollars.** Stage 2 could only
observe that "fulfilment labour sits in marketing and sales" with the amount UNKNOWN. The FY1999 10-K prints
the amount: fulfilment costs **$12.1m (1997) → $50.3m (1998) → $188.4m (1999)** (L1969-1972). DERIVED:
fulfilment grew **15.6× in two years against net sales 11.1×** (`188.4 ÷ 12.1`; `1,639,839 ÷ 147,787`), and
as a share of sales **8.19% → 8.25% → 11.49%** — i.e. **the cost of delivering an order rose 3.24 percentage
points of revenue in the single year the network doubled**, after holding flat through the year of the biggest
facility programme to date. *Mechanism:* the company's own MD&A — "Marketing and sales expenses increased as a
percentage of net sales in 1999 … primarily due to increases in fulfillment expenses associated with our
expansion of our distribution center network and customer service staff during 1999" (L1980-1984). The
forward sentence — "we … expect that fulfillment costs will decline as a percentage of sales in the future as
additional capacity of our existing distribution center network is more fully utilized" (L1988-1992) — **is a
declaration of an efficiency that had not occurred**, and its own premise ("additional capacity … is *not*
fully utilised") is an admission of idle capacity in the year that the fixed-asset base went 8.4×.
*Alternative explanation on the record:* **mix, and it is filed, not inferred** — "the operational challenges
of shipping **non-uniform and heavy products** as part of the fulfillment of toy, electronics, home improvement
and other product orders" (L1033-1036), with those stores launched **July and November 1999**, so a
peak-quarter cost step is expected on the company's own description. Mix and productivity **cannot be
separated** on this record: the filings give no order count and no category-level cost line, so the movement
is High-confidence and its decomposition is **UNKNOWN**.

**M.2 The year trading generated no cash, and the "self-funding" reading died inside the stage.** The audited
pre-working-capital subtotal is a **printed line** and it is negative in every year — **$(26,160)k (1997),
$(41,433)k (1998), $(320,987)k (1999)** — so FY1998's headline **+$31,035k** sits *inside* a $(41,433)k hole
filled by **+$72,468k** of working-capital change, of which payables alone (**+$78,674k**) is **2.53×** the
reported inflow (§K.3 for the full decomposition). FY1999 then falsifies the reading: payables supplied
**+$330,166k** and the total float **+$230,112k**, the largest in the company's history, and operating cash
flow was **still $(90,875)k** — "Net cash used by operating activities amounted to $90.9 million for 1999"
(L2257-2258). Days payable outstanding, DERIVED from filed balances and filed cost of sales: **101.3 (1997) →
86.8 (1998) → 125.2 (1999)**, i.e. the terms **tightened** in the year the "self-funding" claim was available
and **stretched 38 days** in the year it stopped being. *Mechanism:* payables scale with purchases and
purchases scale with inventory, which consumed **$(172,069)k** in 1999 — the float chased the stock instead of
funding it. *Alternative explanation:* ordinary retail working-capital behaviour at 4× growth, the same
objection Stage 2 recorded for FY1997; unanswered, because no vendor contract guaranteeing terms appears in any
filing in the window. **Consequence for the register: "first positive operating cash flow" is removed as a
validation signal at any date** (ST3E-21, OC-205), and §L does not carry it. Confidence **High**.

**M.3 Advertising's fall to 9.9% of sales is a decomposition effect, not efficiency — while the line containing
it rose.** Advertising expense is real, discrete and audited in four consecutive annual notes: **$3.4m (1996),
$21.2m (1997), $60.2m (1998), $140.9m (1999)**; as a share of net sales **14.3% → 9.87% → 8.60%**
(DERIVED against $147,787 / $609,819 / $1,639,839). The temptation is to read that as leverage. It is not:
advertising and fulfilment **share one caption**, and the caption's percentage of net sales **rose from 21.8%
(1998) to 25.2% (1999)** (company-printed, L1960) **in the same year advertising's share fell 1.3 points** —
because the other component grew 3.75× while sales grew 2.7×. The ratio fell because the **denominator was
pulled up by the first-party merchandise base while the money actually spent to serve each customer moved out
of advertising and into fulfilment**; the company says so in the sentence quoted at M.1. Two further
contaminants make the advertising line a poor efficiency numerator in this period: FY1999's supplemental cash
flow prints "Equity securities of other companies received for non-cash revenue for advertising and
promotional services … **$54,402**" (L2884-2885) — a third of the year's advertising was settled at least
partly in **barter**, and accrued advertising moved **+$42,382k** in 1999 against **+$9,617k** in 1998; and
the FY1999 note warns that the standard-setters were reviewing "the financial statement classification of …
fulfillment and order processing costs" by e-commerce companies "**including Amazon.com**" (L1993-2000).
**Ruling: no advertising-efficiency claim may be made for any year in this stage.** What the filings support is
three separate movements, reported separately: advertising per dollar of sales fell; cost to serve per dollar
of sales rose; the composite line rose. **Confidence High** (all inputs printed, arithmetic shown). The
Stage-2-derived "net sales per dollar of advertising" ladder (4.6× / 7.0× / 10.1×) is **retracted as an
efficiency claim** for FY1998 onward and is retained, if at all, only as a spend-to-revenue ratio with M.3
attached. `>>> CONFLICT FOR §U` (ST3E-20, OC-206).

**M.4 Q4-1999: the company wrote off inventory, and named the failure.** "Gross margin decreased in 1999 due
to the introduction of new product lines, particularly toys and electronics, and **inventory-related charges of
approximately $39 million incurred in the fourth quarter of 1999**" (L1890-1892); the risk-factor restatement

decreased our gross margins**" (L1049-1050) — which sits **three lines below the company's own advance warning of this exact failure mode**:
"We may fail to accurately predict **the optimal inventory levels at our distribution centers for the fourth

**None below is claimed to have succeeded, none is scored, and none is asserted to have been available on

**Record-selection warning (§2):** the alternatives are visible because a survivor printed them; failed 1998

**O.1 Raise equity first, and leave the $1.25bn converts unissued.** *Visible:* a **$2,000,000,000 universal

converts themselves were sold **2.5× oversubscribed against the ask in six days**. *For:* equity carries no
coupon; the converts added a **4¾% cash obligation from 1999-08-01** (≈**$59.4m** a year DERIVED) to a
business whose trading cash generation was **$(320,987)k** in 1999, and they were struck at a conversion price
that **never entered the money on any filed 1999 range** (§M.13), so the equity option they sold never
relieved the debt. *Against:* equity would have been dilutive at a moment when the founder's voting position

have bought at any price. *Considered:* **UNKNOWN**; no document prices a cash alternative. *Others pursuing
it:* **CDnow and N2K** are named by Amazon among "competitive combinations that strengthen their competitive
positions", and **the two merged** (10-Q Q3-1998, FY1998 10-K) — the record's only in-window example of a
pure-play choosing consolidation over further buying; and **barnesandnoble.com** took **strategic** money from
Bertelsmann. Confidence **Medium-High** (every input audited or company-stated and printed); **UNKNOWN (whether
the price was obtainable in cash, and what it would have cost)**.

**O.5 Keep the Senior Loan, grow inside its covenants.** *Visible:* the facility "may be increased to an amount
not to exceed **$100 million**", was prepayable at par-plus-fees, and its **accounts-payable aging** covenant
policed precisely the line that produced FY1998's positive operating cash; the alternative path was taken and
cost **$2.0m of unamortised loan fees** written off on prepayment. *For:* retaining a lender relationship would
have kept a **secured** route open at a moment when all the new paper was unsecured and subordinated, and
covenants that force discipline on supplier float are exactly what M.2 shows the float needed. *Against:* the
50% sweep of any future raise made the facility a **cap** on the very financing the company wanted; the notes
required **no cash interest before 2003-11-01**; and the first lien was over substantially all of the assets
the company was about to spend $287m a year acquiring. *Considered:* the ordering of the 424B2's own
use-of-proceeds sentence — retire the Senior Loan **first**, then general corporate purposes — is the nearest
thing to a stated preference in the corpus, and it is a **stated consequence**, not a weighing. *Others
pursuing it:* not characterised in any Amazon document. Confidence **Medium** (mechanism); **UNKNOWN** whether
unsecured terms were available in 1998 at the price actually paid, which is the whole question.

**O.6 Appoint the operating layer when the money arrived, not in September 1999.** *Visible:* through FY1998
the officer body was still headed by one man holding three offices, with **no COO and no separate finance
chief**, and the board did not change size (ST3E-F2); the FY1999 10-K exhibit index then shows **three**
executive offer letters dated **1999-06-23, 1999-09-02 and 1999-09-04, the first two amended and restated on
1999-09-30**; and the founder's own 1998 letter claims "**we significantly strengthened our management team**"
for a year in which no such appointment is on the record. *For:* the sequence M.1→M.4 is a management-capacity
story — fulfilment cost outrunning sales by 74% in one year, then $39m of charges for failing to size
inventory for a peak — and the hires are precisely the profiles (Black & Decker worldwide power tools; Delta
Air Lines CFO; AlliedSignal operations GM) that the failures suggest were missing. *Against:* the company
**did** hire in 1997 (Dalzell's offer is dated 1997-08-13 and its prior-year compensation is in the 1998
proxy), and the Q4-1999 charges cannot be attributed to the absence of an executive: **no filed causal link
exists between any 1999 hire and any 1999 outcome**, and the Galli appointment predates the peak by only four
months, so an earlier hire's marginal effect is unmeasurable on this record. *Considered:* **UNKNOWN**; no
succession or hiring plan document exists. *Others pursuing it:* Amazon's own filings record the **hiring of
B&N's former CIO as VP Business Expansion in July 1996** (Stage 2, carried forward) — evidence that incumbent
talent was reachable — and **Wal-Mart's** solicitation suit, which is the same fact seen from the incumbent's
side. Confidence **Medium** (the sequence is filed and the inference is named as inference); **UNKNOWN
(counterfactual effect)**.

**O.7 Reach foreign customers by export from the US estate, without buying foreign retailers.**
*Visible:* Amazon's filed "sales to customers outside of the US" were **25% of 1997 net sales and 20% of
1998** — before, in the 1997 case, any foreign operation existed at all (the International column prints `--` for 1997 in FY1999 Note 14, L4211); the International segment's first audited year was **$21,806k = 3.6% of FY1998 net
sales** with **$2.8m** of foreign long-lived assets and a **$(25,498)k** segment loss. *For:* export delivered
the geographic reach the ARS advertises with **no acquisition, no foreign lease to 2008, no foreign DC staff**,
and the audited foreign operating business remained a rounding item for two years. *Against:* export cannot
carry a **language, catalogue or local-payment** business, and the acquisition route produced visible results
the company could date: the UK and German stores launched **October 1998** with "combined fourth-quarter sales
… nearly quadrupled over the third quarter" and "25% of our fourth-quarter 1998 sales … derived from
Amazon.co.uk, Amazon.de, and music, video, and gift sales" (the last figure is the source of the "international
share" series, and it is **Q4-only and multi-category**, so it does not measure geography alone — §M/§L row 5).
Also against: the segment loss **grew** to $(79,223)k with revenue up 7.7×, so the loss was not a start-up
artefact of the year one. *Considered:* the choice of **acquisition over organic foreign entry** is visible in
the instruments (Bookpages, Telebook, IMDB settled April 1998; sites launched October 1998); the export-only
alternative is **never posed in any document** → UNKNOWN. *Others pursuing it:* **barnesandnoble.com with
Bertelsmann** — an online media group's answer was a well-capitalised joint venture "in several countries"
(10-Q Q3-1999); **Yahoo!/AOL** pursued portal commerce "either directly or in collaboration with other
retailers" (10-Q Q3-1999). Confidence **Medium-High** on the numbers (all audited, one document family);
**UNKNOWN** on whether export-scale trading could have been sustained, since no per-country cost exists.

**O.8 Go deeper in books instead of wider: the specialist path the SIC code quietly abandons.**
*Visible:* the registrant classified itself under **SIC 2731 (books publishing)** through the IPO and
reclassified to **retail catalog/mail-order bounded 1998-06-03 / 1998-06-12** — a filed, datable decision to
describe itself as a general retailer; Stage 2's in-window objective was "the leading online retailer of
information-based products and services, **with an initial focus on books**"; and the depth route was still
being extended as late as the **500-title** programme and the out-of-print service. *For:* the two filed
category facts that argue against breadth are now quantified — music and video margins "are lower than book
gross margins", with a "proportionate impact" warning, and the FY1999 gross-margin fall to **17.7%** is
attributed **first** to "the introduction of new product lines, particularly toys and electronics". *Against:*
depth in books was already supply-capped at the **400,000** fillable titles with **no distributor contract**,
and B&N's pending **Ingram** purchase struck at exactly that dependency; and the founder's signed letter
frames breadth as the objective itself ("anything they might want to buy online"), with the risk accepted in
the same paragraph. *Considered:* the sequencing is company-authored and dated, so the **trade-off** was
disclosed; the permanent-specialist path is **never posed** → UNKNOWN. *Others pursuing it:* **Book Stacks**
(CUC-owned) and the niche sites (Stage 1/2 record); **CDnow/N2K**, a music-only pure-play, which chose to
**merge**; and the class row "major store-based retailers" (10-Q Q3-1999). Confidence **Medium**.

**O.9 Boundary note (not a counterfactual).** What Stage 3 **establishes**, tested against the same discipline
Stage 2 applied to the IPO, is narrower than the growth statistics it contains. By 1999-12-31 the company had:
audited revenue of **$1,639,839k** growing 169%; a second and third category year; two foreign operating
segments; a filed ten-building estate; **$1.576bn** of securities sold into the market plus **$774,409k** of
stock handed to targets; ten years of leases signed; and a **$39m** inventory write-off in its first automated
peak. What it had **not** established, on any filed number, is the thing Stage 2 could not establish either:
that a customer was worth acquiring. **Revenue scaled, the loss scaled faster, and the loss was financed by
paper and by suppliers — and the dataset still cannot say which of those three facts the founder was
optimising,** because the only in-window statement of the hierarchy is a shareholder letter, and the letter
says "Certainly, some will turn out to be mistakes" without naming one.
