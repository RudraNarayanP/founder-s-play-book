## A. EXECUTIVE STATE SUMMARY

At pricing Amazon was a Delaware bookseller, federally classified as a **book publisher, SIC 2731**, with one fulfilment point. Audited FY1996: net sales **$15,746k**, gross profit $3,459k at a **22.0% gross margin**, operating loss $(5,979)k, net loss $(5,777)k, cash $6,248k, payables $2,852k, accumulated deficit $(6,025)k, **151 full-time employees** at 1996-12-31 (10-K405: 158, same date — open conflict). Last filed interim state, 1997-03-31: Q1 net sales **$16,005k** — one quarter equal to all of FY1996 — margin ≈22.0%, net loss $(3,038)k, cash $7,162k, **working capital $79,000**, 256 employees, ≈340,000 accounts, ≈80,000 daily visits, "repeat customers … over 40% of orders" (`10-K405 Items 6/7; S-1 (orig.); No. 5 [T1 · FACT, audited / unaudited interim]` [S2B-06…14, S2C-01, S2D-33/35]). Net sales include **outbound shipping and handling**, cost of sales includes freight, so every margin is also a postage figure; **no GMV or bookings figure exists in Stage 2**.

The audited record establishes what the smooth narrative drops: **growth was price-led, by declared intent.** From March 1997 the Amazon.com 500 and featured titles went to **40% off list**, and the same document warned this "will reduce gross margins below those experienced during 1995 and 1996" (No. 5 MD&A [T1 · FACT] [S2A-59, S2C-04/05]). Post-boundary, as consequence: FY1997 sales $147,758k, **margin 22.0% → 19.5%**, **net loss $(27,590)k**, and **that year's +$3.5M of operating cash was a $29.8M accounts-payable build by the company's own MD&A** — so on **1997-12-23** lenders covenanted the **accounts-payable aging** with substantially all assets pledged, capping the funding mechanism (10-K405 [T1 · FACT] [S2B-19/20/41/42, S2E-02]). In-window: payables $2,852k → $5,650k against **$79,000** of working capital.

The machine was single-threaded: **"The Company currently processes all sales through its warehouse in Seattle"** (424B1 [T1 · FACT] [S2D-01/67]); Ingram 59% of 1996 purchases under no contract; ≈16% of the catalogue distributor-supplied (§C). Failure stayed arithmetically live, contemporaneously on record: 1996-12-09, "**If we are profitable within the next two years, it will be by accident**", in an article noting the company **declined to disclose revenue** while analyst estimates reached "well over $10 million" against an audited $15,746k (Fortune [T2] [S2A-76/77/78]).

### A.1 What the founders held — in filed terms, and no more

One cheque and one board seat. **$8,000,014** of Series A sold **1996-06-21** to Kleiner, Perkins, Caufield & Byers, VIII (555,161 sh, $7,800,012.05) and KPCB Information Sciences Zaibatsu Fund II (14,235 sh, $200,001.75) at **$14.05** (DERIVED check: 555,161 × 14.05 = 7,800,012.05 ✓; 14,235 × 14.05 = 200,001.75 ✓; schedule total **$8,000,013.80**, the filed $8,000,014 being its rounding), on an executed agreement filed as Ex. 10.2 [S2B-21/22, lineage]. Plus 5,000 shares at **$40.00** to two incoming directors, 1997-01/02 (DERIVED: 40.00 ÷ 14.05 = **2.85×**, +184.7% in ~7 months) [S2B-26/27]. The preference was struck **at cost**, non-cumulative, no ratchet, no multiple, with automatic conversion only above a split-adjusted **$3.33** (DERIVED: $20.00 ÷ 6) and $7,500,000 — the cheapest capital the firm ever raised, paid for with a board seat, and the reason **the price, not the preference, was the binding event** [S2B-24/30]. Cumulative pre-IPO equity "$2.0 million and $8.2 million" through 1997-03-31 = **$10.2m** (reconciles: 8,000,014 + 200,000 = 8.2m ✓; common 1,272k + 231k + 437k = 1,940k ≈ 2.0m ✓), inside which sits the Stage-1 Section 4(2) placement of $1,007,000 to 23 investors, investors unnamed [S2B-55/56]. ### A.3 The hardest external signal, stated precisely and not upgraded

**Amendment No. 3 (1997-05-09): 2,500,000 shares at $12.00–$14.00. Amendment No. 5 (1997-05-14, the same day as pricing): 3,000,000 shares at $14.00–$16.00. Announced 1997-05-14 at $18.00** — $2.00 above the ceiling the company had filed that morning, on a **20% upsize** (DERIVED: 3,000,000 ÷ 2,500,000 = 1.20; 18.00 ÷ 16.00 − 1 = **+12.5%**; 18.00 ÷ 15.00 = **+20%** against the assumed price carrying every pro-forma in the document) inside one week, taken down in full by a ten-house firm-commitment syndicate led by Deutsche Morgan Grenfell with Alex. Brown and Hambrecht & Quist at a 7.00% gross spread [S2A-38/39/53, S2B-31/33/36, **lineage** — one source in four dated states; the *differential* is the evidence, the repetition is not]. **A 20% upsize plus a repricing above the filed range inside one week is the hardest available external validation signal, and it is precisely that and nothing more: outside investors priced a belief that the model would repeat.** It does not evidence that the model repeated. A subscription price is not a unit economic; the offering's own stated purposes were capital, a public market, future access and "**visibility and credibility**" [S2A-41, S2B-40]; the same document warned that growth rates "are not sustainable" [S2C-58]; dilution of **$13.20** (assumed $15.00) and **$15.85** (actual $18.00) per share is filed [S2B-52, S2E-12]; no selling stockholder was monetised, so nothing here is an insider exit [S2B-32]; the prospectus itself printed a wire remark that the B&N suit "would probably put a 'little bit of a damper'" on the deal, under a disclaimer that the words were neither "authorized" nor "endorsed" by the company [S2B-X-10]; and `S2E-38` rules the event "a resource discontinuity and **not** a repeatability certification." Market capitalisation at pricing, 23,858,702 × $18.00 = **~$429m** (DERIVED), is a May-1997 public fact and **must not be run backwards into a June-1996 venture valuation** [S2E-31].

### D.6 Assessment of the four tests

Four mechanisms were tested inside this window, and the scoreboard is one measurement and three silences. Only the price programme produced a filed number checkable against a forecast the company itself made in advance — and what it checked was a **cost, not a demand result**. The Associates Program produced the strongest *structural* evidence of repeatability available, 4,800 third-party storefronts live at 1996-12-31 reproducing Amazon's catalogue with none of Amazon's headcount or leases, and produced **no yield measurement at all**, its membership number degrading in precision across three successive filings. The out-of-print service proved that the company's expansion was *depth, not breadth*, and proved it by disclosing a two-to-six-month wait and a permanent "may not be available at all" inside an offering document. Bought traffic proved that demand was partly purchased and that the company named both its vendors and its vulnerability to them, while measuring nothing.

Read together, the four tests answer Stage 1's question and decline to answer Stage 2's. They establish that a firm of 11 → 256 people could put a licensed catalogue in front of ~80,000 visitors a day, take payment from ~340,000 cumulative accounts in over 100 countries, route each order to a wholesaler it had no contract with, and grow net sales five quarters running to a quarter larger than the whole prior year. They do not establish that any single order made money: the denominator "orders" was never published, so ">40% of orders from repeat customers" cannot be independently checked [S2B-64]; the only contribution concept the filings support is **+$59k on $15,746k** for FY1996 [S2B-63]; and every demand-side quantity is the seller counting itself [S2C-08]. *Would this still read as plausible if the company had failed five years later?* Yes — and it is close to what the company's own documents describe: a founder predicting profit "by accident" in December 1996; a registrant warning inside its own IPO document that its growth was unsustainable and its pricing programme would destroy margin; a book publisher with $79,000 of working capital, one uncontracted supplier, one under-insured building, no redundant systems, an unfiled valuation, a management team aged in months, personal guarantees still underwriting its card acceptance, a lawsuit filed three days before pricing whose disposition the record never discloses, and a price of $18.00 that proves only that someone was willing to pay it. **The Stage-2 verdict is therefore narrow and explicitly provisional: demand and revenue repeated; the economics were not shown to repeat; that test transfers to Stage 3 unperformed.**
