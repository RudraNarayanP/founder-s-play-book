### G.2 One order, end to end
Capture — search a **licensed** bibliographic catalog, virtual basket, card details online or by e-mail/telephone/fax → automated e-mail confirmation within minutes, second notice on shipment [F-16, F-53, D-18] → card handling through guaranteed merchant accounts; reject handling undocumented [E-25, D-65] → routing: proprietary software splits instantly-fillable titles over electronic vendor interfaces from the residual, which goes to a **manual special-order group** [E-19] → inbound: distributor ships "often… within hours" (S-1) vs "next day on our loading dock" (Bezos 1999) — different legs, **not reconciled** [E-20, E-62] → consolidate and pack at one Seattle warehouse; packing attested only retrospectively [E-21, E-61] → outbound UPS or Airborne Express [J-37] → revenue at shipment, so late-December 1995 orders shipping in January fall outside $511,000 [E-35]. Elapsed time is a **band, not a number**: immediate, 48–72 hours, four–six weeks for the in-print remainder, two–six months out of print, "some titles may not be available at all" — a 1997 filing describing 1996-97; **no 1995 timing measurement exists** [E-41].

That the incumbent then bought the largest online front door — **B&N as AOL Marketplace's exclusive bookseller from March 1997**, own site planned — is Stage-2 evidence, recorded here only as a firewall against retro-fitting a moat onto Stage 1 [C-49].
