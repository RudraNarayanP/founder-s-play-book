## M. NEGATIVE SIGNALS / FAILURES

1. **Selection collapses under the company's own disclosed ceiling — and the disclosure then vanishes.** "Of the
   more than 2.5 million titles offered by the Company, **up to 400,000** are currently supplied by book
   distributors and wholesalers, including Ingram and B&T" (A5; identical in the original and A3 — one lineage,
   three printings). DERIVED **16.0%** fillable: ≈2.1M listings were sourcing requests against "four to six
   weeks" in-print and "two to six months" out-of-print, both with "some titles may not be available at all", and
   the FY1997 discount tiers (20/30/40/up-to-89%) apply to **the same 400,000**. *Alternative:* the special-order
   desk filled most of the 2.1M — untestable, since **no fill, back-order, cancellation or returns figure exists
   anywhere** (D-1). **The sentence is absent from the FY1997 annual report** — it vanished the year the company
   no longer had to certify it in an offering. **Very high confidence; selection cannot carry the repeatability
   claim** [S2E-08, S2B-47].
2. **FY1997's positive operating cash is a $29.8M payables build, by the company's own MD&A.** Operating
   activities **provided $3,522k** against a $(27,590)k loss: "primarily attributable to increases of **$29.8
   million in accounts payable**, $5.1 million in other accrued expenses and $2.9 million in accrued advertising
   … largely offset by a net loss of $27.6 million". DERIVED: 3,522 − 29,845 = **−$26,323k** ex-payables; payable
   days 84.7 → 100.3; ≈$0.202 of payables growth per $1 of sales; the counterparty was Ingram, 58% of purchases,
   under no contract guaranteeing "the continuation of particular payment terms or the extension of credit
   limits". *Alternative:* ordinary retail float — but inventory rose 15.7× the same year and nothing shows the
   terms extendable. **Any "1997 negative operating cash" sentence is falsified by the 10-K** (F-2) [S2E-02,
   S2B-19, S2B-20].
3. **$79,000 of working capital at the last pre-IPO balance-sheet date.** Six weeks before the final prospectus:
   cash $7,162k, working capital $79k (0.5% of that quarter's sales), equity $2,763k; runway ≈17 days of FY1996
   operating burn, ≈3 days of the Q4-1996 loss run-rate. The "four days" form and the claim that the 424B1
   corroborated A5's $41,079k pro-forma are **retracted defects** → §K row 3. On the filed numbers the IPO **was**
   the working capital [S2E-03, S2B-14].
4. **What the stage produced next: the funding mechanism was capped.** *(Consequence, not Stage-2 state.)* On
   **1997-12-23** the company drew $75,000,000 under a three-year **senior secured** loan, **first-priority lien
   on substantially all of the Company's assets**, LIBOR + 3.50% then + 4.00%, covenants on minimum cash, EBITDA
   and capex — and on its **accounts-payable aging** — with a 50% sweep of any future raise, dividends prohibited,
   750,000 warrants at $52.11. A lender identified the actual source of the year's "positive" cash and capped it
   [S2B-41, S2B-42].
5. **Music was intent, not capability.** "The Company **intends** over time to expand its catalog into other
   information-based products, such as music" (10-K405, a year into FY1997); "titles … includes primarily books
   **but also a small number of** CDs, videotapes and audiotapes" (identical in March 1997 and in the 10-K);
   music/video appear only as **competitors'** business. Dated in-window expansion is four items — 500-pricing and
   out-of-print (Mar 1997), Gift Center and the second DC (Nov 1997) `(PB)` — all book-adjacent; the most-cited
   1996 launch, the Associates Program, is **never dated in any filing**; and the company's own factor warns it
   "may not benefit from the first-mover advantage" [S2E-07, S2E-44, S2A-69].
6. **The margin decline is the price-led signature; the "improvement" is a denominator.** 22.0% → 19.5%;
   quarterly 22.00 → 18.75 → 18.95 → 19.53 `(PB)`; cause filed: "a combination of **lower prices** and lower
   overall shipping margins, partially offset by improvements in product cost", after the company told IPO
   investors the March 1997 change would do exactly that. Every expense ratio fell on the denominator while the
   operating-loss ratio improved and the loss quintupled; marketing at 26.4% of sales against a 19.5% margin means
   1997 did not cover the cost of being known [S2E-01, S2B-08, S2B-10].
7. **The dependencies held, and the demand side was rented.** Ingram 59% → **58%** of purchases on a base ~9×
   larger, no vendor contract anywhere in the filing or exhibit index, the same distributor disclosed helping
   rivals "establish and operate online sites for booksellers", and the catalogue file **licensed from unnamed
   third parties** (licensor UNKNOWN, D-8); demand bought from CNET, Yahoo!, Pointcast, Excite, Lycos, Quote.com
   and CNN with **no spend, start date or result** for any, and by the FY1997 filing "**significant fixed
   payments**" to Internet aggregators with "no assurance that these arrangements will generate adequate
   revenues" — a word absent from all three 1997 registration documents [S2E-05, S2E-06, S2C-40, S2C-43].
8. **One building, a team measured in months, terms that qualify "control".** All sales processed through one
   Seattle warehouse; no redundant systems or disaster-recovery plan; "periodic system interruptions, which it
   believes will continue"; the transaction system "is not integrated with the remainder of the Company's
   accounting and financial systems" — it could not measure itself with the system presented as its advantage.
   11 → 151 → 256; "the majority of the Company's senior management joined the Company within the last four/five
   months"; **no CIO**; "difficulties in attracting a sufficient number of qualified software developers"; no
   finance officer until Covey in 1996-12. Filed control 43% (March) → ≈41% / ≈51% with family (May): one risk
   factor, two numbers, because the deal was upsized. **151 vs 158 at 1996-12-31 is unresolved across two Tier-1
   filings and must not be averaged** → §U [S2E-04, S2E-15, S2E-47, S2B-49, S2A-93].9. **The incumbents were awake, and one sued on the weakness in item 1.** B&N's letter January 1997; **suit
   1997-05-12 (S.D.N.Y.)** alleging false advertising "because it is not a 'bookstore' and does not physically
   warehouse most of the titles it offers" — two days before A5, three before pricing. B&N's own 10-K
   (1997-05-02): 431 superstores, revenues $2.448bn, net earnings $51.2m in the year Amazon lost $5.8m, and a
   344,000 sq ft DC opened September 1996 "stocking over 400,000 titles" for overnight delivery at "very deep
   discounts". Between A3 and A5 Amazon's own text changed to "B&N … **has launched** a Web site"; Simon &
   Schuster "has begun selling"; Borders "announced"; Amazon wrote of itself "**Barriers to entry are minimal**".
   Counter-hindsight: B&N lost $52.976m in fiscal 1996 and closed 18 stores. The suit then **vanishes** — the
   FY1997 10-K reports no material proceeding; outcome **UNKNOWN** (D-4) [S2E-09, S2C-13, S2C-17–22, S2E-40].
10. **The company disowned its own best press, and the unit-level question stays open.** The only third-party
    praise filed is *Time*'s "Best Websites of 1996" (item not retrieved); the registrant printed and rejected a
    DEC advertisement claiming 3,000% growth and "the world's most prosperous online bookstore", and rejected the
    April 1997 report that its President said it "easily could be" profitable [S2E-22, S2E-42]. And because no
    order count, fill rate or returns figure exists, the repeatability question cannot be answered at unit level
    from the primary record at all [S2E-39].

**Survivorship note (§25-equiv.).** These are the negatives a 1997 registration statement was obliged to
disclose about a firm that survived to file one, and the selection asymmetry is measurable: the 400,000 ceiling,
the online-security factor, the sales-tax factor and the "dependence on continued growth of online commerce"
factor all appear in March 1997 and are **absent from the FY1997 annual report**, and the B&N suit disappears
between them. A retired disclosure is not a resolved condition — the Delaware DC opened the same year the
sales-tax risk was removed, with no statement that the exposure ended. Failed 1996 entrants left no prospectus,
so §M is not a base rate [S2C-49, S2E-40].

## O. COUNTERFACTUAL OPPORTUNITIES

**None below is claimed to have succeeded, and none is scored.** "Considered" = evidence someone looked at it.
**Record-selection warning (§25-equiv.):** these alternatives are visible because a survivor's prospectus and the
press that survived with it recorded them; failed 1996 entrants left no option list — unarchived, not short.

**O.1 Stay books-only, permanently.** *Visible:* the filed objective is "the leading online retailer of
information-based products and services, **with an initial focus on books**", and the registrant classified
itself under **SIC 2731, books publishing**; a year later it still calls itself a book retailer plus "a smaller
number of CDs, videotapes, audiotapes". *For:* one category already carried 58-59% uncontracted dependency and a
16% fillable ceiling. *Against:* the new-business factor warns that margins in new areas "may be lower".
*Considered:* the sequencing is company-authored; the permanent-specialist choice is never posed in any document
→ **UNKNOWN**. *Others:* Book Stacks (CUC-owned); the niche sites (Entrepreneur's Wave, alt.bookstore,
Books2Buy). Medium.

**O.2 Licence the engine.** *Visible:* stated policy was "to license commercially available technology **whenever
possible** rather than seek internally developed solutions"; the catalogue file and the security layer are bought
from unnamed third parties; and the prospectus describes **Ingram helping other entrants "establish and operate
online sites for booksellers"** — a packaged storefront route existed and was being sold. *For:* a working
integrator stack with no inventory, no leases, no payables exposure. *Against:* the disclosed asset is an
order-routing rule capped at 400,000 and dependent on someone else's interface; the final prospectus claims **no
patent**, so there was nothing protectable to licence; the clause is gone by 1998. *Considered:* **no document
shows it** → UNKNOWN. *Others:* Ingram; per Amazon's own description, the small entrants. Medium (mechanism);
**UNKNOWN (consideration)**.

**O.3 Sell to, or partner with, an incumbent.** *Visible:* B&N's January 1997 letter and 1997-05-12 suit;
B&N/AOL from March 1997; CUC's Book Stacks; **Bertelsmann AG named as a competitor** in the FY1997 10-K (the
investment is dated 1998, out of window — F-1); Simon & Schuster selling direct; B&N's former CIO hired by Amazon
as VP Business Expansion in July 1996; the filed concession that rivals "may be able to secure merchandise from
vendors on more favorable terms". *For:* capital and a supply contract, the two things the filings concede were
missing. *Against:* ≈41% founder / ≈51% family voting control, and a board seat held by the general partner of
the funds that had just bought in. *Considered:* **UNKNOWN** — the only account of a B&N partnership meeting
rests on an uncited 2025 essay (W-7): a research task, not evidence of consideration. *Others:* Book Stacks/CUC,
B&N/AOL, Crown Books. Medium; **UNKNOWN (consideration)**.

**O.4 Go slow on categories and on price.** *Visible:* Fortune 1996-12-09 reports Bezos's "list of 20 products …
including books, music, magazines, and PC hardware and software" — contemporaneous evidence of an **intention**,
not a schedule; in 1999 he denied sequencing ("at first, all we knew is that we were going to sell books"), and
neither account is a plan document → §U. Filed in-window: "The Company **may choose** to expand". *For:* margin
was the only ratio measuring the customer proposition and it fell deliberately, and a slower programme needs less
stock, hence less payables. *Against:* "Barriers to entry are minimal" and an incumbent stocking 400,000 titles
for overnight dispatch are the timing case for speed — in the record, attributed to no deliberation.
*Considered:* the trade-off was disclosed in advance, so it was known and taken; whether a slower path was
weighed → **UNKNOWN**. *Others:* B&N discounted from a stocked position; Book Stacks undercut the typical 10%
with a flat 15% (Stage 1). Medium.

**O.5 Take different collateral terms.** *Visible (mostly after the boundary — which is the finding):* the only
secured-money document is the 1997-12-23 loan (first lien on substantially all assets, LIBOR + 3.50/4.00%,
cash / EBITDA / **accounts-payable aging** / capex covenants, 50% sweep, no dividends, 750,000 warrants at
$52.11). In-window evidence that non-bank capital was reachable: the 1997-02-12 GE Capital master lease (each
schedule a "Finance Lease"), $3,021k of fixed assets under a financing agreement, November 1997 vendor financing
at an imputed 7.7%. *For:* any of these avoids pledging the whole company, and avoids a covenant on the lever
(payables) that produced the year's operating cash. *Against:* $79k of working capital, no owned real estate, a
$27.6m loss year forming, no supplier contract — the terms available describe the company, not a choice among
many. *Considered:* **no document records a search for alternative terms**. *Others:* incumbents funded from
earnings (B&N $51.2m net). Low-Medium (mechanism); **UNKNOWN (menu actually available)**.

**O.6 Defer the offering.** *Visible:* the deal priced in the same week B&N sued, with that suit's "damper" line
printed and disclaimed inside the prospectus; the audited FY1997 year was five months away; the offering carried
**no specific use of proceeds**. *For:* deferral buys an audited year and a settled litigation posture, and the
Series A trigger did not expire. *Against:* on filed numbers the alternative to $49,103k was the $79k position —
≈17 days of FY1996 operating burn — with payables days already lengthening to 100. *Considered:* the price walk
is the only process evidence and it runs the other way — the deal grew larger and dearer, not later. *Others:*
incumbents raised nothing because they did not need to. Medium (facts); **UNKNOWN (whether deferral was
weighed)**.

**O.7 Boundary note (not a counterfactual).** The IPO is tested and **does not by itself evidence a repeatable
model**: at 1997-05-15 the company had one quarter above $16m, one warehouse, one category, no filed shipping
economics, $79k of pre-IPO working capital and a $27.6m loss year forming. What the stage establishes is
narrower: revenue and account acquisition repeated across consecutive quarters with a recurring Q4; prices for
one security moved $14.05 → $40.00 → $18.00 above range; unit economics stayed structurally unobservable because
the denominators were never published [S2B Repeatability 9, S2E-45].
