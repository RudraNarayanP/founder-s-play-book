# THE FOUNDER'S PLAYBOOK — AMAZON.COM, STAGE 1 — PART 4 (sections P–U)

Company: Amazon.com, Inc. (originally Cadabra, Inc., Washington). Stage 1: **1993 idea formation →
1995-12-31**. This volume contains sections **P, Q, R, S, T, U**; narrative sections A–O live in
parts 1–3 and claim records in the appendix. Numbering continues across parts and is not re-based.

**Metric-basis discipline (method §6, §8, §13).** "Net sales" is the audited revenue line (shipment basis,
inclusive of outbound shipping and handling) — never GMV, bookings or orders-received. FY = calendar year
(EDGAR fiscal-year end 1231), **except** the first period, a 1994 stub of 179 days. Audited figures
(Ernst & Young, in the 1997 S-1/S-1/A) are kept separate from company press claims, self-measured counts,
third-party estimates quoted inside a filing, and this dataset's own DERIVED arithmetic; every DERIVED
value has its arithmetic printed in §P.2. Titles "advertised" ≠ "sourceable" ≠ "stocked". "Visits" is the
filing's own word and expressly excludes "hits". Retrospective filings describing in-window facts are
tagged as such.

**Hindsight firewall.** Nothing here is weighted by the 1997–99 outcome; Fortune's 2026 ranking is not
evidence about 1995. Unrecoverable figures are `UNKNOWN`. **Confidence:** High = 2+ independent sources or
a primary document · Medium = one reliable source · Low = conflicting, vague or retrospective-only ·
UNKNOWN = none recovered.

### P.2 Arithmetic for every DERIVED figure

d1 `10,000 ÷ 1,700,000 = $0.005882/share` (Ex. 10.12 basis). d2 `10,000 ÷ 10,200,000 = $0.00098/share`
(filing's restated basis; the counts differ by a factor of exactly 6 = 4:1 Nov-1996 × 3:2 Apr-1997). d3
`5 Jul → 31 Dec 1994 = 26+31+30+31+30+31 = 179 days`. d4 `582,528 × 0.1717 = $100,019.06`. d5
`847,716 × 0.1717 = $145,552.83`. d6 `(0.3333 − 0.1717) ÷ 0.1717 = 0.9412 → +94.1%`. d7 `100,019 +
145,553 + 49,995 = $295,567` (dossier rounding: $295,568). d8 `1,272,000 − 295,568 = $976,432` residual,
which **includes commingled option-exercise proceeds and is an upper bound on un-named share purchases**.
d9 `102 ÷ 511 = 19.96%`. d10 `200 + 171 + 35 = 406`, tying to the filed FY1995 operating-expense total.
d11 `304 ÷ 511 = $0.595 loss per $1 of net sales`. d12 `198 ÷ 511 = 38.75%` against MD&A's "approximately
39%" — an internal cross-check, not a new source. d13 `232 + 52 = $284,000 consumed`. d14 `52 − 232 − 52 +
1,228 = +944; 52 + 944 = $996` (ties to the audited closing cash to the dollar). d15 `81 − 24
(accumulated depreciation) = $57`. d16 cash-flow line "Increase in inventories … -- (17)": opening $0
(1994 stub), so closing ≈ `0 + 17 = $17,000`; the balance is inferred from movements and Note 1 warns of
prior-year reclassifications. d17 `17 ÷ 511 = 3.33%`. d18 `511,000 ÷ 11 = $46,455` (year-end headcount as
denominator, not an average, so it flatters productivity; stated to prevent misuse). d19 `511,000 ÷
318,000,000 = 0.161%`. d20 `2,500,000 − 400,000 = 2,100,000`; `1,000,000 ÷ 400,000 = 2.5×` **across
different years (1995 claim ÷ 1997 disclosure) — illustrative of the mechanism, not a 1995 measurement.**
d21 `996 ÷ 284 = 3.51 years`. **Not computed, deliberately:** FY1995 annualised run-rate (invalid on a
≈5.5-month period), per-order economics (no order count exists), and any whole-company valuation (no
in-window instrument states one).

products for sale on its Web site in July 1995", that it "began selling books on its Web site in July
1995", and that "for the period from inception through July 1995, the Company had no sales" (S-1,
1997-03-24; S-1/A, 1997-05-14).
CLAIM B: Amazon's own press release of 1995-10-03/04 reports that "in just its first four weeks of
operation, the company shipped books to customers in all 50 states and more than 45 countries" — which
counts back to circa early September 1995, not to 16 July (eleven weeks).
WHY THEY DIFFER: three candidate readings, none excluded. (i) *Public launch vs commencement of sales*:
the release may define "operation" as unrestricted public ordering, with July as a restricted or
soft-opened trading month. (ii) *Loose PR arithmetic* off an unstated base. (iii) *Both are
company self-reports* with no independent verification of either, so the tension is between two
statements by the same author, not between a claim and a measurement. Note also the internal ambiguity
flagged at D-01: "through July 1995" can mean "up to the start of July" or "through the end of July",
and the second reading would put **zero** recognised sales in July 1995.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: both Tier 1; A is audited and appears in two separately-accessioned filings and controls
revenue dates; B is a contemporaneous company announcement and is the only dated 1995 statement about
when outside demand appeared.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: report two distinct facts and keep them apart — recognised trading from
**July 1995** (High), and the company's own claim of 50-state/45-country shipping within **four weeks as
of 4 October 1995** (High that it was claimed, Low that it was verified). Do not compute a launch date
from the release.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: which event "operation" counts from; whether any July orders were recognised in a
later period; whether the release understated its own age for effect.
CONFIDENCE: Medium (on the reconciliation), High (that the conflict is unresolved).

**U2 — The "first sale" legend.**
CLAIM A: the first non-employee order was placed on **3 April 1995** by John Wainwright, a software
engineer at Kaleida Labs and friend of Shel Kaphan, for Douglas Hofstadter's *Fluid Concepts and Creative
Analogies* at $27.95, after Kaphan e-mailed him to "create an account and order some books" (MarketWatch
2015-07-15; Atlantic 2012-10-31; Yahoo Creators 2025).
CLAIM B: every company statement places the first sale in **July 1995** at the earliest — the S-1's
"inception through July 1995 … no sales", the 1996 release's "less than a year ago", the 1999 release's
"started selling books online in July 1995" — while the popular canon fixes a **day**, 16 July 1995,
which no primary document supports.
WHY THEY DIFFER: the customer account is a personal recollection in an interview cycle of 2012–2015, and
its date falls before the company's own earliest trading window; the "16 July" date is anniversary
compression from a 2005 newspaper piece and a 2025 essay citing a 2013 book; the object also mutates
(Hofstadter's AI essays vs "a science textbook" in the Seattle Times 2005; "Mr. Wainwright" vs "John
Wainwright"; "Fluid Concepts" vs an "Artificial Intelligence" book in the Daily News rendering).
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: A is Tier 2, internally self-contradicting (the same Atlantic article prints both
3 April 1995 and Amazon's own July 1995 dating) and its three tellings descend from **one** interview
cycle. B's month is Tier 1 and audited; B's day is Tier 2/3 and unreferenced.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: the "first sale" is a **company- and customer-originated legend with
variant details**, presentable only as a variant list; the launch is stated at the precision the record
supports — **July 1995** — and no individual may be certified as the first customer. The only number
common across versions is $27.95.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: total on the day, the identity and the title. A genuine April 1995 invitation-only
transaction, if accurate, would mean the first-ever order predates every official dating — which the
audited statements do not exclude, because an insider beta order could be immaterial and non-revenue.
CONFIDENCE: High (that the legend is unresolved); Low (on every specific).

**U3 — 1 million versus 2.5 million titles.**
CLAIM A: the live catalogue was advertised as "more than one million different titles" on 1995-10-04, and
as "a catalogue of more than 1 million titles" / 1.1 million on 1996-06-14.
CLAIM B: "The Company offers more than 2.5 million titles" (S-1/A, 1997-05).
WHY THEY DIFFER: they are measurements at different dates, not competing estimates — about 18–21 months
of catalogue growth, including an out-of-print service that began only in March 1997.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: both Tier 1 and both the company's own; each is authoritative for its own date.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: **Stage 1 = >1 million advertised. 2.5 million is a 1997 number and must
never be carried backwards.** In both years the advertised figure outran the physically sourceable pool
(up to 400,000 titles via distributors, who held up to 350,000) — the novelty was order-by-order access
to a distributor file, not owning stock.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: the catalogue count at 1995-12-31 is nowhere disclosed; whether the 1995 figure
counted listings, in-print titles, or a licensed database is unstated.
CONFIDENCE: High.

**U4 — "Cadamia, Inc." versus "Cadabra, Inc."**
CLAIM A: the entity created on 1994-07-05 was "Cadabra, Inc., a Washington corporation", evidenced by a
signed subscription for 1,700,000 shares at $10,000 filed as S-1 Exhibit 10.12, corroborated by Kaphan
(2011) and Stone (2013).
CLAIM B: widely repeated accounts name the original entity "Cadamia, Inc."
WHY THEY DIFFER: B has no traceable primary at all; it behaves like a phonetic corruption propagated by
aggregation, whereas A is a signature on an instrument filed under securities liability.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: overwhelmingly A. **"Cadamia" occurs zero times in the full S-1 text searched** (1.44 MB).
Separate documented null, so no one repeats it: the string "Cadabra" does **not** occur in the S-1/A
accession 0000891020-97-000839, which carries only the prospectus body and a condensed exhibit list — the
name is evidenced by the original accession's exhibit set, not by every filing in the series.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: record **"Cadabra, Inc."** as the original legal name and "Cadamia" as an
unsupported variant. The naming *rationale* (a lawyer mishearing "Cadabra" as "cadaver") remains a
founder claim, first retrievable in 1999.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: a Washington amendment record naming another entity would overturn this; that
registry could not be reached (see U-none / S).
CONFIDENCE: High.

**U5 — $26 billion versus roughly $11 billion for the US book market.**
CLAIM A: US book sales were "approximately $26 billion in 1996" (Euromonitor, quoted inside Amazon's own
S-1).
CLAIM B: the ~$10–12bn consumer-book-retailing figure the trade used at the time — against which the
same filings' competitive arithmetic (top two retailers under 25%; B&N superstores ~9%) is coherent.
WHY THEY DIFFER: definition. A research firm's "book sales" can include educational, professional,
religious and serial channels, or measure publisher/wholesale turnover rather than retail receipts.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: only A carries a named source and date in retrieved material. **B's underlying trade
figure was NOT retrieved**, so B cannot be asserted here and must not be given equal footing by being
printed as a number.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: for Stage-1 purposes treat any dollar market size as UNKNOWN-in-this-
dataset and build the market case on structure instead — titles in print (1.2M per B&N; ~1.5M
English-language per Amazon) versus titles stocked (~130,000 in an average superstore) versus titles
sourceable (up to 400,000), all multiply evidenced.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: without an AAP/Bowker primary, neither the level nor the definition of the 1994–96
market can be fixed.
CONFIDENCE: High (that the conflict is definitional and unresolved); the ~$11bn side is Low.

**U6 — The "2,300 percent" growth statistic.**
CLAIM A: Bezos read that the Web was growing 2,300% a year and this drove the decision; he used the
figure publicly ("when it's growing 2,300% a year, weeks are important", LA Times 1997-07-20).
CLAIM B: the retrieved filings contain **no such statistic anywhere** — the 2,300% figure appears
nowhere in the S-1, and the only traceable underlying data are a February 1994 newsletter's byte and
packet **factors** (×2,057 and ×2,560), which a later book reconstructs as a conflation of a cumulative
multiple with an annual percentage ("Amazon began with a math error").
WHY THEY DIFFER: A is a founder refrain repeated in later tellings (which are not independent of each
other); B is an absent primary plus an arithmetic critique resting on one secondary reconstruction whose
source document has never been examined.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: B. Contemporary period measurements of Web scale (Netcraft 603,367 responding sites
Dec-1996; IDC ~35M users end-1996; the CERN-era server census in the tens) contradict a durable
2,300%-per-year rate, though they do not contradict an enormous multiple off a near-zero base in 1993–94.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: **classify the 2,300% as a FOUNDER CLAIM of unestablished provenance**
(RD-002, closed as untraced), record its genealogy — 1997 journalism quoting him, 2000 first-person,
2013 book reconstruction — and never carry it into the metrics table as history. The defensible
statement is that a 1993–94 Web-traffic series showing roughly 2,000-fold annual change convinced him the
medium was inflecting.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: the Quarterman newsletter itself has not been examined; a genuine 1994 table may
sit in an unarchived source.
CONFIDENCE: High (that the figure is unsupported); Medium (on what he actually read).

**U7 — The 1994 roadmap/planning document: evidence rung 3 only.**
CLAIM A: a 1994 "roadmap" — variously a 91-page notebook, titled "Future of Computer Stores", the plan
shown to investors — directed the founding decision.
CLAIM B: the ladder tops out at **rung 3, founder description**. No artifact, page image, facsimile,
auction record or finding aid was located; **not one quoted sentence** of any such document exists in the
record; the earliest dated description is the LA Times of 1997-07-20 ("pecked out a business plan on his
laptop") and the earliest first-person statement is Sheff, spoken 1999 / published 2000 ("I wrote the
first draft of the business plan in the car on the way"). The strings "the roadmap", "91 pages" and
"Future of Computer Stores" return **zero hits** across the 1.44 MB S-1, the 606 KB FY1997 10-K and every
cached file; external hits are Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn only.
WHY THEY DIFFER: A is amplification layered on top of B, probably through Tier-4 motivational retelling;
the format of the alleged document even drifts across tellings (laptop document 1997 → prose first draft
1999 → Excel projections in the passenger seat 2013).
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: decisive for B. The audited S-1 is a hard negative control: it never mentions a planning
document, states the firm "was founded to capitalize on the opportunity for online **book** retailing,"
and places other categories only inside a risk factor as something the company "may choose" to do.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: section C may state only that Bezos **described** drafting a plan during
the 1994 drive and that his parents reviewed it before investing; it may not name a roadmap, a page
count or a title, and may not attribute an everything-store conception to 1994 except as a D.E. Shaw
conversation recalled in 2013. Bezos's own scope statement: "at first, all we knew is that we were going
to sell books."
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: the plan may exist privately; nothing in the record shows that any independent
observer has ever read it.
CONFIDENCE: High (that rung 3 is the ceiling).

**U8 — Fortune's self-conflict on the Walmart streak (universe-level precedent).**
CLAIM A: Fortune's 2026 **Fortune 500** text credits Walmart with **13** years at #1.
CLAIM B: Fortune's own **Global 500** text describes a **12-year** run.
WHY THEY DIFFER: different universes and different count conventions (US-only versus global, and whether
the just-published year is counted) inside one publisher's edition.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: equal — both are the same authority, so neither can be preferred on source standing;
the discrepancy is internal to the source.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: **record the conflict rather than smooth it**, exactly as done for the
Wikipedia-versus-Fortune rank conflicts and Home Depot's fiscal-year boundary irregularity in the universe
freeze. This is the precedent that licenses the same treatment in U1–U7: an unresolved conflict in the
record is a finding, not an embarrassment.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: which streak convention each Fortune text used; unresolved, and immaterial to
Amazon Stage 1 except as method.
CONFIDENCE: High (that the conflict exists); UNKNOWN (which is right).

**U9 — Additional numeric conflicts carried, not reconciled.**
CLAIM A: 11 employees, which dossiers variously date to **1995-12-31** and to the filing's own wording
"From January 1, 1996 to December 31, 1996, the Company expanded from 11 to 151 employees", i.e. an
**as-of 1996-01-01** count; and 151 employees at 1996-12-31 in the S-1 versus 158 in the FY1997 10-K.
CLAIM B: memoir headcount of "four employees" in the garage, and press counts of "5 → 125 → >150" across
September 1996 to January 1997.
WHY THEY DIFFER: the 11 is a filing measure whose exact as-of date is a one-day interpretive choice at the
Stage-1 boundary; 151 vs 158 is a restatement on an undisclosed basis (possible contractors or a later
roll-forward); "four" and "5" are recolourings of a period for which no filing discloses a number at all.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: the filings dominate (both audited-adjacent), and 11 is unaffected by the 151/158
dispute; the memoir figures are Tier 2/3 and non-independent.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: use **11 at the Stage-1 endpoint** with the as-of basis stated explicitly,
keep 151 and 158 side by side labelled "restatement of unknown basis" rather than "error", and record all
1994 and mid-1995 headcounts as UNKNOWN.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: whether 11 is a full-time-only count, and whether contractors were inside it.
CONFIDENCE: High (count), Medium (date basis).
