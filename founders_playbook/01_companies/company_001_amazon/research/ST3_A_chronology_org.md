# AMAZON STAGE 3 — A. CHRONOLOGY AND ORGANISATION

**Dataset:** THE FOUNDER'S PLAYBOOK — forensic reconstruction, Company 001 Amazon.com (CIK 1018724).
**Stage:** 3 (chronology and organisation). **Opening: 1997-05-16**, the first day on which periodic
filings rather than a registration statement govern. Stage 2's endpoint is closed and inherited here
without re-litigation: IPO effectiveness **1997-05-15**, substantive date **1996-12-31**, Stage-3
opening **1997-05-16** (`stage_2_part_1.md` §STAGE BOUNDARY JUSTIFICATION). **Closing boundary is the
question this dossier answers**, not an assumption: see `## Boundary assessment`.
**Stage definition (method §6):** the earliest defensible point at which the company had become a
**materially scalable operating organisation rather than a promising startup**. IPO, market leadership
and valuation are admissible as endpoints only if one is genuinely the first evidence of that
transition (method §7) — they are not used as endpoints here.
**Hindsight firewall (method §2):** the 1997-99 company did not know it would survive. No decision
below is described as obviously right; no later market outcome is used as evidence about an earlier
choice. Record-selection null: the surviving archive is the **winner's** filing record, so it is dense
on capital markets and thin on operations. Internal organisation charts, rejected options, warehouse
opening events never filed, and any independent headcount verification are **unrecoverable because
they were never public** — this dossier's operational spine is therefore partly a *disclosure* spine
(§6 of method §14 rule 6: four families, one of which — periodicals — is untried here and is recorded
as UNTRIED, not as a null).
**Confidence scale:** High (2+ independent sources or primary document) · Medium (one reliable source)
· Low (conflicting/retrospective-only) · UNKNOWN.

**Filing-lineage rule (method §3), applied throughout.** Registration File No. 333-23795 (S-1
original + Amendments Nos. 1-6 + 424B1 + the 8-A12G that incorporates it) is **one source**. Each S-4,
S-3, S-8 and its own amendments/POS AM/424 supplements is **one lineage**. A 10-K and its exhibits are
one filing. Repeated copying across a lineage never counts as corroboration; corroboration below is
only from *outside* the lineage (a different form family, an auditor's separate report, an
acquired-company's own statements, a third-party filing).

**Two traps carried from the intake, both binding here.**
1. `8-A12G` (acc. 0000891020-97-000704, filed 1997-05-02) Item 1 misdates the prospectus as "dated

   is never cited for a 1996 date.
2. **Constant 17-line offset** between the two local copies of the same accession. Line numbers in

   part of the citation. The four S-1/A files exist twice (hyphen and underscore forms, byte-identical
   bodies); **the hyphenated names are cited** per `STAGE3_INTAKE_MANIFEST.md` §8.

**Post-boundary discipline runs forward from here:** everything after this dossier's chosen endpoint
is tagged `(PB)` — post-boundary — and is not folded into the window it describes. Stage-2 material is

## Boundary assessment

### The test used

Method §6 places Stage 3's end at **the earliest defensible point at which the company had become a
materially scalable operating organisation rather than a promising startup**, and §7 forbids using the
IPO, market leadership, or a valuation as the endpoint unless one is genuinely the first evidence of
that transition. Stage 2 established the working discipline on this company: **capability** (what it
could do), **declaration** (what it said it would do), **operation** (what it demonstrably did), and
only the third counts. Three axes are used here, each requiring a *document* rather than a reputation:

- **Axis P — physical operation:** distribution sites actually in service, evidenced by a filing using
  the verb "opened"/"housed … operations", not by a lease commencement or an announcement.
- **Axis T — transactional operation:** revenue the company actually recognised from something other
  than selling its own inventory into a single category.
- **Axis M — managerial operation:** a documented management structure that no longer runs on the
  founder-plus-few-VPs pattern Stage 2 inherited.

### Recommendation

**Stage 3 ends 1999-06-30 (substantive), disclosed 1999-08-16, confidence Medium-High.**
The Q2 1999 Form 10-Q (acc. 0000891020-99-001426, filed 1999-08-16) is the first document in the record
in which all three axes are simultaneously satisfied by *operation* rather than by instrument, plan or
announcement: a distribution centre actually "opened", third-party seller commissions inside the
recognised revenue line, and a President/COO — hired on a filed offer letter — standing between the
founder and the operating company. The two-epoch form mirrors Stage 2's own treatment (IPO
effectiveness 1997-05-15 with the substantive date at 1996-12-31): here the substantive date is
1999-06-30 and the evidencing filing lands 1999-08-16.

**Confidence: Medium-High, not High.** Three reasons: (i) headcount is undocumented at 1999-06-30, so
the scale claim on Axis M rests on two annual points eleven months apart; (ii) the *amount* of
third-party commission revenue in Q2 1999 is never disclosed, so Axis T is proved present but not
proved material; (iii) "In late June 1999" and "opened a new distribution center in Nevada" are
company statements inside a quarterly MD&A, not audited assertions — the audited part of the Q2 1999
10-Q is the financial data schedule, not the premises narrative.

### The argument against this recommendation

The strongest case is for **C3, 1998-12-31**, and it deserves to be stated at full strength.

1. *Geometry.* Stage 1 closed on an audited fiscal cut (1995-12-31); Stage 2's substantive date is
   1996-12-31. A Stage 3 ending mid-quarter at 1999-06-30 breaks a dataset that has otherwise used
   audited annual closes, and 1998-12-31 is the only audited cut inside Stage 3 that carries a SIC-code
   change, a category change, a country change and a positive operating cash flow at once.
2. *The SIC change is the filing system's own verdict.* Stage 2's central lesson was that the SIC code
   said bookseller. Between the FY1997 and FY1998 annual reports the code itself moves from a
   publishing classification (2731) to a non-store retail classification (5961) — arguably the single
   most independent-feeling datum available, because the company chose it in a form EDGAR validates. On
   that reading the transition is complete at 1998-12-31 and everything after is just more of it.
3. *Cash.* A company funding itself from operations at +$31.0M is not a promising startup, whoever
   its officers are.

**Why the recommendation nonetheless stands.** The SIC-code argument is weaker than it looks: 2731 is
itself a *publishing* code that never described a retailer, so the change to 5961 documents a
correction of a misclassification, not a change of business — and the FY1997 10-K already described the
company as "the leading online **retailer** of books" under the publishing code, so the two filings
disagree about what the code means, not about what the company does. The cash argument is answered by
the same statement's own components: FY1998's +$31,035k operating cash flow is produced against a net
loss of $(124,546)k and is more than explained by a **+$78,674k movement in accounts payable** (ST3A-35)
— supplier float, not operating profit, and a scale-dependent *financing* behaviour rather than proof
of an operating organisation. On the network, the FY1998 10-K's own Item 2 leaves one US facility
leased-but-closed and states the company still "intends to establish one or more additional
distribution centers within the next 12 months"; and its executive-officer table is **one name shorter**
than the FY1997 table. A year whose warehouses are half-open and whose officer roster has contracted is
a capability year. Stage 2's rule — the filing's own words govern — points at 1999-06-30, where the
verb changes to "opened", the revenue line acquires a commission, and the management structure acquires
a COO.

**Second-strongest rival, retained on the record: C5, 1999-09-30**, on the ground that one quarter's
"opened" statement may be a single facility rather than a network, and that the marketplace axis is not
materially proved until zShops commissions are recognised alongside auctions commissions in Q3 1999.
Anyone preferring a *conservative* endpoint should take C5; anyone requiring an audited annual cut
should take C3 and accept the three objections above.

### U.201 — what the five 1999-03-11 Sales Agreements are
**CLAIM A:** `_EVIDENCE_CACHE.md` (Stage-3 intake, periodic-spine table, Q1 1999 row): the five "SALES AGREEMENT, DATED MARCH 11, 1999" exhibits are "the material third-party selling/merchant contracts Stage 2 could only point at… the contractual trace of the marketplace model."
**CLAIM B:** the exhibit index of the same filing: all five are "Sales Agreement, dated March 11, 1999, by and between Amazon.com, Inc. and **The Buschman Company**" (L1660-1669) — one counterparty, not merchants — and "**Contains omitted, confidential material, which material has been filed separately with the SEC pursuant to a request for confidential treatment under Rule 24b-2**" (L1678-1681), with no exhibit bodies inline for them.
**WHY THEY DIFFER:** A infers from the exhibit *title* without reading the counterparty field; in 1990s SEC usage a "Sales Agreement" is as often a securities-purchase or equipment-sale instrument as a merchant contract.
**EVIDENCE WEIGHT:** B is the filed text of the very document A cites; a use-note cannot outweigh its source.
**BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION:** the marketplace's contractual trace is **not** in these exhibits; it is the 8-K of 1999-03-30 (auctions launched) and the Q2/Q3 1999 net-sales definitions (ST3A-53, ST3A-55). The Buschman agreements' subject matter is UNKNOWN and unrecoverable from EDGAR.
**RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY:** what The Buschman Company contracted for; whether any filed merchant contract exists in the window at all.
**CONFIDENCE:** High (that A is wrong); UNKNOWN (subject matter).

### U.210 — what the SIC code proves
**CLAIM A:** the FY1997 10-K405 header reads "STANDARD INDUSTRIAL CLASSIFICATION: **BOOKS: PUBLISHING OR PUBLISHING AND PRINTING [2731]**" and the FY1998 header reads "**RETAIL-CATALOG & MAIL-ORDER HOUSES [5961]**" — offered as proof that the business changed classification, which Stage 2's own lesson (the code said bookseller) makes rhetorically powerful.

**EVIDENCE WEIGHT:** Item 1's prose in both filings is the better evidence of the business; the codes evidence what was typed.
**BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION:** the SIC change is **weak evidence** and must not carry a boundary verdict. Recorded here because the case for candidate C3 leans on it (`## Boundary assessment`, "The argument against this recommendation", point 2).

**CONFIDENCE:** High (that the codes differ); Low (that the difference means what it appears to mean).

### U.211 — FY1997 vs FY1998 cumulative-sales statements use different bases
**CLAIM A:** FY1997 10-K405 L241-242: "Through December 31, 1997, the Company had sales of more than $164 million" — a cumulative-since-inception figure.
**CLAIM B:** the same filing's selected data and the ARS 1997 give FY1997 **annual** net sales of $147,787k, i.e. "$147.8 million", against $15,746k for FY1996.

**RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY:** none material — the arithmetic is stated so the reader can see the reconciliation.
**CONFIDENCE:** High.
