**State at the pricing (1997-05-15).** One warehouse, in Seattle, ~50,000 sq ft under a lease expiring 31 October 1999; plus ~42,400 sq ft of administrative, engineering, marketing and customer-service space at 1516 Second Avenue expiring 31 July 1999; no owned real estate; "The Company currently processes all sales through its warehouse in Seattle." There is no second facility, no announced site, and no material capital-expenditure commitment in the priced document.

**How one order physically moved (as filed).** Customer builds a basket and clicks "buy", supplying shipping and card details by e-mail or telephone; the system confirms by e-mail within minutes. Behind that, an application set covers "accepting and validating customer orders, organizing, placing and managing orders with suppliers, receiving product and assigning it to customer orders, and managing shipment of books to customers". Proprietary routing software splits the order book: lines fillable through electronic vendor interfaces go out to distributors, which often ship "within hours"; the remainder goes to a manual "special order group" that sources hard-to-find titles. Product is received at the Seattle warehouse, assigned to the customer order, and shipped out via chosen delivery option (overnight, various international, gift-wrapping), with a second e-mail when it ships. Transaction processing tracks six availability statuses. The audited policy that revenue is recognised "when the products are shipped to customers" confirms that title passes at the warehouse dock, i.e. Amazon, not the distributor, is the shipper of record.

**Change from Stage 1.** Stage 1's model — buy the book from a distributor per order, repackage, ship (a sequence known only from retrospective sources) — is replaced in-window by a leased single-site warehouse with software-routed ordering, automated vendor interfaces, a manual exception desk, barcode-based receiving (evidenced only as a prospectus art instruction), and stored customer profiles. What did **not** change: still one site, still minimal inventory (average cost $571K at end-1996, $939K at 31 March 1997), still no long-term supply contract, still no redundancy or disaster-recovery plan, still no integrated accounting system.

**Post-window marker (out of Stage 2, for continuity only).** The FY1997 Form 10-K405 filed 30 March 1998 records that "In November 1997 the Company opened a 200,000-square-foot distribution center in Delaware and expanded its Seattle distribution center to 85,000 square feet", with offices at ~88,000 sq ft. These are post-1997-05-15 values and appear nowhere in this dossier's Stage-2 state cells; the vocabulary shift from "warehouse" to "distribution center" also belongs to the second half of 1997.

## Narrative notes

1. **Stage 2 is the transition from an improvised packing operation to a documented single-site distribution system, and nothing more.** The filings show a company that in eighteen months took a small office/warehouse sublease, replaced it with a ~50,000 sq ft purpose-leased warehouse, and grew its offices to 42,400 sq ft by adding floors on a monthly cadence — and that still told investors, on the day its shares priced, that all sales processed through one Seattle warehouse with no redundancy and no disaster-recovery plan. The build-out is real but is not yet a network.
2. **The most underused evidence in this window is the exhibit set, not the narrative.** The prospectus prose gives rounded areas; Exhibits 10.28-10.32 give the leases themselves with dates, addresses, sizes and rents, and the four Columbia Building areas sum exactly to the narrative's 42,400 sq ft. That is the strongest internal consistency check available for Stage 2 operations.
3. **The gap between catalogue and obtainability is disclosed, not hidden.** 2.5M titles offered, up to 400,000 from the distributor tier, distributors themselves stocking up to 350,000, and out-of-print work measured in months. The operating system's job was therefore exception management: software routes the fillable orders and a named "special order group" chases the rest. This is the operational content behind the marketing phrase "the world's largest bookstore".
4. **Accounting and operations were decoupled by design and by admission.** Development effort went to order volume and customer service "and less on traditional accounting, control and reporting"; the management information system required significant manual effort for financial reporting. In a forensic frame, that is a statement that the numbers in the filings and the order flow ran on different systems.
5. **Scaling strain is documented as a conditional and as cost lines, never as an incident.** Rent, product development and headcount all jump by multiples inside the window; the record contains no dated outage, no backlog figure, no unfilled-order rate, and no service-level measurement. Claims that Amazon's site fell over repeatedly in 1996-97 are not supported by anything in this dossier, and neither is the opposite claim that it did not.
6. **An employed technical organisation does appear in-window, in Tier 1, and it displaces recollection.** Kaphan as VP R&D from October 1994 and CTO from March 1997; Spiegel VP Engineering from March 1997; Risher VP Product Development from February 1997; Duenas VP Operations from January 1997 with a FedEx background. The company simultaneously says it intends to hire a CIO. What remains undocumented is size and composition of any of those teams.
7. **Stack questions stay open.** The registration statement never names a language, operating system, database or server vendor; it names only ISPs, distributors, a lessor, and the fact that encryption and catalogue data are licensed in. The 2011 Kaphan interview remains the only route to C/NCSA/Oracle and must stay tagged as retrospective, outside this window.
8. **Retrospective sources disagree with the filings on physical scale** (Sheff's 17,000 sq ft office; HistoryLink's 200 sq ft warehouse) **and add colour the filings cannot confirm** (the bell that was switched off, the lichen-book workaround, MacKenzie Bezos sealing a freight contract, packing during holiday surges). These are Tier 2 with retrospective or founder-claim class and are recorded as such; none of them may override the lease record.
9. **Terminology discipline.** "Distribution center" is not this window's word: the priced documents say "warehouse". The Delaware 200,000 sq ft / Seattle 85,000 sq ft figures belong to November 1997 and later and are used only as a continuity marker.

## Contradictions

**C-1 — "License, don't build" versus "internally developed"**
- CLAIM A: "The Company's current strategy is to license commercially available technology whenever possible rather than seek internally developed solutions" (S-1/A No. 5 lines 2406-2408).
- CLAIM B: "The Company uses an internally developed system for its Web site, search engine and substantially all aspects of transaction processing" (lines 700-702).
- WHY THEY DIFFER: They are different scopes stated for different audiences: a procurement doctrine in the Business section and a liability admission in Risk Factors; both are true of a system where the storefront and order pipeline are home-grown while catalogue data, encryption and infrastructure components are bought.
- EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Both are Tier 1, same document, same lineage; neither is corroborated externally because no in-window technical inventory exists.
- BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Amazon built the transaction and merchandising layer and licensed everything that did not differentiate it — consistent with a company reporting $1,154K of purchased software at 31 March 1997 alongside $1,575K of quarterly product development expense.
- RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: The boundary is nowhere drawn; no component list exists.
- CONFIDENCE: Medium.

**C-2 — Two ISPs and multiple T1s versus "no redundant systems"**
- CLAIM A: connectivity obtained from UUNet and Interconnected Associates over multiple dedicated T1 lines (lines 2434-2436).
- CLAIM B: "The Company does not presently have redundant systems or a formal disaster recovery plan" with substantially all hardware at one leased Seattle facility (lines 741-747).
- WHY THEY DIFFER: One describes path diversity to the network, the other describes compute and site resilience; a second ISP does not survive a fire at the single building.
- EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Both Tier 1 in the same priced document; the risk-factor wording is the carefully-vetted one.
- BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: In-window Amazon had dual-homed connectivity and single-homed everything else; redundancy claims about Stage-2 Amazon are unsupported.
- RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Whether the T1s terminated on separate equipment is not stated.
- CONFIDENCE: High.

**C-3 — 2.5 million titles offered versus 400,000 obtainable versus 350,000 stocked**
- CLAIM A: the catalogue offers "more than 2.5 million titles" (line 326).
- CLAIM B: "up to 400,000 are currently supplied by book distributors and wholesalers" (lines 2386-2387) and distributors "carry up to 350,000 of the best-selling titles" (line 1975).
- WHY THEY DIFFER: Different objects — listing versus sourceability versus physical stock. The listing is a database; the supply is a search problem solved by the special order group and by months-long out-of-print lead times.
- EVIDENCE WEIGHT: All Tier 1 and in the same filing; no in-window document tests the sourcing success rate.
- BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: The operating claim of Stage 2 is that ~84% of the advertised catalogue sat outside the distributor tier and was worked manually or not at all; availability windows of four to six weeks and two to six months are the honest expression of that.
- RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: No fill-rate data; the 400,000 figure is "up to" and unaudited.
- CONFIDENCE: High on the tension, Low on magnitude of real availability.

**C-4 — Filed estate versus retrospective estate**
- CLAIM A: 42,400 sq ft of offices plus a ~50,000 sq ft Seattle warehouse at pricing, 256 employees (S-1/A No. 5 lines 2591-2600, 2577).
- CLAIM B: Sheff (1999/2000) has the company in a "17,000-square-foot office" with 614 employees in 1997; HistoryLink (2025) has a "200-square-foot warehouse" at launch.
- WHY THEY DIFFER: The Sheff office number cannot be reconciled with the filed leases and probably conflates an earlier Bellevue/SODO space; the HistoryLink figure describes 1995, outside this window's filings, and is not contradicted because the filings are silent for 1995.
- EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Tier 1 dated instruments (leases, amendments, exhibits) outweigh Tier 2 memoir by two orders of proximity; the leases' areas even sum exactly to the filed 42,400.
- BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Use the filed 42,400 + ~50,000 sq ft for Stage 2; treat 17,000 and 200 sq ft as unverified colour, and the 614-employee and $147M figures as full-year 1997, i.e. post-boundary.
- RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Whether Amazon ever occupied a ~17,000 sq ft configuration between Feb 1996 and Jan 1997 is not determinable from the record on disk.
- CONFIDENCE: High for the filed numbers, Low for the memoir numbers.

**C-5 — Associates "fulfillment by Amazon.com" versus "carries minimal inventory"**
- CLAIM A: Associates embed links and their customers' orders are handled "for fulfillment by Amazon.com", the Associate avoiding "the expenses associated with ordering and fulfillment" (lines 2336-2343).
- CLAIM B: Amazon "carries minimal inventory and relies to a large extent on rapid fulfillment from major distributors" (lines 2376-2379).
- WHY THEY DIFFER: Both are true because Amazon's fulfilment was a sourcing-and-shipping function performed from a warehouse holding only ~$571K-$939K of stock; the promise made to an Associate site was indistinguishable from the promise made to a direct customer.
- EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Tier 1 same document; no third-party Associate contract is filed, so the commission and service terms are untestable.
- BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: The Associates Program extended demand without extending supply risk — distribution cost was pushed onto the distributor tier that Amazon had no contract with.
- RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Whether any Associate order was shipped from a distributor directly to the end customer is not stated.
- CONFIDENCE: Medium.

**C-6 — Dated strains versus undated strain**
- CLAIM A: cost and scale series move violently inside the window (rent Q1 1997 $242K vs FY1996 $257K; product development $1,575K vs $263K; headcount 151 to 256 in one quarter; visits 2,200 to 80,000 in fifteen months).
- CLAIM B: the filings record no incident — no outage date, no backlog, no unfilled-order count, only that failure "could" occur and that brownouts are a market condition.
- WHY THEY DIFFER: Disclosure practice (a registration statement describes conditionals, not operating logs) plus the admitted weakness that the management information system was poor at traditional reporting.
- EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Tier 1 for both halves; the absence of incident data is therefore informative about the reporting system, not about uptime.
- BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Stage 2's operational story is a company scaling its cost base and headcount faster than its own ability to measure what the scaling did to service; the honest finding is that strain is visible in ledgers and invisible in service records.
- RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Uptime in-window is genuinely unknown in both directions.
- CONFIDENCE: High on the asymmetry, UNKNOWN on the underlying performance.
