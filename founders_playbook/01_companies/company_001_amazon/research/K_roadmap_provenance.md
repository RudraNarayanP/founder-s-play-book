# AMAZON STAGE 1 DOSSIER — K. THE 1994 PLANNING DOCUMENT: PROVENANCE

## Verdict
**The evidence ladder tops out at rung 3 (founder description). Rungs 1 and 2 are empty.** No physical or digital copy of any 1994 Bezos planning document, no page image, no facsimile, no auction or manuscript-sale record, and no archive finding aid was located in the cache, in the 1.44 MB S-1, or across six external searches; and **not one word of the document's text is quoted anywhere in the record**, which means no independent observer has ever been shown to have read it. The earliest date at which the existence of a written plan is asserted is **1997-07-20** (Los Angeles Times, reporting Bezos's account that he "pecked out a business plan on his laptop" while MacKenzie drove west), and the earliest first-person founder statement is the Sheff interview, conducted **1999**, published **2000** — "I wrote the first draft of the business plan in the car on the way." Both are retrospective, and neither gives the document a title, a page count, or a physical description; the plan's format drifts across tellings (laptop document, 1997 → prose first draft in a car, 1999 → Excel revenue projections typed in the passenger seat, 2013). The three specific attributions the corporate-history agent could not find support for — **"the roadmap," "91 pages," and "Future of Computer Stores" — have no antecedent at all**: they are absent from every cached artifact, and external searches for them return only Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and motivational aggregator pages, i.e. Tier-4 amplification with no traceable origin, so this must be recorded as a single-source-and-nothing-behind-it lineage rather than a corroborated fact. Even Brad Stone's *The Everything Store* Chapter 1 — the modern wellspring of the origin lore, which does describe Bezos carrying a notebook of jotted ideas at D.E. Shaw and typing spreadsheet projections on the drive — **never mentions a 91-page plan or quotes from any planning document**; the "everything store" concept in Stone is attributed to a Shaw-Bezos conversation at D.E. Shaw resting on unnamed executives' 2013 recollections, and the task's premise that Richard Brandt's *One Click* is a 1999 account is wrong (the cited edition is Portfolio/Penguin, **2011**). The audited S-1 is a hard negative control: it states the company "was founded to capitalize on the opportunity for online **book** retailing," defines its catalog as books plus "a small number of CDs, videotapes and audiotapes," and places expansion into other categories only inside **Risk Factors** as something the company "may choose" to do — and never mentions a planning document. **Section C may therefore state only that Bezos described drafting a business plan during the 1994 cross-country drive, that it was reviewed by his parents before they invested, and that no copy, text, page count or title has ever surfaced**; it may not name a roadmap, a 91-page notebook, or "Future of Computer Stores," and it may not attribute an everything-store conception to 1994 except as a D.E. Shaw conversation recalled in 2013 — Bezos's own statement on scope is "at first, all we knew is that we were going to sell books."

## Section C entitlement (drafting guidance)
Section C may state only that Bezos described writing a first-draft business plan during the 1994 drive, that the plan was reviewed by family investors, and that no copy, page image, quoted passage, page count, or title has been located. It may **not** state "91 pages," "the roadmap," or "Future of Computer Stores" as facts.

## What Bezos said — and when he said it
**Headline finding of this section: there is no 1994 Bezos statement in the record.** The cache advertises `sheff.txt` as the "single best founder-state document ... *before* any success existed to justify him," and directs the reader to "quote verbatim with its 1994 date." That instruction cannot be followed. The file's own first line of substance reads "This interview was conducted in 1999 and published in 2000," and its interior confirms this: it discusses zShops, auctions, a $22 billion valuation, 12 million users, Drugstore.com/Pets.com investments, and "sales of $1 billion this year." All post-1994. **Every Sheff quote below is therefore a retrospective founder statement and must be tagged as such.** The nearest thing to an in-window founder statement found anywhere is the 1997 Los Angeles Times relay, itself three years after the event.

The three Sheff passages that bear directly on the plan, verbatim with question context:

1. On whether he *wrote* the plan (the only first-person authoring statement in the cache):
   > DS: "But she was pleased to come to Seattle?"
   > Bezos: "Yes. We flew to Fort Worth, where my dad gave us a 1988 Chevy Blazer. **I wrote the first draft of the business plan in the car on the way.** I initially incorporated under the name Cadabra."

   Establishes: Bezos claims personal authorship of a first draft, written during the drive. Does **not** establish a page count, a title, a physical notebook, or any surviving copy. Note he says "first draft," implying later drafts, and never names the document.

2. On scope — the direct answer to "did you always plan to sell everything":
   > DS: "How did you decide to branch out from books? Did you always plan to sell toys and music and other products?"
   > Bezos: "**If we were very successful, we thought we would try other things. But at first, all we knew is that we were going to sell books.**"

   Establishes: in Bezos's own retrospective account, category breadth was **contingent on success**, not a blueprint. This is the strongest single item against reading an everything-store intention back into 1994.

3. On how the product category was chosen:
   > Bezos: "It seemed to me that the next stage would be transaction-based companies. So I thought, Let's do a retail company. **I made a list of 20 different products to sell online.**"
   > Bezos: "In 1994 we passed the elbow in the curve ... If you were to print a catalog with all the available books, it would be larger than 50 New York City phone books ... Yet here was a technology that could put the whole catalog in the hands of customers. **So the notion of infinite shelf space became key. With books, I decided I could create true value for customers.**"

   Establishes: a 20-item screen and a selection-driven rationale for books, matching the S-1's stated logic word-for-word in substance ("virtually unlimited online shelf space," "vast selection"). Suggests the S-1 strategy language, not a private notebook, is where the documented reasoning actually lives.

4. On price versus selection (inverts the popular low-price origin story):
   > Bezos: "We have data indicating that **customers rank selection as our most valuable asset. Ease of use and convenience are second and price is third.**"

5. On stated expectations and perceived risk:
   > Bezos: "**We thought we would sell a book a day for a long time.** But the wholesalers have a ten-book minimum."
   > Bezos: "If anybody had predicted what has actually happened, he would have been institutionalized. **No reasonable person would have predicted this.**"
   > Bezos: "my boss and good friend David Shaw was very respectful but said **Amazon.com might be a good idea for somebody who didn't already have a good job.**"

   Establishes: an explicitly modest ex-ante expectation. This is the anti-hindsight passage — Bezos himself disclaims predictive ambition for the 1994 state of mind.

6. On timing and location decision rules:
   > Bezos: "The two primary factors in deciding where to go were that I wanted to be near a major book warehouse and near a pool of technical talent. Seattle had both."
   > Bezos: "It was discovering the startling fact that web usage was growing at 2300 percent a year ... **Believe it or not, it was illegal to do commerce over the Internet in the spring of 1994.**"

## Intent vs outcome
**What is actually evidenced in-window (1994-1997):**
- S-1 (1997), the audited baseline: "Amazon.com was founded to capitalize on the opportunity for online **book** retailing"; catalog "titles" = "primarily books but also a small number of CDs, videotapes and audiotapes." Incorporated July 1994; bookstore live July 1995.
- S-1 places movement beyond books only in **Risk Factors** ("The Company **may** choose to expand its operations by ... promoting new or complementary products"), i.e. a contingent future possibility hedged for investors — not a documented plan.
- 1995 launch page (via HistoryLink): "One million titles, consistently low prices." Books only.
- The S-1's stated strategic logic — unlimited shelf space, vast selection, structural cost advantage, educated purchase decisions from metadata — is **entirely book-specific** and is itself a complete account of founding intent that requires no notebook to explain it.

**What is only later framing:**
- "Everything store." Earliest traceable source is Stone 2013 (Ch.1), attributing the phrase to a D.E. Shaw brainstorm between Shaw and Bezos, resting on unnamed former DESCO executives' recollections plus Shaw on the record in the New York Times Magazine, 1999-03-14. HistoryLink's 2025 essay repeats it but cites Stone p. 3 — **same lineage, not corroboration.**
- Bezos's own 1999 formulation is "**customer store**," and in the same interview he denies an ahead-of-time diversification plan. By 1997 the LA Times reports only that affinity marketing was on his whiteboard and that he "declines to say whether affinity marketing is in Amazon.com's future" — ambition sketched, not planned.
- Therefore: **an everything-store conception is NOT evidenced in-window as Amazon's founding scope.** It is evidenced (a) as a 1994 DESCO conversation idea via 2013 recollection, and (b) as a 1999-era company positioning. Reading it into 1994 as the roadmap's content is the classic outcome-projected-backwards move, and section N should say so explicitly.

## Narrative notes
- The provenance structure is a **funnel into a single source**. Every specific claim about the document ("91 pages," "the roadmap," "Future of Computer Stores") either has no traceable antecedent at all, or resolves to Bezos's own retellings — the 1997 laptop version, the 1999 "first draft in the car" version, and the 2013 Excel-projections version. There is no third-party who says they saw it, and no quotation of it.
- Three mutually inconsistent physical descriptions of the same founding artifact across 16 years (laptop document / prose draft in a car / revenue projections typed into an Excel spreadsheet) is itself evidence about the tradition, not just noise. If a 91-page document existed and mattered, the format would not drift this far.
- The most likely mechanism of amplification: a genuine, unremarkable, circulated startup business plan of unspecified length — real enough that parents read it and wrote $150,000 — progressively inflated in the retelling into a named, page-counted prophetic artifact once the outcome made such an artifact valuable.
- The audited S-1 is a strong negative control: an issuer describing its own origins in a 1.44 MB document under securities-law liability, with a "History of the Enterprise" and a founder biography, never mentions a planning document. Any notebook claim must be reconciled against that silence, and cannot be squared with it by citing the company's later self-narrative.

## Contradictions
**C-1 — What the founding document physically was.**
CLAIM A: a **laptop** business plan typed en route (LA Times, 1997-07-20: "pecked out a business plan on his laptop").
CLAIM B: a prose **first draft** written in the car, medium unspecified (Sheff, Bezos speaking 1999).
CLAIM C: an **Excel spreadsheet** of revenue projections typed in the passenger seat (Stone 2013, Ch.1: "typing revenue projections into an Excel spreadsheet—numbers that would later prove to be radically inaccurate").
CLAIM D: a **91-page notebook** titled "the roadmap" / "Future of Computer Stores" (aggregator lore).
WHY THEY DIFFER: A, B and C are the same event retold three times by one witness and one relay, with the medium conforming to whatever the teller assumed; D adds a page count, a title, and a physical form no witness mentions. EVIDENCE WEIGHT: A-C all trace to Bezos's own account (B is his own words; A and C are journalists' and a biographer's renderings of it), so they are one lineage with three formats, not three confirmations. D has no lineage at all. BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: a short working plan, probably typed and partly numerical, drafted on the 1994 drive and circulated to family and early investors; its length, title and survival are undocumented. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: whether anything longer than a working plan ever existed, and whether a paper copy survives in private hands (the parents, or early investor Nick Hanauer's circle, are the plausible custodians).

**C-2 — Was the plan's scope books or everything?**
CLAIM A: everything-store intent from the start (Stone's DESCO brainstorm, 2013; and the popular retrospective framing).
CLAIM B: books-only intent, diversification contingent on success (S-1 1997 founding purpose = "online book retailing"; Bezos 1999: "at first, all we knew is that we were going to sell books"; S-1 relegates new categories to Risk Factors).
WHY THEY DIFFER: A is the company's later identity read backwards by a 2013 author relying on 1994 recollections gathered ~19 years later; B is what the issuer told the SEC under liability in 1997 and what Bezos said in 1999 and 1997. EVIDENCE WEIGHT: B is contemporaneous (1997) and internally consistent; A is retrospective, partly anonymous, and itself concedes a true everything store was judged "impractical" at the start. BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: the 1994 plan's documented scope was a **book superstore exploiting unlimited online shelf space**; the everything-store idea existed as a D.E. Shaw conversation and became the company's realized strategy later. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: how much of the selection-logic was generalised to other categories in the unexamined plan text.

**C-3 — Where the 2,300% figure came from.**
CLAIM A: Bezos "happened upon a site projecting annual Web growth at an astounding 2,300%" (LA Times 1997) — an external published projection.
CLAIM B: it was **Bezos's own interpolation** of Quarterman's *Matrix News* February 1994 charts (bytes +2,057x, packets +2,560x), and Stone calls it a math error.
WHY THEY DIFFER: A compresses the story into a found fact, which is how the founder himself usually told it ("discovering the startling fact"); B restores the arithmetic from the underlying newsletter. EVIDENCE WEIGHT: B is the only version that names a document, a date, and a calculation. BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: the trigger statistic is Bezos's interpolation of *Matrix News* no. 26 (Feb 1994) data, not a figure someone else published. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: the exact Matrix News issue number was not verified this pass (Stone gives the month, not the issue).

**C-4 — Cache vs record on Sheff's date.**
CLAIM A: `_EVIDENCE_CACHE.md` — Sheff's "1994 interview/profile," Tier 1, to be quoted "with its 1994 date."
CLAIM B: the document's own text — conducted 1999, published 2000.
WHY THEY DIFFER: a mislabelled cache entry, propagated from the page's subject matter rather than its stated dates. EVIDENCE WEIGHT: B is the artifact's self-description, corroborated by its internal references to 1999 events. BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Tier-1 as an artifact of 1999, Tier-4 as evidence of 1994. RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: none — but the cache must be corrected or every downstream section inherits a false contemporaneity.
