# AMAZON STAGE 2 — A. CHRONOLOGY AND ORGANIZATION

Stage 2 opens **1996-01-01**, immediately after Stage 1's endpoint at **1995-12-31**, and is mined to
the first hard evidence that part of the business model became repeatable, or a decisive
institutionalisation event. Evidence is drawn primarily from local copies in `../sources/`
(original S-1, accession `0000891618-97-001309`, filed 1997-03-24; S-1/A Amendment No. 3,
`0000891020-97-000755`, filed 1997-05-09; S-1/A Amendment No. 5, `0000891020-97-000839`, filed
1997-05-14; Form 10-K405 FY1997, `0000891020-98-000448`, filed 1998-03-30). Line numbers cited below
are against the **headered** restored files in `../sources/`.

**Registration-lineage rule applied:** the original S-1, Amendment No. 3 and Amendment No. 5 are one
drafting lineage. A sentence present verbatim in all three is recorded as **one** source witnessed
three times, and is cited to the earliest accession that carries it. Where the wording *differs*
between accessions (headcount dates, Bezos's post-IPO stake) the divergence is a real conflict and is
reported as such.

## Boundary assessment

### Recommended Stage-2 endpoint: **1997-05-14/15 — the IPO pricing and effectiveness**

Three candidates were tested. Only one survives on dated Tier-1 evidence.

**(1) The May 1997 IPO — RECOMMENDED.** The company's own press release is dated and states the
terms: "SEATTLE, WA (May 14, 1997)-- Amazon.com, Inc. (NASDAQ: AMZN) announced IPO of 3,000,000 shares
of Common Stock at $18 per share" (S2A-53). The 424B1 final prospectus followed on 1997-05-15
(S2A-57). This is a **decisive institutionalisation event** on every axis the stage definition
requires, and each axis is separately filing-provable:

| Axis | Evidence in-window | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Legal form | Reincorporated in Delaware, agreement 1996-05-28, **effected 1996-06-18** | S2A-21/22/23 |
| Founder role | Bezos signs as **CEO** on the 1996-05-28 merger instrument; was President/Secretary/Treasurer before | S2A-24/25/28 |
| Finance function | Joy Covey joins as CFO **1996-12**; Bezos surrenders Secretary/Treasurer **1997-03** | S2A-31 |
| Board | Alberg + Doerr **1996-06**; Cook **1997-01**; Stonesifer **1997-02**; Audit and Compensation committees constituted by the S-1 | S2A-91/92 |
| External capital | Series A **$8,000,014 on 1996-06-21**, Kleiner Perkins, on a signed Purchase Agreement (Ex. 10.2); then **$54m gross** at $18.00 | S2A-11/53/56 |
| Disclosure regime | Audited 1995/1996 accounts filed and made public; Ernst & Young consent in the final amendment | S2A-57 |

The IPO is also the point at which the market, not the company, certified the model. **Amendment No. 5
was filed on 1997-05-14 with a stated range of $14.00–$16.00 and the same day the deal was announced at
$18.00 — above the top of the range the company had itself filed that morning** (S2A-39 vs S2A-53).
Five days earlier, Amendment No. 3 had put the range at $12.00–$14.00 on 2,500,000 shares (S2A-38); by
Amendment No. 5 it was 3,000,000 shares at $14.00–$16.00. A 20% upsize and a repricing above the filed
ceiling inside one week is the hardest available evidence that something outside the building validated
this business. That is a stronger boundary signal than any internal metric, and it is a *market*
judgement rather than a management claim.

**(2) The 1996 fiscal-year close — the real alternative, and the stronger "repeatability" case.** On the
narrow question "when did part of the model become repeatable?", the evidence points at
**1996-12-31**, not May 1997, and it points at a specific component rather than the whole business:

- **Repeat behaviour, not new behaviour:** "repeat customers currently account for over 40% of orders"
  at the 1996 close (S2A-04). Repeat purchase is the definition of repeatability.
- **A mechanism that scales without headcount or premises:** the Associates Program stood at
  **"over 4,800 registered members as of December 31, 1996"** (S2A-63), with Fortune dating the
  acceleration — "over 1,800 such 'associates' in the past three months" — to 1996-12-09 (S2A-80). This
  is the one part of the Stage-2 model that demonstrably reproduced itself with negligible marginal cost.
- **Continuity, not a launch spike:** five consecutive quarters of rising net sales,
  875 → 2,230 → 4,173 → 8,468 → 16,005 ($000) (S2A-43), and FY1996 audited at $15,746k on $511k.

Why this is nonetheless **not** the recommended endpoint: the quarterly 1996 path is expressly
**unaudited** (S2A-44); the year-end headcount is **conflicted** (151 vs 158, S2A-C1); and at
1996-12-31 the company had **no CFO, no completed board, no Delaware-registered securities regime, and
no externally observable price for anything it sold**. A stage boundary set on 1996-12-31 rests on
internal figures the company chose how to present. Repeatability that nobody outside could verify is not
yet an institutional fact. The 1996 close is therefore best recorded as the **substantive** endpoint and
the IPO as the **documentary** one; Stage 2 is bounded at the IPO precisely because that is where the
substance became externally verifiable.

**(3) "The move beyond books" — REJECTED.** This candidate fails outright on primary evidence, and it
fails at both ends of the window:

- Amazon's own press archive dates the **music store opening to June 1998**, outside Stage 2
  (S2A-70).
- The FY1997 10-K — the annual report covering the period in question — still says "Amazon.com is the
  leading online retailer of books. The Company **also sells a smaller number** of CDs, videotapes,
  audiotapes and other products" (S2A-68), and still frames expansion as intent: "The Company **intends
  over time** to expand its catalog into other information-based products, such as music" (S2A-69).
- The S-1 (1997-03-24) says the same thing in the same words (S2A-17). In both filings, music and video
  appear substantively only in the **competition** section, as other people's business (S2A-18, S2A-71).
- The company's SIC classification on the EDGAR header is **2731, "BOOKS: PUBLISHING OR PUBLISHING AND
  PRINTING"** (S2A-58).

What *did* expand in-window was **depth within books**, not breadth across categories: the out-of-print
service (March 1997, S2A-60) and the Amazon.com 500 at 40% off (March 1997, S2A-59). The "2.5 million
titles" claim that makes it look like diversification is a **definitional** move — "titles" was defined
to include "a small number" of non-book items (S2A-72) — and is materially qualified by the admission
that of those 2.5m titles, **"up to 400,000 are currently supplied by book distributors"** (S2A-74).

**Conclusion.** Stage 2 = **1996-01-01 → 1997-05-15**, bounded at the IPO. It ends not because the
business was proven — it was not, and the founder was on record in December 1996 saying profit would
arrive "by accident" (S2A-78) — but because on 14–15 May 1997 the organisation acquired the legal form,
the officers, the board, the auditors' consent, the public price and the reporting obligations that make
a stage separately auditable. Stage 3 should open on 1997-05-16 with a company that is still, by its own
annual report, a bookseller.

## Narrative notes

**1. Stage 2 is a financing-and-staffing story, not a product story.** The dated events cluster
tightly: Series A on 1996-06-21; reincorporation effected 1996-06-18; two directors in 1996-06; the CFO
in 1996-12; two more directors in 1997-01 and 1997-02; VPs of Marketing, Operations, Publisher Affairs,
Product Development and Engineering in 1997-01 to 1997-03; S-1 on 1997-03-24; IPO on 1997-05-14/15. Nine
of the eleven disclosed officers and directors joined **after** the Series A closed. The operating
business barely changed shape across this — it sold books, it sold deeper into books, and it added a
gift centre and an out-of-print service. What changed was the corporate machinery wrapped around it.

**2. The "new management team" is the company's own words, and it is an admission.** The risk-factor
heading is literally "MANAGEMENT OF POTENTIAL GROWTH; **NEW MANAGEMENT TEAM**; LIMITED SENIOR MANAGEMENT
RESOURCES", and the text: "The majority of the Company's senior management joined the Company within the
last four months, and **some officers have no prior senior management experience at public companies**"
(S2A-10). The filing also says it "**intends to hire a Chief Information Officer**" as of 1997-05-14
(S2A-36) — an unfilled officer seat at the moment of the IPO. Amazon went public with a CFO four months
in post, a CTO whose title was four weeks old, and a self-declared gap in its own technology leadership.

**3. The company was fragile in ways it disclosed itself.** Accumulated deficit $6.0m against $6.2m of
cash at 1996-12-31 (S2A-15/16). 59% of 1996 inventory purchases from one wholesaler, with "**no long-term
contracts or arrangements with any of its vendors that guarantee the availability of merchandise, the
continuation of particular payment terms or the extension of credit limits**" (S2A-73). A single
under-insured Seattle building (S2A-88). "Periodic system interruptions" that management expected to
continue (S2A-87). Operating losses widening quarter on quarter through the IPO ($(2,395)k Q4 1996 →
$(3,102)k Q1 1997, S2A-43). Founders' control at ~51% (S2A-93/94). This is not a retrospectively
imposed pessimism; it is the issuer's own risk factor section, filed to induce the investment.

**4. The diversification narrative does not survive contact with the filings.** The popular account —
books in 1995, music in 1996/97, then everything — is wrong at the documents. Music shipped June 1998
(S2A-70). In December 1996 Fortune reports Bezos's original "list of 20 products… including books, music,
magazines, and PC hardware and software" (S2A-77); in 1999 Bezos denied sequencing at all: "at first, all
we knew is that we were going to sell books" (Sheff, Playboy, **conducted 1999 / published 2000** — see
the C-1 metadata correction in `_EVIDENCE_CACHE.md`; this is a retrospective founder claim and is *not*
evidence of 1996 intent). Both statements post-date the decision window and neither is a plan document.
Hindsight firewall applied: the fact that music eventually arrived in 1998 is not evidence that 1996
category decisions were rational, and no Stage-2 filing commits Amazon to a date for it.

**5. What genuinely looks repeatable in-window is smaller and more specific than "the business model."**
Two things, both dated: the Associates Program at 4,800 sites by 1996-12-31 (S2A-63), and repeat
purchase above 40% of orders at the same boundary rising above 58% by 1997-12-31 (S2A-04, S2A-65).
Everything else — the catalogue count, the shipping promise, the inventory position — was still being
bought rather than reproduced, and the "two- to six-month" out-of-print delivery promise (S2A-60) is the
company printing its own inability to fulfil its headline claim.

**6. The price of the stock moved more than the business did.** Same security, same lineage, five
weeks: $12.00–$14.00 (1997-05-09) → $14.00–$16.00 and +20% shares (1997-05-14 morning) → $18.00 (1997-05-14
announcement). And Series A paper that cost $14.05 in June 1996 was marked at $40.00 to incoming
directors in January–February 1997 (S2A-85). The one place where an apparent internal disagreement
survives on the record is the ownership table: Bezos at 43% in March, 41% in May (S2A-93).

## Provenance and method notes

- **Lineage accounting:** findings S2A-01 through S2A-98 rest on **five distinct sources**, not on the
  ~15 accession-level citations they generate: (i) the S-1 registration lineage [items 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 —
  counted as ONE]; (ii) the FY1997 10-K405 [item 4]; (iii) Amazon's own press archive [items 11, 12, 13];
  (iv) Fortune 1996-12-09 [item 14]; (v) retrospective material [items 7, 8, 9]. Repeated copying of a
  sentence across accessions 1309/755/839 is **one** quotation witnessed three times.
- **Hindsight firewall:** no post-1997-12-31 outcome is used anywhere in this section as evidence that a
  1996 or 1997 decision was rational. The one forward-looking item used — the June 1998 music store — is
  used **only to establish a negative**: that a category did *not* ship inside the window.
- **Numbers deliberately not produced:** revenue per employee; any blended headcount; any figure derived
  from Fortune's "34% a month"; the $54.0m IPO gross (labelled an arithmetic derivation, S2A-56, not a
  quotation).
- Nothing in `../sources/` was deleted, moved or tidied. This file is the only output written.
