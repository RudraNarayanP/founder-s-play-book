## Narrative notes

**1. Two chronologies are being conflated in most published accounts, and the dataset must separate them.** There is (i) a *corporate* chronology — 1994-07-05 subscription for Cadabra, Inc., a Washington corporation, $10,000, sole subscriber Bezos; a March-1995 S-corp-to-C conversion; February/July/December 1995 share sales at $0.1717 and $0.3333; a June-1996 Delaware merger; and (ii) an *operating* chronology — Bellevue house, then SODO, Kaphan from October 1994, Davis a month later, a soft launch some months before July 1995, the July 1995 opening, ~2,200 visits/day and $511 K of recognised net sales in 1995, 11 employees on 1996-01-01. The corporate chronology is documented in a signed federal filing and is robust; the operating chronology is reconstructed from a company statement plus participant memory. Nothing retrieved in this session bridges them at day level.

**2. The "roadmap" problem.** The brief asks for Bezos's 1994 "roadmap"/notebook. What the retrieved record supports is narrower: (a) Stone reports Bezos habitually "recording ideas in a notebook he carried with him" at D. E. Shaw; (b) Kaphan testifies that when he arrived in October 1994 "all that existed of Amazon was on paper"; (c) NYT (1999) and Stone describe projections being typed during/around the relocation; (d) Bezos's own filing says the first year's activity was "development of the necessary computer infrastructure and initial planning and development of the Amazon.com site and operations." No document image, quoted contents, catalogue entry, or archive holding for a 1994 roadmap was found. This is recorded as A-10 UNKNOWN rather than paraphrased from secondary lore.

**3. Sequencing problems inside the record itself.** Three examples. (i) The S-1 gives May 28 and June 18, 1996 for the same reincorporation (agreement vs effectiveness). (ii) The Feb 9, 1995 shareholder agreement in Exhibit 10.13 is styled "Amazon.com, Inc., … a Delaware corporation," which is impossible for that date; the exhibit is therefore a conformed form and must not be used to date the name change (A-45). (iii) The company says it had no sales through July 1995, while its first technical hire says the site was publicly reachable and taking orders in a "soft launch" roughly two months earlier, and a 1995 Tallahassee newspaper reports the site being tested in October 1995. The accounting statement and the transactional reality are not the same object.

**4. Departure date is bounded, not fixed.** The filing places Bezos at D. E. Shaw "to June 1994"; Stone places the notice conversation in "that spring"; Kaphan's recruiter contact "knew Jeff was going to leave" by late spring. An actual last day (a payroll/HR date) does not exist in any retrieved source. Do not let the dataset assert one.

**5. Who went first is unresolved.** Kaphan's memory is that Bezos was "just arriving … and setting up his house when I moved up from California" (Kaphan arriving October 1994), i.e. Bezos (with MacKenzie) first, Kaphan second; but Kaphan also recalls Bezos looking at California office space in late spring, and Stone records a single joint drive by Jeff and MacKenzie via Fort Worth. MacKenzie's own employment end date at D. E. Shaw — which would fix the household's departure window — was not retrieved and is listed as a gap.

**6. The "first sale" is a company legend with a paper trail of commemoration and no paper trail of transaction.** Amazon's own history page asserts the title and buyer (A-71); a 2015 press report attributes the same claim to a company spokesperson email and to a named building (A-72); reference aggregators give only the month (A-73); contemporaneous 1995 journalism evidences real orders without identifying the first one (A-74, A-87). The dataset should record: *claim exists, is founder/company-originated, is internally inconsistent on the title, and is unevidenced at transaction level* — and separately record the $27.95 figure as part of the claim, not as a measured price.

**7. Ordering matters for the Stage-1 thesis.** Amazon was neither the first to sell books remotely on a network (Book Stacks Unlimited, dial-up, 1992) nor, on the retrieved record, the first incorporated Web bookstore. What is documented is that Amazon's incorporation preceded its selling by twelve months, that the selling window itself began months before any outside observer wrote about it, and that the enterprise at the end of that first year consisted of $511 K of revenue, eleven employees and 2,200 daily visits. Any judgement about whether the July-1995 timing was "right" is outside the firewall and outside this file.

## Contradictions

**C-1. Original corporate name: "Cadabra, Inc." vs "Cadamia, Inc."**
CLAIM A: The entity Bezos subscribed for on 1994-07-05 was "Cadabra, Inc., a Washington corporation" (S-1 Ex.10.12; also Kaphan 2011; Stone 2013).
CLAIM B: Widely repeated accounts name the original entity "Cadamia, Inc."
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: Claim A is a signed document filed with the SEC; Claim B has no traceable primary in this session and appears in aggregators.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Overwhelmingly A (one Tier-1 exhibit + two independent participant/reportorial accounts; "Cadamia" occurs zero times in the 1.44 MB S-1 text searched).
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Record "Cadabra, Inc." as the original legal name; log "Cadamia" as an unverified variant, likely a corruption of Cadabra.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: A Washington amendment record naming some other entity would still overturn this; the registry could not be reached.

**C-2. When did amazon.com start selling: soft launch ≈May 1995 vs "July 1995" vs "no sales through July 1995."**
CLAIM A: S-1 — "began selling books on its Web site in July 1995"; "For the period from inception through July 1995, the Company had no sales."
CLAIM B: Kaphan — soft launch "a couple months earlier" than the July hard launch; Jeff Holden buying books pre-July.
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: Accounting recognition (audited, conservative, month-level) versus operational availability. A soft launch reachable by the public but not marketed can produce real orders that the filing subsumes into "July."
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: A is Tier 1 and governs revenue dates; B is first-hand technical testimony and governs "reachable-by-outsiders" dates.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Two-stage record: *public reachability* 1995-Q2 (low confidence on month), *commercial/recognised launch* 1995-07 (high).
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: No artefact establishes that the soft launch accepted third-party payment.

**C-3. Reincorporation date: 1996-05-28 vs 1996-06-18 vs "June 1996."**
CLAIM A: "On May 28, 1996, the Company reincorporated in the state of Delaware" (Note 6) and Ex.2.1 merger agreement dated May 28, 1996.
CLAIM B: "On June 18, 1996, the registrant effected a reincorporation…" (Item 5); summary page: "reincorporated in Delaware in June 1996."
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: agreement/board date vs Delaware filing-effectiveness date.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Both are the same filing; the distinction is documentary, not factual.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Merger agreement 1996-05-28; effect 1996-06-18.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Minimal; noted so the dataset does not "pick" one date silently.

**C-4. Exhibit 10.13 dates a Delaware corporation to 1995-02-09.**
CLAIM A: The exhibit is "made and entered into as of this 9th day of February 1995 by and between Amazon.com, Inc. … a Delaware corporation."
CLAIM B: The company did not exist in Delaware before June 1996 (A/C-3).
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: Exhibits were conformed to the post-reincorporation entity for the 1997 filing.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: B (the operative facts of the filing).
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Treat as a template/conformed document; it evidences a Feb-1995 shareholder relationship, not a Feb-1995 name or jurisdiction.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Whether the executed original also bore "Amazon.com" in Feb 1995 — undetermined.

**C-5. The first sale: Hofstadter vs an AI textbook; "Mr. Wainwright" vs "John Wainwright"; July 16 vs undated.**
CLAIM A: aboutamazon.com — first book "Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies" by Douglas R. Hofstadter, bought by "Mr. Wainwright."
CLAIM B: NY Daily News URL slug/lede describes "Artificial Intelligence" book at $27.95 and names "John Wainwright"; both stories trace to an Amazon spokesperson.
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: Both are commemorative restatements of the same 1995 order by a company reconstructing it from internal records more than a decade later; the "AI book" framing may reflect the buyer's own memory of the title (both titles are computing texts).
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Weak on both sides; the only quantified element common to the versions is $27.95.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Record as a founder/company-originated legend, unday-dated, title unresolved; anchor actual 1995 commercial activity to $511 K net sales and ~2,200 daily visits in December.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: High — this is precisely the kind of claim the firewall is for; it must not be laundered into fact by repetition.

**C-6. The road trip: coast-to-coast drive, or flight plus a Texas-start drive?**
CLAIM A: Founder-circulated version (NYT's rendering): MacKenzie driving a Chevy Trail Blazer end-to-end while Bezos typed the plan and called angels.
CLAIM B: NYT 1999 ("It turns out… he flew to Texas and drove from there") and Stone 2013 (fly to Fort Worth, borrow father's 1988 Blazer, drive northwest).
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: A is origin-myth amplification; B is two independent reconstructions of travel logistics.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: B (two sources; one contemporaneous to 1999).
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: A relocation occurred in mid-1994 involving a borrowed vehicle and on-the-road work on projections; the transcontinental framing is founder embellishment.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Exact routing/dates.

**C-7. Kaphan's status: "first employee"/"forgotten founder," or VP R&D from October 1994.**
CLAIM A: HistoryLink/GeekWire — "Kaphan was Amazon's first employee and some in the industry consider him a co-founder."
CLAIM B: S-1 — Bezos is sole July-1994 subscriber; Kaphan's tenure begins October 1994; Kaphan himself: "I didn't get founder's stock," "to be completely technically true … he is the founder."
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: A is a cultural/journalistic framing; B is contractual.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: B for legal status; A for practical contribution.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Employee #1 after the founder, first technical hire, present ~9 months before revenue; not a shareholder-founder at incorporation.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: Whether MacKenzie Bezos, whose 1994-95 check-signing is attested, should be counted as employee #1 or #2 — no retrieved record settles it.

**C-8. Timing of "first-mover" framing.**
CLAIM A: S-1 — Amazon "opening for business as 'Earth's Biggest Bookstore' in July 1995" and "one of the most widely known, used and cited commerce sites on the Web."
CLAIM B: Smart Business — Book Stacks Unlimited operating a remote-access bookstore from 1992, >500,000 titles; Stone — DESCO test-buys from Book Stacks/WordsWorth in 1994.
WHY THEY MAY DIFFER: A's superlative is about Web storefront scale/brand; B is about chronological priority on networks generally.
EVIDENCE WEIGHT: Both stand; they measure different things.
BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION: Amazon was not the first networked book seller; it was the first, on the retrieved record, to run a Web-catalog, distributor-fulfilled bookstore at scale from July 1995.
RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY: WordsWorth's and other 1994-95 sites' exact launch dates were not established by retrieved primary sources.
