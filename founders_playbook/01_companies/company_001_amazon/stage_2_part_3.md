both directions; part_1's DEFECT-9 note carried "l.22", which lands on a blank line while the cluster text is at

keyed a self-reference to a number was undone by the next edit. The last filed ceiling was set on 13 May, not on
the morning of 14 May (see §Q 1997-05-13); no document states an hour for any 13–15 May event**), and this §Q
covers exactly that span with two classes of adjacent row: **boundary-adjacent** rows inside 1997-05 that change how
an in-window reading works, and **post-boundary `(PB)`** rows printed only to date an absence (the music store, the
Delaware centre, the risk-factor retirements). **No 1995-12 row is printed:** the stage's opening datum is the
1996-01-01 employee sentence, and the December-1995 anchor behind it is Stage 1's, so the class promised by an
earlier draft does not exist and has been removed from the class list rather than filled with an imported row. The
famous milestones are the least valuable rows here, so they are short: nothing in this table is worth more than
the date it fixes. Rows are ordered by date — the `(PB)` block included, and the `UNKNOWN` rows collected in one
block at the tail so that an undated object never sits inside the dated sequence. Where a date is disputed, the row
sits at the earliest candidate and the dispute is carried in-cell, never merged (→ U.44, U.101). **Two date keys are
periods, not days, by convention: the `1996-Qn` rows are the filed quarterly tables, whose register twin is keyed to
the quarter-END date (e.g. §Q `1997-Q1` ↔ `timeline.csv` `1997-03-31 BOUNDARY STATE`), and a `1996-12`-style key
means the filing itself goes no finer than the month.** A §Q row and a register row may therefore carry different
labels for one datum without carrying different dates. **One further ordering rule, printed so that this header and
its rows cannot diverge: a row whose date cell is a SPAN — a documented absence running forward from a start date —
does not bind the point-dated rows printed after it, and the `(PB)` block is ordered by the earliest day each row
names.** **`(PB)` = post-boundary; `(L)` = same registration lineage
as another cited accession, so it adds no independence.**

**U.44 — Employees at 1996-12-31: 151, 158, and Fortune's 110.**
**CLAIM A:** "As of December 31, 1996, the Company employed **151 full-time employees**" (S-1 original, Business —
Employees l.2364, 1997-03-24; the same document's risk-factor sentence "from January 1, 1996 to December 31, 1996 …
from 11 to 151 employees", l.667). **CLAIM B:** "growing from **158 employees as of December 31, 1996** to 614

**WHY THEY DIFFER:** the three are not three counts of one population. A is a **full-time** count; B is an
unqualified "employee base" in a growth-strain sentence, plausibly including the independent contractors and
temporary employees the same 10-K says the company "also employs" (l.516–518) but never numbers; C is a journalist's
figure taken **three weeks before** the measurement date. **`158 − 151 = 7`** — seven people, or one definition.
**EVIDENCE WEIGHT:** A and B are both Tier 1 and both the issuer's own paper; they are **not** independent, and B
comes from the document the corpus's adversarial pass calls its weakest load-bearing despite its audit (S2E-38).

**band** (§P.2 s17); §R Employees prints "151 / 158 / 110 — three numbers, one season." Any Stage-2 productivity,
cost-per-head or bandwidth figure is a band and is labelled so.
**RESIDUAL UNCERTAINTY:** permanently unrecoverable on public evidence — **a 1996-12-31 payroll roster is not a
public document**, and no 4:1 or 6:1 arithmetic on filed numbers can distinguish a definition from a headcount.
**CONFIDENCE:** High (that each figure was stated); **Medium and unresolved** (which describes 1996-12-31);

**U.47 — How to say how big FY1996 was, and what $18.00 against $14.05 means.**
