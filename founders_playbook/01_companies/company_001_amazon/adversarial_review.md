## 4. Alternative explanations for early traction that the standard story suppresses

Mechanisms only; none may be called decisive, and each carries its alternative (spec §16).

- **Directory and editorial placement.** The 1995-10-04 release says "Netscape and Yahoo have each included
  Amazon.com in their 'What's New' and 'What's Cool' lists" (**T1 company claim**); no independent dated
  listing was retrievable, and the August-1995 Mosaic "What's New" archive — 3,084 entries — holds no Amazon
  company entry (**T1**, mirror). Alternative: placement explains discoverability, not purchase, and for 1995
  is asserted rather than evidenced.
- **Search.** The same release advertises an on-site search engine as the core capability (**T1**); the Seattle
  Times on 1997-01-05 credited "two key search engines" for pointing to Amazon "as one of the top sites among
  thousands on the Web" (**T1** observation; engines unnamed, no share attributed). Alternative: search
  visibility followed press and directory features rather than producing them.
- **The founder's prior network.** A six-week paid beta of "about 300 friends and family" (**T2**, 1999); the
  first non-employee order placed by the first engineer's friend (**T2**, 2015); Alberg's December 1995
  purchase at $0.3333 and board seat (**T1**). Alternative: the network seeded first transactions and capital,
  not the curve.
- **Distributor-file access.** "The Company licenses some of its catalog and other information from third
  parties" (**T1**; licensor unnamed, stays UNKNOWN), with up to 400,000 titles procurable through distributors
  and Ingram at 59% of 1996 purchases (**T1**). Alternative: the access was non-exclusive — distributors
  "help establish and operate online sites for booksellers", so the same file armed rivals.
- **Owned and earned press.** Self-issued releases via a PR agency, syndication, the Time mention. The ratio
  is real but the sum small — FY1995 marketing and sales of $200,000 against $511,000 of net sales (39%) — and
  the earliest *confirmed* paid print advertising is January 1997, outside the stage. Alternative: the
  marketing fingerprint belongs to 1996-97, not the launch months.
- **Scarcity of substitutes.** Netcraft counted 603,367 responding sites in December 1996 (**T1**); trade press
  still counted a "big three" in online bookselling by November 1997 (**T2**). Alternative: a thin field raises
  the value of any single channel, describing the environment rather than execution.
- **The filing's own demographic rationale.** "The demographic overlap between frequent book buyers and
  Internet users is high" (**T1**), consistent with the 1996 list's web-design titles. Alternative: the
  category choice alone explains the demand; no mechanism has to.

## 5. Survivorship and hindsight audit

AUDIT 4 test — **would this still read as plausible if the firm had failed in 2000?** §1 is written so that
it would: its surviving facts are structural (an incorporation, a launch month, an audited $511,000, a lease,
a guarantee), while its most quoted "achievement" elements (50 states, one million titles) are graded as
company copy. The economics row is deliberately unfavourable — 20% gross margin against 39% marketing predicts
failure in most readings — and saying so keeps the rest credible. Three hazards are recorded, not argued
away. **(i) Evidence survivorship:** the load-bearing documents exist because the company registered an
offering in 1997; failed contemporaries left no S-1, so a filing-centred Stage 1 over-represents the
survivor's side of the record, including risk factors drafted for liability rather than history. **(ii)
Language hazard:** "first mover advantages and momentum" is hindsight filed as fact and appears here only as
the company's claim. **(iii) Causal hazard:** no sentence asserts that directory placement, the beta or the
discount schedule *produced* growth. The knowability split holds that founder reasoning in 1994-95 is largely
unrecoverable (§6, N-1), so decisions are explained from what the actors could reasonably have known — a thin
in-print catalog, a two-distributor channel, an alphabetical directory — and from no evidence about what
happened next. Weak signals are stated, not implied: a year with no sales after inception, four-to-six-week
availability, a lost delivery test at parity of price.

## 6. Negative contemporaneous coverage and documented nulls

**Doubting coverage in the corpus.** LA Times 1997-07-20: the company "is either the next light bulb or
another Edsel"; it had built "a large portion of its business on selling nerd books to nerds"; it "had lost $9
million real dollars to generate sales of $32 million" as of March; a same-price delivery test was won by
Barnes & Noble; and a Publishers Weekly technology editor credited paid advertising — "What Amazon.com did
differently was to take out newspaper ads and start spreading money around". Sheff 1999/2000 records
Forrester's George Colony naming it "Amazon.toast". Barnes & Noble's January 1997 claim letter and 1997-05-12
false-advertising suit attacked the "Earth's Biggest Bookstore" origin claims themselves. The dating is itself
a finding: all of it post-dates 1995-12-31 and serves as near-window or retrospective source, not Stage-1
reception — which is why no dated 1995 consumer or trade review of Amazon.com exists in this corpus at all.

- **N-1 No contemporaneous founder-state interview exists for Stage 1** (COR-06). Sheff is a 1999 interview
  published 2000, and Sheff's genuinely 1994 Wired profile of Bezos remains unfound; the one in-window founder

- **N-2 The Washington Secretary-of-State record was unreachable** (corqs.access.wa.gov failed on three

## 7. Residual risks to this reconstruction

- **R-1 Independence is partial.** The challenges were written by an agent that had read the corpus, so "prove
  the dominant narrative wrong" was conducted with knowledge of the answers.
- **R-2 Single-origin corroboration.** LA Times 1997, Sheff 1999/2000 and Stone 2013 all trace to Bezos, and
  HistoryLink 2025 cites Stone; counting them as independent would inflate confidence across §1.
- **R-3 Restored-and-pending evidence.** After the 2026-09-23 deletion incident six cited documents (lat.txt,
  stone.txt, wiki.txt, p.html, amztimeline.html, wb_amazon.html) remain missing; four load-bearing quotations
  were re-verified verbatim against restored files, and records resting on the missing six are marked
  "restoration pending" (COR-08).
- **R-4 Grade drift between registers.** G tiers HistoryLink T4 while the binding register caps it at Tier 2;
  both appear above and the merge must apply the ceiling, not an average.
- **R-5 Overruling in the other direction.** Reading every company statement as self-interested leaves the
  filings' concessions as the only trusted material, biasing Stage 1 toward false conservatism about things
  admitted against interest.
