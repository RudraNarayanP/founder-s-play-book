**Novel or not.** Dated and filed in-window: search over a licensed ISBN-keyed file; per-order access to a
distributor pool via electronic interface; a manual special-order desk behind it; e-mail confirmation at
negligible cost; hyperlink syndication via Associates (membership dated, start **not** → U.112); and a discount
programme the company warned would cost margin. Not novel: buying a book from a wholesaler per order. Two 1997
numbers wearing the same name are **different objects** — Amazon's "2.5 million titles" are listings, B&N's a
bibliographic database mediated by a store. Amazon denied structural protection: "**Barriers to entry are
minimal, and current and new competitors can launch new sites at a relatively low cost**" [S2C-12 · T1 · FACT].
Of four candidate advantages the record defends **aggregated long-tail availability** (~3× a superstore),
**purchase volume** with the distributor tier and **supplier float** — two contingent on third parties'
continued willingness [S2E-25 · INFERENCE, Medium].

**Coda (§I).** Read without hindsight, the competitive position at 1997-05-15 is: **one of at least six** named online
book sellers; not the cheapest on ordinary titles (Book Stacks at 15% against Amazon's typical 10%, and the company
declining to give figures to the press the same month); facing an incumbent which had launched **a front door, not a
store**, at the filing date and whose website appeared in the lineage only between the 9 and 14 May amendments — but
which had a **344,000 sq ft** distribution centre already open since September 1996, stocking the same ~400,000
titles Amazon could reach only through a rented interface; a publisher upstream that had "begun selling a number of
books" online six days before pricing; and a distributor arming small entrants in the same sentence in which it
supplied 59% of Amazon's inventory. *Mechanism* of any advantage: aggregated fillable long tail, purchase volume with
one wholesaler, supplier float — all three contingent on third parties' continued willingness (S2E-25, **INFERENCE,
Medium**). *Alternative reading the record cannot exclude:* that B&N's overnight-plus-deep-discount plan, executed,
would have nullified the only two axes on which Amazon's filed offer could lose — wait and price; its execution is
**UNKNOWN** in-window, and bn.com's 1997 sales reach this corpus only through Tier-3 chronologies never checked
against a B&N filing (S2E-23). *Confidence:* **High** row by row, **Medium** on any comparative verdict, **no row
graded on anything after 1997**, and **no moat retrofitted** — the registrant itself disclaimed one, verbatim, in the
document asking for $54,000,000.

## O. COUNTERFACTUAL OPPORTUNITIES

**None below is claimed to have succeeded, and none is scored.** "Considered" = evidence someone looked at it.
**Record-selection warning (§25-equiv.):** these alternatives are visible because a survivor's prospectus and the
press that survived with it recorded them; failed 1996 entrants left no option list — unarchived, not short.

**O.1 Stay books-only, permanently.** *Visible:* the filed objective is "the leading online retailer of
information-based products and services, **with an initial focus on books**", and the registrant classified
itself under **SIC 2731, books publishing**; a year later it still calls itself a book retailer plus "a smaller
number of CDs, videotapes, audiotapes". *For:* one category already carried 58-59% uncontracted dependency and a
16% fillable ceiling. *Against:* the new-business factor warns that margins in new areas "may be lower".
*Considered:* the sequencing is company-authored; the permanent-specialist choice is never posed in any document
→ **UNKNOWN**. *Others:* Book Stacks (CUC-owned); the niche sites (Entrepreneur's Wave, alt.bookstore,
Books2Buy). Medium.

**O.2 Licence the engine.** *Visible:* stated policy was "to license commercially available technology **whenever
possible** rather than seek internally developed solutions"; the catalogue file and the security layer are bought
from unnamed third parties; and the prospectus describes **Ingram helping other entrants "establish and operate
online sites for booksellers"** — a packaged storefront route existed and was being sold. *For:* a working
integrator stack with no inventory, no leases, no payables exposure. *Against:* the disclosed asset is an
order-routing rule capped at 400,000 and dependent on someone else's interface; the final prospectus claims **no
patent**, so there was nothing protectable to licence; the clause is gone by 1998. *Considered:* **no document
shows it** → UNKNOWN. *Others:* Ingram; per Amazon's own description, the small entrants. Medium (mechanism);
**UNKNOWN (consideration)**.

**O.3 Sell to, or partner with, an incumbent.** *Visible:* B&N's January 1997 letter and 1997-05-12 suit;
B&N/AOL from March 1997; CUC's Book Stacks; **Bertelsmann AG named as a competitor** in the FY1997 10-K (the
investment is dated 1998, out of window — F-1); Simon & Schuster selling direct; B&N's former CIO hired by Amazon
as VP Business Expansion in July 1996; the filed concession that rivals "may be able to secure merchandise from
vendors on more favorable terms". *For:* capital and a supply contract, the two things the filings concede were
missing. *Against:* ≈41% founder / ≈51% family voting control, and a board seat held by the general partner of
the funds that had just bought in. *Considered:* **UNKNOWN** — the only account of a B&N partnership meeting
rests on an uncited 2025 essay (W-7): a research task, not evidence of consideration. *Others:* Book Stacks/CUC,
B&N/AOL, Crown Books. Medium; **UNKNOWN (consideration)**.

**O.4 Go slow on categories and on price.** *Visible:* Fortune 1996-12-09 reports Bezos's "list of 20 products …
including books, music, magazines, and PC hardware and software" — contemporaneous evidence of an **intention**,
not a schedule; in 1999 he denied sequencing ("at first, all we knew is that we were going to sell books"), and
neither account is a plan document → §U. Filed in-window: "The Company **may choose** to expand". *For:* margin
was the only ratio measuring the customer proposition and it fell deliberately, and a slower programme needs less
stock, hence less payables. *Against:* "Barriers to entry are minimal" and an incumbent stocking 400,000 titles
for overnight dispatch are the timing case for speed — in the record, attributed to no deliberation.
*Considered:* the trade-off was disclosed in advance, so it was known and taken; whether a slower path was
weighed → **UNKNOWN**. *Others:* B&N discounted from a stocked position; Book Stacks undercut the typical 10%
with a flat 15% (Stage 1). Medium.

**O.5 Take different collateral terms.** *Visible (mostly after the boundary — which is the finding):* the only
secured-money document is the 1997-12-23 loan (first lien on substantially all assets, LIBOR + 3.50/4.00%,
cash / EBITDA / **accounts-payable aging** / capex covenants, 50% sweep, no dividends, 750,000 warrants at
$52.11). In-window evidence that non-bank capital was reachable: the 1997-02-12 GE Capital master lease (each
schedule a "Finance Lease"), $3,021k of fixed assets under a financing agreement, November 1997 vendor financing
at an imputed 7.7%. *For:* any of these avoids pledging the whole company, and avoids a covenant on the lever
(payables) that produced the year's operating cash. *Against:* $79k of working capital, no owned real estate, a
$27.6m loss year forming, no supplier contract — the terms available describe the company, not a choice among
many. *Considered:* **no document records a search for alternative terms**. *Others:* incumbents funded from
earnings (B&N $51.2m net). Low-Medium (mechanism); **UNKNOWN (menu actually available)**.

**O.6 Defer the offering.** *Visible:* the deal priced in the same week B&N sued, with that suit's "damper" line
printed and disclaimed inside the prospectus; the audited FY1997 year was five months away; the offering carried
**no specific use of proceeds**. *For:* deferral buys an audited year and a settled litigation posture, and the
Series A trigger did not expire. *Against:* on filed numbers the alternative to $49,103k was the $79k position —
≈17 days of FY1996 operating burn — with payables days already lengthening to 100. *Considered:* the price walk
is the only process evidence and it runs the other way — the deal grew larger and dearer, not later. *Others:*
incumbents raised nothing because they did not need to. Medium (facts); **UNKNOWN (whether deferral was
weighed)**.

**O.7 Boundary note (not a counterfactual).** The IPO is tested and **does not by itself evidence a repeatable
model**: at 1997-05-15 the company had one quarter above $16m, one warehouse, one category, no filed shipping
economics, $79k of pre-IPO working capital and a $27.6m loss year forming. What the stage establishes is
narrower: revenue and account acquisition repeated across consecutive quarters with a recurring Q4; prices for
one security moved $14.05 → $40.00 → $18.00 above range; unit economics stayed structurally unobservable because
the denominators were never published [S2B Repeatability 9, S2E-45].
