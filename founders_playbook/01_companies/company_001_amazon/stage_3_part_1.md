document's weight. Corroboration requires an **independent origin**: E&Y's signed opinion, an acquired
company's own audited statements filed under Rule 3-05, a counterparty's instrument (the five Buschman
Sales Agreements), a holder-signed Schedule 13G, a pleaded complaint as reported by a defendant, or a
periodical that did not use the filing.

**Hindsight firewall (§2, absolute).** Nothing here treats the company's eventual survival or scale as
evidence that a 1997–1999 decision was rational, that incumbents were fools or asleep, that "selection",
"technology", "the flywheel" or "the platform" was a moat, or that any outcome was foreseeable. The 1999
company **did not know it would survive**, and the record says so in its own voice: the Q3-1999 10-Q's
distribution-risk heading reads "Risk of Distribution Center Expansion" and warns that its new plants
"**may fail to operate properly** to ensure that customer demand is met, particularly during the fourth
quarter of 1999", that it had "**no previous experience with automated distribution centers**", and that
it might be "unable to adequately staff our distribution and customer service centers during these peak
periods". Those are not prudential boilerplate to be read past: they are the contemporaneous state of
mind of the operating layer this volume's recommended boundary is built on, and they are cited as such.
Post-boundary values appear **only as consequences and only under `(PB)`**.

**Record-selection null (§2), per section.** Stated in full at §S in a later volume and previewed where

### Recommendation, and the argument against it

**Recommended: Position C — Stage 3 closes 1999-09-30 on executed instruments, disclosed 1999-11-15,
Confidence Medium.** Grounds: it is the only position whose test **does not fire at a date its own author
rejects**; it rests on **signed paper and on automation**, not on a verb's first appearance in a document
the company drafts about itself; it removes rather than grades the axis the record cannot evidence (T′);

**unaudited quarterly MD&A**. **Position B is retained as the mandatory sub-endpoint Stage 2 imposed**, so
the FY1997 audited close and the 1997-12-23 borrowing are inside Stage 3 on every reading and are not at
issue. **Position A's endpoint survives as a dated disclosure event** (1999-08-16) and all of its facts
are used; only its criterion falls. `ST3_A`'s own text already conceded C: "Anyone preferring a
*conservative* endpoint should take C5" — `ST3_E` inverts that, finding **C5 is the earliest date that
survives and C4 is the conservative-looking one that does not**.

**Costs of moving to C, stated rather than absorbed.** (i) Thirteen `(PB)` tags in `ST3_A` are voided and

### B.4 The FY1997/FY1996 restatement problem, as an operating fact

The window's comparative years do not have one value. **PlanetAll was merged on 1998-08-27 under
pooling-of-interests accounting, which then required retrospective restatement of all periods presented.**
The consequence is not cosmetic: FY1997's net loss as **filed** was **$(27,590)k**; as **remembered** in
FY1998 it is **$(31,020)k**, 12.4% larger; its operating loss moves $(29,209)k → $(32,595)k; accumulated

net sales did **not** move ($15,746k in both witnesses) while its loss moved $(5,777)k → $(6,246)k. **The
1997 and 1996 loss figures every later source quotes are the restated numbers.** The rule applied
throughout this volume: **every restated value carries the label RESTATED in its own cell; no restated
value reads as filed; and no ratio is quoted without naming which vintage produced its denominator.**

**$609,996k** (FY1998 10-K) vs **$609,819k** (FY1999 10-K, three places); FY1997 gross profit $28,813 vs

### D.1 Capacity: the plant problem, in filed lines

The stage's physical record is a **cost series with no output series**, and the asymmetry is the point.
What is filed: **rent** $270,000 → $2.1m → $8.5m (audited — `ST3A-26` calls it "the cleanest single
money-trail of the physical build-out", and it is a **31×** rise 1996→1998); **capex** $(7,603)k →
$(28,333)k → $(287,055)k; **gross fixed assets** $43,585k → **$366,977k** (8.42×) with **$83,290k in
construction in progress** at 1999-12-31; **leased assets** $442k → $52,374k (**118.5×**, DERIVED — the
machine estate is financed, not bought); **depreciation** $3,442k → $9,692k → $36,806k, with **Q4-1999
alone at $13,871k, exceeding all of FY1998** (DERIVED from cumulative 10-Q figures 5,223 / 13,325 /
22,935); and **fulfilment cost** $12.1m → $50.3m → $188.4m, i.e. **8.19% → 8.25% → 11.49% of net sales**.
What is not filed, at any date, in any of the 99 files: **a throughput figure, an order-capacity figure,
a cycle time, an error rate, a fill rate, or a dated outage with a duration**. The nearest approach to a
failure record is two consecutive 10-Qs listing "systems, including temporary power outages at
distribution centers, delayed transportation of" among named failure modes in the controls discussion —
**enumerated, not evidenced as experienced**, and this file does not upgrade them. **Mechanism (§16):**
the spend curve is visible because it is audited; the output curve is absent because it was never
required. **Alternative explanation the record cannot exclude:** that the company could not yet have
produced output numbers, since the plants opened inside the reporting period — which is the same thing as
saying that **at the boundary the network was a construction project being measured by invoices**.

### D.2 Participation: sellers, listings, and the counting problem

Three objects the literature routinely conflates must be held apart: **net sales**, **GMV**, and
**listings**. The corpus contains **only the first**, and from 1999 the first **mixes** own-inventory
price with third-party placement fees, sales commissions and payment-service fees **with no published
split** — so every 1999 growth ratio in this stage is computed on a denominator whose composition changed
mid-run. The single marketplace quantity that exists is "**1 million registered users and 1.5 million
listings**" for Q4-1999, which is: a **count of registrations**, not of sellers who sold; **combined**
across Auctions, zShops and sothebys.amazon.com, so not attributable to any one service; **dated to a
quarter**, not a day; **after both candidate endpoints**, so it cannot rescue A's Q2 transactional leg;
and a **company self-report in its own Item 1**. **`ST3E-13`'s formulation is adopted as the Stage-3
statement of record:** "the only seller-side quantity in the record is a combined registered-user and
listing count for Q4 1999, undisclosed by service and unverified." The same discipline applies to
**"16.9 million cumulative customer accounts"** (basis changed to "inclusive of Auctions" in 1999, so the
1997 and 1998 terms may not be subtracted from it) and to **Associates** (a count of enrolments, yield
unmeasured). **The liability boundary is the more informative filed fact:** the company does not take
responsibility for delivery of payment or goods, relies on sellers' representations, can suspend accounts
but cannot compel performance, and compensates defrauded buyers through a **$250** guarantee it is careful
to call "**not insurance**." **The trust mechanism was therefore launched before its economics were ever
disclosed, and its cost is absent from the record.**

### D.3 Money as signal, and as anti-signal

The stage's genuinely external signals are **financial**, and their honest reading is negative for the
operating model and positive for market belief: a secured lender funding a retailer **against its own
payables** and covenanting their aging (1997-12-23); a public market taking **~$326m** of discount notes
from an issuer that would otherwise have carried **$2.4m** of debt; a convertible market upsizing **$500m
to $1.25bn in six days**; an acquisition-currency shelf tripled twice in fourteen months; a proposed
**1.5-billion-share** authorisation. Each is witnessed outside the registrant and dated, which makes them
the only externally verifiable material in the stage — and none is a statement about unit economics. By
1999 the financing line had become larger than the trading loss it funded: nine-month interest expense
**$(66,424)k** against pre-working-capital operating cash of **$(320,987)k**.

*Interpretive coda (§16 requires all four parts).* **`INFERENCE`, Confidence Low** — and the reading it
replaces is withdrawn (COR-23): "the largest signal in this window is that **the cost of capital fell**, not
that the cost of doing business did." **Evidence:** the printed coupons and their cash dates — 10% Senior
Discount Notes (1998) paying no cash before 2003-11-01 and accreting to $530m face on ~$326m gross; 4¾%
convertible subordinated notes (1999) with a cash coupon from 1999-08-01 and a $156.055/6×-vintage conversion
option. **Mechanism:** none demonstrable on this record. The two are **not the same instrument on the same
ranking** — a zero-coupon senior discount note and a cash-coupon convertible subordinate — and the convertible's
low coupon is explained by the embedded equity option, which §L and §M.13 say was live from the first quarter
after closing; comparing the coupons while ignoring the option value is what made the direction look clean. A
comparable price observation (effective yield to accreted maturity, secondary price, or a peer issue of the same
week) **exists nowhere in `sources/`**, so no arithmetic is manufactured here and **the cost of capital is
UNKNOWN as a measured quantity in this stage**. **Alternative explanation, taken from this project's own
volumes:** the §N row for 1997-11-07 → 1998-05-05 lists among the information available "a window open for US
high-yield convertibles" — i.e. 1999 technology-paper **demand for the instrument**, which prices the issuer's
luck with a market, not an improvement in its terms. **Confidence: High** on the coupons, dates and amounts;
**Low** on any claim that capital got cheaper; **UNKNOWN** on whether the cost of doing business fell.

### D.5 Assessment

**What Stage 3's signals, taken together, actually evidence at the recommended boundary.** A
**capacity-and-participation build underwritten by capital markets**, documented at every step by
instruments: nine plants opened across the window, a machine estate contracted before three of its sites
had names, ~$1.58bn of notes and converts raised (DERIVED: 326 + 1,250), an authorised-share ladder multiplied fifteen-fold, a
third-party selling mechanism launched with its liability boundary explicitly drawn, and a management
layer bound by signed offers in the last month of Q3 1999. **What they do not evidence is a model that
reproduced cash.** The pre-working-capital operating subtotal is negative at every year-end in the
window; fulfilment cost per sales dollar **rises**; the first audited quantification of the entire new
business group is a **gross loss**; every demand quantity is self-measured and **two of the series change
definition mid-run**; and the company's own filings in the quarter of C's endpoint predict that the new
plants may not work. **The best-supported reading is therefore narrower than either a triumph or a
failure narrative: by 1999-09-30 this was an organisation that had bought, built and staffed the
apparatus of scale faster than it could have measured it, and had financed that race by selling its own
payables cycle to lenders and its own future to the convertible market.** A **signal was needed on
repeatability in this stage, and none is available on the evidence** — which is itself the stage's
forensic finding, and the reason the §U conflict is about *which axis counts* rather than about a date.

**Confidence scale (method §3).** High (2+ independent sources or a primary document) · Medium (one
reliable source) · Low (conflicting, vague, retrospective-only) · UNKNOWN. **Filing-lineage rule:** all
1997–2000 Amazon documents cited here are the **same registrant lineage**; three printings of a
sentence inside one accession, or between an accession and its own ARS reprint, are **one source**.
Independence is claimed only against another registrant's filing, an auditor, contemporaneous press not
derived from the filing, or a counterparty's signature block.

**Post-boundary tag.** `(PB)` marks a value whose basis lies outside the stated date of the section
using it. Used throughout, per §J.6.

**Quotation rule.** Every string in quotation marks was read at the named line of the named local
file. Where EDGAR breaks a sentence across lines the break is preserved as `\n`.

### E.0 What this section can and cannot reconstruct

The unit of evidence for Stage-3 product history is **the filing, not the decision**. No internal
category-expansion memo, no schedule, no resourcing paper and no rejected option survives in the public

sorted into three states, kept apart and never merged, per the discipline Stage 3's product dossier set:

- **DECLARED** — a dated company document says the company intends to.
- **LAUNCHED** — a dated company document says it is live, open or launched. *A forward-tense sentence

- **OPERATING AT SCALE** — a dated company document attaches a volume, revenue, capacity or share figure
  to it. *A number-one-online-seller claim never fills this column unless a number sits with it.*

categories opened in 1999 no category-level figure exists anywhere in the 99 local documents.

## F. CUSTOMER

### F.0 The counting problem, stated once and applied per row

**Nearly every demand-side number in this window is the company counting itself.** That is not a caveat
attached to the section; it is the section's finding, and it must be readable from each row. Three
distinct self-counting devices operate, and they are not the same fault:

1. **The issuer's own operational register** — cumulative accounts, repeat share, order counts. Never
   audited as a metric, never defined, and never checked against an external panel.
2. **A third-party measurement quoted by the issuer** — Media Metrix, Forrester, Opinion Research Corp.,
   Relevant Knowledge. The instrument may be independent; **the selection of it is not**, and no underlying
   report is in `sources/`. Under method §3 a figure quoted inside a filing is one source however many of
   that company's documents quote it.
3. **The issuer counting its own intermediaries** — Associates enrolments, portal relationships. The unit is
   a party the company signed, not a customer reached.

No document in the 99-file corpus supplies an independent count behind any of these. ⚑ **Endpoint note:**
the entire 1999 demand series below is post-boundary under a 1997-12-31 or 1999-06-30 close; under the
1999-06-30 reading F2's terminal value is 10.7m not 16.9m, and the seller-side rows F9–F10 arrive only
through the FY1999 10-K.

### G.0 The finding of this section, stated before the evidence

**The network was a build-out, not a network.** That is the load-bearing conclusion of Stage 3's operations
reconstruction, and it is not a judgment about the future of the company: it is a description of what the
documents evidence at 1999-12-31, and it is carried by six independent classes of filed fact —

1. **nine distribution-centre openings dated across the window**, with **five of them in a single nine-month
   period** and no opening day for any;
2. **fulfilment cost rising as a share of net sales across the whole build** — 8.19% → 8.25% → **11.49%** —
   so the capital did not produce a unit-cost improvement inside the window;
3. **$83,290K of construction in progress at 1999-12-31, equal to 22.7% of gross fixed assets** — more than a
   fifth of the estate not yet in service at the count date;
4. **both pre-1999 plants manually operated**, on the company's own statement, alongside "we have no previous
   experience with automated distribution\ncenters";
5. **the five Buschman exhibits of 1999-03-11 — materials-handling equipment contracts for three sites "yet
   to be determined", prices redacted, no throughput, no capacity, no completion date**;
6. **the one incurred operational failure in the window** — Q4-1999 inventory-related charges which the
   company states "significantly decreased our
gross margins", and whose amount it then gives.

company had a committed, contracted, part-occupied physical programme whose operating economics had not yet
