### D.6 Assessment of the four tests

Four mechanisms were tested inside this window, and the scoreboard is one measurement and three silences. Only the price programme produced a filed number checkable against a forecast the company itself made in advance — and what it checked was a **cost, not a demand result**. The Associates Program produced the strongest *structural* evidence of repeatability available, 4,800 third-party storefronts live at 1996-12-31 reproducing Amazon's catalogue with none of Amazon's headcount or leases, and produced **no yield measurement at all**, its membership number degrading in precision across three successive filings. The out-of-print service proved that the company's expansion was *depth, not breadth*, and proved it by disclosing a two-to-six-month wait and a permanent "may not be available at all" inside an offering document. Bought traffic proved that demand was partly purchased and that the company named both its vendors and its vulnerability to them, while measuring nothing.

Read together, the four tests answer Stage 1's question and decline to answer Stage 2's. They establish that a firm of 11 → 256 people could put a licensed catalogue in front of ~80,000 visitors a day, take payment from ~340,000 cumulative accounts in over 100 countries, route each order to a wholesaler it had no contract with, and grow net sales five quarters running to a quarter larger than the whole prior year. They do not establish that any single order made money: the denominator "orders" was never published, so ">40% of orders from repeat customers" cannot be independently checked [S2B-64]; the only contribution concept the filings support is **+$59k on $15,746k** for FY1996 [S2B-63]; and every demand-side quantity is the seller counting itself [S2C-08]. *Would this still read as plausible if the company had failed five years later?* Yes — and it is close to what the company's own documents describe: a founder predicting profit "by accident" in December 1996; a registrant warning inside its own IPO document that its growth was unsustainable and its pricing programme would destroy margin; a book publisher with $79,000 of working capital, one uncontracted supplier, one under-insured building, no redundant systems, an unfiled valuation, a management team aged in months, personal guarantees still underwriting its card acceptance, a lawsuit filed three days before pricing whose disposition the record never discloses, and a price of $18.00 that proves only that someone was willing to pay it. **The Stage-2 verdict is therefore narrow and explicitly provisional: demand and revenue repeated; the economics were not shown to repeat; that test transfers to Stage 3 unperformed.**

### H.2 Knowability, and the record-selection null

**KNOWABLE in 1996–1997:** that US book retailing was structurally fragmented — two largest retailers **<25%** of US
sales, B&N superstores **≈9%** of the consumer market, ~50,000 publishers, "thousands of independent bookstores …

"barriers to entry are minimal," in the registrant's own words, echoed independently next day (D-82); and the cost of
postage, card processing and — by 1997-98 — paid placement. **NOT KNOWABLE:** the dollar size of **online book
retailing** (no category breakout exists; stage_1 gap G-11); whether the ~2.1m-title tail converted; whether the
discount schedule bought incremental demand (no incremental-sales figure was published); whether the single Seattle
building was near capacity; whether the 2.5m listing was operationally real; whether card reluctance was a measured
condition of Amazon's own buyers or a rival's and a registrant's characterisation; what media licensing would
require of a music store (**undocumented, not demonstrated**, S2C-G14). **UNKNOWN, and a finding:** the bibliographic
licensor; aggregator identities and amounts; the outcome of the B&N litigation; whether the founder's personal
guarantees were ever released (S2C-G11); the Associates Program start date (S2C-G9); MatchMaker's shipping status;
the **1996** discount schedule as a filed percentage (S2C-G15).

**Record-selection null (method §2), stated for this part.** What is unrecoverable about 1996–97 is mostly internal
and was never printed: no Amazon page survives for any date in the window, so no assortment, price, availability or
error state is observable at a date; no document discloses **how** "2.5 million titles" was counted, nor how

**fiscal year ending 1997-02-01**, which is not Amazon's. Without that paragraph a well-sourced reconstruction of a
survivor reads as a story about a future winner; with it, Stage 2 is a single-warehouse book router with an

consequence of its newest lever: **3.25 points of gross margin in a quarter**.

only sizing instruments available to a 1997 reader were the registrant's own Euromonitor quotation, third-party
panel counts with undisclosed methodology, and a competitor's 10-K on a different calendar — so structure (shares of
shelf, counts of stores, counts of publishers) was reliably knowable where dollars were not. *Alternative the record
cannot exclude:* that SIC 2731 was assigned mechanically from a fixed list and carries no analytic content at all, in
which case reading it as a market judgement would be an artefact of this reconstruction rather than of 1997.
*Confidence:* **High** on the header and on each string-counted differential; **Low** on any dollar total;
**UNKNOWN** on motive behind every disclosure change.
