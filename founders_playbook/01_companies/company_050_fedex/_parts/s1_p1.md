### Dataset, stage, and how to read this part

**Dataset:** The Founder's Playbook — forensic reconstruction of what each company in the frozen universe
(`00_universe/`) looked like while its outcome was still unknown: origin → first real-world experiment →
repeatable validation → scalable company formation.

**Hindsight firewall (§2).** Nothing here treats the eventual FedEx as evidence that a 1971–1978 decision was

## D — First experiment

STATUS: WRITTEN

**D.1 The experiment the record can date: the launch itself.** Federal Express "took flight on April 17, 1973"
(S4629 l.9217, company print, 1998); "FedEx began operations on April 17, 1973" (S4645 l.3422, NTSB factual
recital, 1998); "began U.S. domestic operations with a fleet of **14 Falcon jets** on April 17, 1973, and
expanded its domestic operations between 1973 and 1980" (S4646 l.2310, NTSB, 1997). Three late printings, two
independent origins (§3), one date. That is the first real-world test this dossier may name: a nightly air/ground
network put into scheduled public service on a stated day, with a stated initial airframe count, and an
expansion run measured by the same federal source as "between 1973 and 1980".

**D.2 What the launch demonstrated.** That a scheduled integrated network could be operated at all on a named
day, with jets in the air and packages delivered to 25 cities per the company's own account. A **capability**
demonstration, not a demand or unit-economics demonstration.

**D.3 What it did not demonstrate, and what the corpus cannot settle.** Whether anyone paid enough: no revenue,
cost, loss or load-factor figure exists for 1973-1978 anywhere on disk. Whether the volume claims are true: the
two available ones contradict (U.2), and the "seven packages" line is a 2009 relay. Whether the fleet was owned
or leased: the word "fleet" is unaccompanied by any title document. The anniversary copy's own anecdotes — "One
courier sold his watch to buy fuel for his van. Others used their own cars for deliveries. The folks in
Pittsburgh did business out of a motel room" (S4629 l.9218-9219) — are single-occurrence in the whole corpus and
unattributed: recorded as **promotional narrative**, not operational evidence.
