NORTHEAST INDUSTRIAL RECONSTRUCTION PROGRESSES IN 1950 -- Peking, Jen-min Jih- 
pao, 26 Jan 51 


During 1950, the Northeast , vernment invested 40 percent of ite budget 
in state-managed industrial reconstruction, This amounted to 77 percent of 
the economic investment budget for the total state-managed economy. Including 
ploy backs by various state-operated industries, reconstruction investment ex- 
ceeded the goal by 3.6 percent. New industrial building construction totaling 
2,448,556 square meters of floor space was carried out during the year. Of 
this amount, 1,570,000 square meters were in workers' dormitory space. 


Minerological investigation during 1950 resulted in the discovery of de- 
posits of two minerals hitherto unknown in the Northeast, white tungsten and 
“ nickel, as well as new deposits of coal, iron, and copper. New industrial re- 
search equipment inutalled has added from 10 to 880 percent to production capa- 
city, Eight thousand students enrolled in industrial schools have been supplied 


with much new experimental equipment. 


Weaknesses revealed have included poorly trained cadres, ambitious indus- 
trial expansion undertaken without knowledge of water supply, climate, soil 
properties, topography, or availability of raw materials or electric power. 

In some cases, there were not even Diueprints. The result has been delays or 
total abandonment of projects. In some cases, dormitory and welfare project 
construction was undertaken before factory construction. 


Poor budgeting, lackadaisical supervision, waste in top-heavy sdministra- 
tion, etc., have brought about wide variations in costs and lack of uniformity 


in results. 


The Northeast Ministry of Industry is endeavoring to make improvements by 
tightening budget policy, separating basic industrial planning units from the 
construction units, and requiring construction contracts. Improved statistical 
methods, better reporting, and training courses for basic workers and cadres 
are other measures undertaken to effect improvement.

FIND TUNGSTEN DEPOSITS IN NORTHEAST -- Shanghai, Te Kung Pao, 3 Jan 51 


During the summer of 1950, over 100 geologists, minerologists, and physi- 
cists from both China proper and the Northeast engageé in prospecting in poth 
South and North Manchuria, They held a conference in Mukden, 25 - 28 Septem- 
ber 1950, to report on their work and study the minerological situation in 
the Northeast, ‘The group reported new finds of coal, iron, and nonferrous 
metals, such as nickel, tungsten, and bentonite deposits.
