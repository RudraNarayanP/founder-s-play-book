VERMONT DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH 
Annual Report to the Citizens of Berkshire 

The established mission of the State Health Department is to protect and promote 
the health of all Vermonters. In working towards this goal, the Vermont Department 
of Health has provided directly, or assured the provision of, preventitive health services 
to communities across the state. During 1978, the following services were received by 
the residents of Berkshire: 

218 elementary school children were examined by a dental hygienist and received 

personal oral hygiene instruction twice during the school year. 

Your elementary school participated in the weekly fluoride rinse program. 


The Dental Program monitored the fluoridation equipment of the Berkshire 
Elementary School. 


Public Health Nurses made 47 individual public health vistits to 10 persons. 


26 children received preventive health examinations and counselling at Well Child 
Conferences. 


24 immunizations were given in Health Department clinics. 
54 doses of vaccine were supplied to school nurses and other public clinics. 


Through the Handicapped Children’s Program, diagnosis and treatment services for 
orthopedic, hearing, cardiac, and other conditions were provided to 15 children. 


Through the Child Development Clinic, 2 children received diagnostic and inten- 
sive follow-up services for suspected physical and/or mental retardation. 


The following services were provided by the Health Department Laboratory in 
Burlington: 


7 blood tests to identify women needing immunization against german measles 
(rubella) to prevent birth defects, 


45 other diagnostic blood tests, 
35 bacteriological and viral cultures, 


18 tests of individual and municipal water systems to monitor drinking water 
quality. 


Through the Emergency Medical Services Program, the Health Department pro- 
vided funds to help with the purchase of equipment for the Enosburg Rescue Squad 
and Richford Rescue Squad. 


14 people received nutrition education and food supplements through the Special 
Supplemental Food Program for Women, Infants and Children (WIC). 


22 children underwent medical evaluation and treatment through the Early Periodic 
Screening, Diagnosis, and Treatment Program (EPSDT). 


33 


8 women were examined for cervical and breast cancer through the Cervical Cancer 
Screening Program. 


Statewide, 278 emergency medical technicians were trained; 157,688 doses of vac- 
cine were distributed to private physicians; 17 hospitals and 48 nursing homes were in- 
spected and licensed; 3500 blood and breath samples were tested for alcohol content; 
2003 restaurants were inspected; 4266 venereal disease office visits and/or treatments 
were paid for and 820 contacts were traced; material to test 46,356 persons for tuber- 
culosis was distributed to private physicians, public clinics, and schools; 90 industrial 
inspections affecting 9,656 workers were done; 39 x-ray machines were inspected; 
treatment for 21,519 children was approved and paid for in total or in part by the Tooth 
Fairy and Denticaid programs; and, in conjunction with Planned Parenthood, 10,134 
persons received contraceptive counselling and service. 


Technical assistance was provided through the office of Local Health Services to 
your Town Health Officer, Velma Dodd, R.N. 


Your Town Clerk, Mrs. Wanda H. Kinney, received support and instruction in the 
handling of vital records from the Division of Public Health Statistics. 


Your participation in these programs is encouraged. Information about all Health 
Department services may be obtained by calling our toll free number, 1-800-642-5148, 
or your local district office in St. Albans. The local office telephone number is 524- 
2516.

TOWN ENERGY COORDINATOR 


With the constant pressure of rising energy costs and the possibility of there one day 
being an oil shortage relating to developments such as in Iran, it would be in all our best 
interests to consider appointing a Town Energy Coordinator as defined in Section 3, 24 
V.S.A., Chapter 23, Subchapter 12, paragraph 1131, enacted in 1976, 


To appoint a Town Energy Coordinator, the Act calls for the following article to be 
placed on the town meeting warning: 


‘*Shall the town authorize the selectmen to appoint for a one year term an 
energy coordinator.”’ 


A volunteer in this position can be a valuable resource person to town residents and 
a contact through whom the Vermont Energy Office can funnel information and ideas 
on energy matters. For example, the recently passed Federal Energy Act provides in- 
come tax rebates for homeowners and matching funds for energy conservation projects 
in schools, hospitals and municipal buildings.

BUDGETARY CONSIDERATIONS 

The increases in the budget are, in most cases, the result of inflation and tuition, 
which increases, also because of inflation. We are anticipating an increase of seven 
tuition students, which, with the increase in tuition noted above, accounts for the in- 
crease of $21,500.00 in the tuition in the budget. 


NEW CODING SYSTEM 


Our budget for the 1979-80 year must be based on the new State Coding System, ac- 
cording to Handbook II, revised. In order that there be no confusion on the part of the 
public, we have arranged our budget with both the traditional system and the new 
system. The new system may provide more pertinent information. All reports to the 
Vermont Department of Education, beginning in July of 1980, must be made using this 
new system. Therefore, after this year, our budget will be presented to the public 
utilizing only the new coding system 


KINDERGARTEN 


We have been very pleased with our kindergarten program this year. It is included in 
the regular budget. We feel that it has had a definite beneficial effect, and that we are 
giving our young children a better chance to achieve educational success through this 
program. 

We do receive one full daily membership for each kindergarten child, even though 
the child attends only a half-day of school and the teacher is paid only one-half of a 
regular teacher’s salary. This full daily membership is very beneficial to us under the 
Miller formula, which is used to compute our State Aid. 


CURRICULUM COORDINATOR’S REPORT 


Funds have increased over the past year, but have been spent in meeting inflation, 
raises in salary and fringe benefits. 
Health Services beyond Fiscal year 1979 will be funded 100% by local tax dollars.

Teacher Aides funded by Title I provide support to instruction in reading and 
mathematics in most classrooms. 

Many youngsters are receiving Speech, Hearing and/or Language Pathology ser- 
vices this school year under grants from the Division of Special Education (75%) and 
Title I (25%). Beginning next year, Title I will not fund this service, as it falls under the 
umbrella of P. L. 94-142--Education for All Handicapped Children. We are, hopefully 
in 1979-80, to have an audiologist in the district in conjunction with a federal project at 
the University of Vermont. 

Parent Advisory Councils are receiving greater support of eligible parents. 

September, 1978, brought us a new level of Special Education class, for older 
students. Our Supervisory Union now has classes for students 9-18 years of age. We 
are trying to establish classes for children 6-8 years of age, and for early essential 
education, ages 3-5. Much of these programs will be funded by either the division of 
Special Education and Pupil Personnel Services, State Department of Education, 
and/or the ‘“‘Flow-Through”’ dollars generated by the handicapped-child count required 
by the federal government under P. L. 94-142. 

Students whose parents are employed in agriculture or agriculturally-related in- 
dustries are counted under a Title I program for Migrant students. Where there are 10 
or more such students in a school, a tutor in reading and mathematics can be hired. 
Additional support and medical services are available.

-8. In the Fall of 1974, Reading programs were reviewed and adopted; 1975, 
Mathematics; 1976, Language; 1977, Social Studies; and in 1978, Science programs. 
All schools are phasing in different parts of each subject. All schools in the Supervisory 
Union will be using the same textbook in most cases. It is costly, but paramount that we 
keep our materials current and staff fully trained in meeting the needs of our students. 

Parents are encouraged to visit our schools and to share with us in the develop- 
ment of thier child. 


VOCATIONAL EDUCATION 


When this report was written last year, we were still searching for a direction of en- 
deavor for improved Vocational Education. At this time, we have at least determined 
that direction. Based on an affirmative vote in Enosburg, during November, 1978, the 
funds for construction of an area vocational center have been accepted. When 
Enosburg purchases land on which to construct the center, we will be en route. The 
doors could possibly open by September of 1980. 


The recommended programs, both secondary and adult, are as follows: 


. Agriculture (Ag. Production & Forestry/Conservation) 

. Power Mechanics (Small implement, Auto, & Ag. Mechanics) 

. Distributive Ed. (Retail and Wholesale Marketing) 

. Building Trades (Carpentry, Wiring, Cabinetry) 

. Electricity (Electricity, Appliance repair, Wiring; Electronics) 

. Business Education (Secretarial, Accounting, Office Procedures/ Management, 
and Introduction to Data Processing) 

7. Diversified Occupation (Level IV Special Education) 

8. Cooperative Education
