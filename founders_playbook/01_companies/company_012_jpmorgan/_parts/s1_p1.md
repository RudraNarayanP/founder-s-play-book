## A. EXECUTIVE STATE SUMMARY

At the close of the proposed Stage-1 window the corpus does not describe one company in one state; it describes **three sets of printed ancestries and one registrant name that reaches all three by adoption rather than by descent**. What can be stated, with its carrier:

**The Manhattan line (1799–1913 vintage print).** A New York water-supply incorporation, chartered by a bill passed 1799-04-02, capital **$2,000,000** at $50 par, with a clause "permitting the Company to employ all surplus capital in the purchase of public or other stock or in any other monied transactions or operations" (booklet l.122-126); **Aaron Burr** named as the moving spirit twice, by two rival publishers (l.117-118; `historyofchemica00chemuoft` l.412-422); directors elected 1799-04-11 at "the house of Edward Barden, Innkeeper" with "AARON BURR" on the list (l.156-170); **Daniel Ludlow chosen President** (l.177); a banking office opened **1799-09-01** in a house bought "on the site of the present No. 40 Wall Street" (l.228-231); first cashier **Henry Remsen** (l.243); dividends paid from **July 1800** and an aggregate of **$19,726,000** to and including January 1913 (l.273-275). The water business ran until roughly the Croton completion of 1842 (l.213-214). No held carrier prints this line failing, suspending, or merging inside the window; the 1955 act is not printed anywhere (§U.003).

**The Chemical line (1823–1913 print).** Incorporated 1823-02-24 at Albany "for twenty-one years, for 'The New York Chemical Manufacturing Company'" with an express anti-banking clause (l.432-439), re-named in the same volume's Part III as "The New York Chemical Manufacturing Company **without Banking Privileges**" (l.5959-5961); a **full banking charter dated April 1, 1824** (l.518-519); capital **$500,000** of which "$100,000 must be employed in the manufacture of chemicals" (l.5975-5977); first president **Balthazar P. Melick**, sworn 1824-08-06 (l.631-645); **John C. Morrison** as "one of the prime organizers" and practical chemist (l.6001-6004); the manufacturing plant bought for **$37,219** and abandoned in 1825, resold in 1834 for **$6,000** (l.5995, 6010-6019); **John Mason** — president from 1831, "not connected with the Bank's organization" (l.914-922) — who died **September 26 or 29, 1839** (§U.008); the **1837 suspension of specie payments** with the other New York City banks, quantified to the dollar (l.1746-1779); the 1844 re-charter with 28 subscriber names and a cashier at **$1,500 per annum** (l.2097-2110); the **first dividend, 6 per cent, declared 1848-12-26 and payable 1849-01-02** (l.2300-2312); and 1857, when it **declined to suspend** while the city resumed (l.3001-3006). This is the only ancestry with a printed failure inside the window, and the only one whose corporate line EDGAR can trace to the registrant.

**The Morgan line (1913 print only).** Its earliest and only first-party carrier in the corpus is a printed letter, dated **New York City, February 25, 1913** (l.37-39), from "Messrs. J. P. Morgan & Co." to a Congressional sub-committee (l.48-52) — a firm already established, answering questions about "concentration and control of money and credit" (l.55-59), signing as **Messrs** (a partnership), naming no beginning, no incorporation, no capital, and no other bank in the corpus: `Chase`, `Manhattan`, `Chemical`, `Guaranty` occur **zero** times in it as entity names (measured this pass, §T.4). Nothing in any held byte says when or how the Morgan house started, and the exact incorporated name **"J. P. Morgan & Co. Incorporated" has no carrier in any family searched** (probe §2, re-tested §2 of this file's own route list). Its in-window person namings arrive as wire items on Chinese-colonial pages (1936, 1937) and are contested at §U.004.

**The registrant at the window's edge.** The paper that ties a name to today's filer is not an 18th- or 19th-century act but the registrant's own late print: CIK 19617 filed as **CHEMICAL BANKING CORP** in 1994 (sidecar `COMPANY CONFORMED NAME`, `FORMER CONFORMED NAME: CHEMICAL NEW YORK CORP`, `DATE OF NAME CHANGE: 19880508`), self-described as **organized in Delaware in 1968**, and it prints its own later combinations — Manufacturers Hanover Corporation merged into it 1991-12-31, Manufacturers Hanover Trust into Chemical Bank 1992-06-19, and "On March 31, 1996, The Chase Manhattan Corporation ('heritage Chase') merged with and into Chemical Banking Corporation … Chemical Banking Corporation changed its name to 'The Chase Manhattan Corporation'" (§B.2 sources). **The surviving person was Chemical, and the name came from the acquired line** — the exact defect the method's rule 4 warns about, on this company's own bytes.

**Still broken at the ceiling:** the bridge from any ancestry to the registrant; the date of the "Chartered 1799" booklet's printing; which Pierpont Morgan the 1936/1937 items name; whether the Morgan house ever suspended; whether any 1799/1823 act was done by a legal person that can be identified; and every financial aggregate for 1799–1955 outside the handful of printed figures at §P.

**Failure remained plausible at every point in this window on evidence that exists.** In 1799 the charter was nearly lost "on account of this clause granting the Company banking privileges" (l.128); in 1824 the Senate vote for the Chemical bill was **22 to 8** against a public "as suspicious of banks then as they are of the so-called 'trusts' now" (l.390, 397-398); in 1837 the bank's specie fell from **$57,661 to $11,383** and a newspaper editor publicly wagered on its notes (l.1776-1815); in 1857 a trust company with **$2,000,000** paid-up capital failed completely and "its business was never resumed" (l.2778-2780) — a contemporaneous demonstration that an institution of this class and size could disappear. None of that is evidence about 1955 or about the modern registrant.

## C. ORIGINAL PROBLEM

**Adapted frame (method §7, spec §19 — financial).** For a licensed banking house the origin problem is not "what product do we sell" but the four problems the method assigns to this model — **licensing, underwriting, capital, trust** — and the corpus lets each be stated from a carrier rather than from general history. Sections C.1–C.4 run in that order; where the standard product-market frame does not apply, that is said rather than quietly dropped.

### D.2 Assessment

**What the experiments demonstrated, on evidence that existed then.** Two things, both narrow. First, that a franchise could be *obtained* by a purpose other than banking and then used for banking: the Manhattan line's own print shows the licence, the committee, the purchase of premises, an opening date, an advertised counter, a named cashier and a named president within five months of the bill. That is a completed transaction cycle, not a plan. Second, that a manufacturing corporation could carry a banking charter and be *tested in the market for its own product* — the Chemical's chemical goods were advertised and praised by a state medical society in 1824, and the bank's later credibility was tested in coin in 1837 and 1857.

**What they did not demonstrate.** Not that banking was the durable business: the water works were abandoned to Croton by 1842 and the chemical works were abandoned in 1825 and sold at a printed loss in 1834, so in both cases **the nominal purpose failed and the incidental one survived** — an observation about the carriers' selection, not a mechanism (mechanism UNKNOWN: the corpus contains no cost accounts, no water-rate revenue, no manufacturing profit line, and no board discussion of the choice). Not that anyone outside the firm's own circle would transact: there is no customer, deposit or account datum for 1799–1853 in the whole corpus (§F). Not that survival was the result of the device: a rival publisher with an interest in the opposite conclusion says the same founding act was a trick, and the same window prints a $2,000,000 trust company failing "complete[ly]" in 1857 with its business "never resumed" (history l.2778-2780) — a contemporaneous demonstration that institutions of exactly this class did disappear.

**Firewall check.** The passage above would read the same way if neither bank had survived to 2026, which is the test method §2 requires. It does not use the modern registrant as a reason for anything, and the 1951 polemic is not used as evidence at all.

### J.1 Mechanism discipline on the one causal claim in this section

The 1913 letter asserts a causal chain — statutory lending limit ⇒ concentration impossible for one bank ⇒ bank size must grow. **Evidence:** one party's printed submission to a Congressional committee, in the year of that committee's hearings. **Mechanism:** the 10% limit is printed; the inference from it to institutional size is the firm's reasoning, not an observation. **Alternative explanation available in the same corpus:** the same letter reports the *opposite* direction of travel for the city's share of national banking resources (23.2% → 18.9%, l.393-397), so concentration was falling by its own numbers while it argued that growth was inevitable — an inconsistency the firm does not reconcile. **Confidence:** Medium that the claim was made; **UNKNOWN** whether large-size growth in 1913-1955 was caused by the lending limit, by deal size, or by charter policy; no held carrier measures it.

## M. NEGATIVE SIGNALS / FAILURES

Banks fail by suspension, insolvency, resolution or loss of licence; the brief's warning is honoured here — **specie suspension in 1837 is written as what the carrier prints, not as what a bank panic is popularly said to mean**.

### M.1 The one documented failure inside the window: the Chemical Bank, 1837

What the held carrier prints, in order: the chapter head "THE FINANCIAL STORM OF 1837 / ALL SPECIE PAYMENTS SUSPENDED" (contents l.37-39; head l.1719-1721); the causes it names (Western land speculation, "State banks recklessly increased their paper currency", the BUS charter not renewed in 1836 and operations ceasing, the July 1836 "specie circular" requiring gold and silver for public lands — l.1723-1738); the market's verdict on the bank's own shares (124 → 98 → 102 → 112, l.1742-1744); the act: "the Chemical followed the other twenty New York City banks in the suspension of specie payments" (l.1746-1753); the balance-sheet effect (specie $57,661 → $11,383; circulation $266,969 → $194,634; "Its profits were only $69,068", l.1776-1779); and the contemporary anger, quoted at length from the *New York Herald*: "The banks of this city were the first to disgrace themselves in a body by suspending, during the midst of a ridiculous panic, widely produced by their own depositors in order to speculate in bullion for exportation. **There was no necessity for the suspension.**" (l.1769-1774).

**Boundary of the claim, stated four ways so it cannot travel.** (i) This is the **Chemical Bank's** suspension, narrated by a 1913 company history. It is **not** a suspension of the Bank of the Manhattan Company, of the Morgan house, or of JPMorgan Chase. (ii) The word "suspension" is used here exactly as the carrier uses it — the refusal to convert notes into coin on demand at that date — and this dossier draws **no** conclusion about depositor losses, note discounts, or the panics' wider economy, none of which the corpus measures. (iii) The volume's own judgment that the suspension was unnecessary is **a 1913 institution quoting an 1837 editor**; neither is an independent finding. (iv) The carrier itself frames 1837 as the bank's formative test, which is hindsight inside the source; it is recorded as the source's arrangement, not as this dossier's.

## U. CONFLICTING EVIDENCE

### U.001 — Manhattan Company capital: $2,000,000 or $2,050,000

**CLAIM A** — capital **$2,000,000** (booklet l.117-120, l.136-137; also the Chemical volume's c.1824 peer table, "Manhattan Company / 23 Wall Street / 1799 / 2,OOO,OOO", history l.543-549). **CLAIM B** — capital **$2,050,000** (the same volume's roll of 1853-09-29, history l.2405; and the booklet's own 1808 sentence, l.294-299). **WHY THEY DIFFER** — they are not the same object: the booklet prints an original chartered capital and then a **1808 increase** made when the Legislature reserved 1,000 shares to the State; the 1853 roll reports a later position. A third datum (the c.1824 table at 2,000,000) sits *after* the stated 1808 increase and therefore does not fit either account. **EVIDENCE WEIGHT** — two independent corporate-print origins, both retrospective; digits in both tables are OCR-exposed (the neighbouring Bank of New York row prints "1,50x3,000"). **BEST-SUPPORTED INTERPRETATION** — carry **both figures with their objects attached**: chartered/initial 2,000,000 (1799), post-1808 2,050,000, and note the internal arithmetic that makes the increase coherent (`d5`: 1,000 reserved shares × $50 par = $50,000 = the stated increase).
