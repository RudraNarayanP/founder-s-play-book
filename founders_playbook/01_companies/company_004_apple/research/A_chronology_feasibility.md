## Verdict (depth recommendation)

**Recommendation: exemplar depth (~22,500 words) is achievable for Apple Stage 1 — with a hard gate and one honest exclusion.** Unlike Walmart, Apple's founding period is *not* archive-empty: in a single probe this run recovered **38 dated records**, of which **20+ are Tier-1 and in-window (1975–1981)** with verbatim passages — including a **first-person technical article by Steve Wozniak published in May 1977** naming the Apple I's design date, its price band and its distribution channel; **Apple's own full-page $1,298/$598 Apple II advertisement (June 1977)**; a **December 1976 editorial naming "Steven Jobs (Apple Computer Co)" at WESCON in September 1976**; **two 1976 dealer advertisements proving Apple-1 retail stock by October–November 1976**; the **Homebrew Computer Club's own newsletters, fully digitised and dated 1975-03 → 1977-12**, in which the earliest Apple trace is a 1976-04-30 report thanking **Steve Wozniak** for bringing an Apple 6502 system to a club; a **February 1981 trade-press publication of the IPO terms and the 1978–1980 revenue/profit series and cap table**; and Apple's own **FY1994 filing sentence fixing incorporation at 1977-01-03 under California law**, independently corroborated in 1981 print. The bulk corpus is already on disk (~12 MB of 1976–81 magazine OCR) and cheaply mineable, and the period's *primary artifacts* — the 3-page **partnership agreement dated 1976-04-01 (Christie's, sold 2026-01-23 for $2,515,000)**, **Jobs's 1973 handwritten job application** (Charterfields, March 2021, $222,400), and **Paul Terrell's own 1976 Polaroids of the first Apple-1 showing at the Byte Shop** — survive publicly photographed.

The exclusion is **money and organisation, not events**. There is **no Apple SEC text before 1994-01-26** (EDGAR floor established; full-text-search floor 2001), so the 1980 prospectus is paper-only; **no founding-era financial document of any kind surfaced** — no invoice from the Byte Shop, no lease, no payroll, no bank loan paper, no signed Markkula term sheet, and **no contemporaneous figure at all for April–October 1976 sales**. The fleet must therefore write an exemplar that is **press-, artifact- and product-shaped rather than filing-shaped**, and hold the financial interior at UNKNOWN. **Gate:** commit exemplar only after fleet briefs A (Wayne/Christie's full text + RR/Charterfields catalogues), C (Kilobaud + Creative Computing + InfoWorld 1977–1981 IPO text) and E (California SoS / Santa Clara County records) each return or formally exhaust; if fewer than three of those five named targets land primary text, **downgrade to forensic-core (~6,000)** rather than pad with iWoz/Isaacson memoir — the method forbids it, and padding here would be easy and fatal. Record count on this probe: **38 AP-records** (AP-01 … AP-38), all with source URLs and, where retrieved, verbatim passages.

**Fit against spec §6 (origin → first real-world experiment → repeatable validation → scalable formation):** Apple can carry all four, and better than either prior probe. **Origin** = Jobs/Wozniak's pre-1976 activity, of which the retrievable public trace is thin-but-real (a 1973 dated document; Blue Box and Atari remain memoir-only). **First experiment** = the Apple I board: dated by its designer in his own 1977 print account and independently corroborated by the Homebrew newsletter of 1976-04-30 and BYTE of October–November 1976 (dealer stock). **Repeatable validation** = the Apple II with a published, sourced price and configuration (Apple's own June 1977 advertisement) and an established multi-state dealer network. **Scalable formation** = the January 1977 corporation plus the 1980 IPO terms in print. What §6 **cannot** be given for Apple is the *financing interior*: the first outside investor's terms, the venture round's documents and the first year's revenue have no surviving retrievable text, so the "repeatable validation" rung rests on orders, advertisements and price, not on accounts.

## Conflicts register (for section U of the dossier)

U-AP-1 **Founding date.** (a) Partnership agreement **dated April 1, 1976** (Christie's-catalogued primary document, AP-20); (b) **incorporation January 3, 1977**, California (Apple's own 10-K, AP-04; corroborated in 1981 print, AP-19); (c) the universe's feasibility register and Wikipedia give "1976-04-01, Los Altos". / WHY THEY DIFFER: three different legal events — partnership, corporation, and a place-of-formation gloss — collapsed into one "founded" date; the register's *place* (Los Altos) is itself unsourced here, while 1976–77 print places the company in **Cupertino** (20863 Stevens Creek Blvd, AP-16/17) and the club correspondence in Mountain View/Sunnyvale. / EVIDENCE WEIGHT: (a) and (b) are both primary; (c) is derivative. / BEST-SUPPORTED: *formed as a partnership 1976-04-01; incorporated 1977-01-03; "Los Altos" is not established by anything retrieved here.* / RESIDUAL: the exact signing place and the partnership's business address UNKNOWN. / CONFIDENCE: High on both dates, Low on place.

U-AP-2 **The first order.** Canonical: "50 boards at $500, then another 50" from the Byte Shop, July 1976. Retrieved set: zero documents; Wozniak 1977 says word-of-mouth then retail stores; Wayne says "a large number"; 1976 print shows only that dealers stocked the Apple-1. / WHY THEY DIFFER: a vivid number preserved by memoir and repeated without citation. / EVIDENCE WEIGHT: all retrospective. / BEST-SUPPORTED: *an early wholesale arrangement with Terrell's Byte Shop almost certainly existed (Terrell and Jobs appear as peers in Dec 1976 print; Terrell kept 1976 Polaroids of the founders showing him the machine), but unit counts and price are undocumented.* / RESIDUAL: High — this is the probe's biggest unresolved chronology item. / CONFIDENCE: Low.

U-AP-3 **Working capital to fill that order.** Wayne: a **$15,000** loan (AP-29). iWoz lineage: **$1,000** raised by selling an HP-65 for $500 and a VW bus for $750 (AP-32). Every history site gives some variant. / WHY THEY DIFFER: two different purposes (board printing vs filling an order) conflated; both retrospective. / EVIDENCE WEIGHT: none Tier-1. / BEST-SUPPORTED: UNKNOWN; the only clean resolution is bank/paper records or a dated prospectus "History" section. / CONFIDENCE: Low.

U-AP-4 **Wayne's percentage.** Agreement as catalogued: 10% (AP-20). Circulating accounts: 12%. / WHY THEY DIFFER: 45/45/10 sums to 100; "12%" is arithmetically impossible against it and appears to be a later distortion, possibly from the second-round 80/20 split lore. / BEST-SUPPORTED: 10% — but only until the lot text is read directly. / CONFIDENCE: Medium.
