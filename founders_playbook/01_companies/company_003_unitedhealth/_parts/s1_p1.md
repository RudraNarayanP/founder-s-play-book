### 4. Basis of numerals — the rule that governs every table in this volume

Every quantity below states its carrier and its basis, and four distinctions are enforced on every
row, because this registrant's own documents blur them:

1. **Fiscal vs calendar.** The registrant's fiscal year is the calendar year (`FISCAL YEAR END:
   1231`, SGML header of the FY1994 accession). "1994" therefore means FY1994 = calendar 1994. Where
   a figure is a January point-in-time (Jan 31, 1995 enrollment; the Jan 1995 column of Schedule F)
   it is labelled as such and is **not** a FY1994 figure.
2. **Period-end vs average vs month-by-month.** Enrollment in `EX-13` is *at year end*; shares
   outstanding are *weighted average*; the aggregate market value on the 10-K cover is *as of
   March 13, 1995*. These are four different time bases and never mix in one comparison here.
3. **Net vs gross vs face, and the pooling restatement.** FY1990-FY1994 figures in `EX-13` are
   **restated for all periods** to include Complete Health and Ramsay-HMO (acquired **1994-05-31**,
   pooling of interests; note at l.7464-7467), and share/per-share amounts are restated for the
   **two-for-one split of 1994-02-23**. The 8-K's Schedule E additionally restates for **HMO
   America, merged 1993-08-31**. So FY1990-1992 columns are *not* as originally reported for those
   years and must be labelled **RESTATED**, never CONTEMPORANEOUS.
4. **Members vs lives vs individuals vs participants.** `3,248,800` is health-plan **members/enrollees**
   at 12/31/1994; `approximately 23 million participant lives` is specialty products **availability**,
   and the filing says in terms that one person may count more than once (l.539-543); `over 40
   million individuals` is a **1997 web** figure. Adding or comparing them is the classic
   time-audit trap (§6) and is done nowhere in this volume.

**`Earnings before extraordinary item` vs `net earnings` is the one place the registrant itself
disagrees with the label.** FY1994 net earnings are printed as `$1,665,214` thousand including a
`$1,377,075` thousand extraordinary gain on the Diversified sale; the same year's "before the
non-operating merger costs and the extraordinary gain" figure is `$310,422` thousand — **5.4×**
the gap between them is one asset sale, and the press release headline leads with the smaller,
operating number. Every row here names which of the three it is.

### A.3 What this stage is, and what it is not

Stage 1 for this registrant is **not** the story of a product finding a market. It is the story of an
**administrative capability being repeatedly re-deployed**: the same claims-processing, contracting,
underwriting and information-systems machinery installed behind a new local plan in a new city, then
converted from a fee-for-management relationship into majority ownership when the risk was worth
holding. The FY1994 filing draws that line itself, in the only structural sentence it offers, and it
is the most load-bearing sentence in this volume: on owned plans *"the Company assumes underwriting
risk in return for the premium revenue it receives"*, while on managed plans it *"receives an
administrative fee for providing its services and generally assumes no responsibility for health
care costs"* (l.177-184). Everything in §P is organised on that distinction.

What Stage 1 is **not**, on this evidence: it is not a documented founding. There is no held first
transaction, no held first customer, no held first premium, no held first network contract, and no
held account of how the 1977 corporation related — if at all — to the "Charter Med" of 1978 print or
of the 2022 newspaper story. The origin of this company is, forensically, a **1995 filing that says
"January 1977" plus a 1978 federal pamphlet that says "Charter Med, Minneapolis"**, and nothing
between them. That is stated as the finding it is.

### C.1 The original problem, stated by the only entities that stated it

For a health-plan manager the "original problem" is not a customer's unmet want; it is a **payer's
cost and a provider's income**, and the held record states both sides of it in period language that is
independent of this company.

The cleanest in-window statement of the demand side is the registrant's own, and it is a general
claim about the market rather than about itself: *"The managed health care industry evolved primarily
as a result of health care buyers' concerns regarding rising health care costs. The industry's goal is
to infuse greater cost effectiveness and accountability into the health care system through the
development of managed care products, including health plans, PPOs, and specialized services such as
mental health or pharmacy benefit programs, while increasing the accessibility and quality of health
care services."* (FY1994 10-K405, COMPETITION, l.797-803.)

The supply side — what a physician organisation was actually trying to buy in the 1970s — is captured
by the one contemporaneous federal text held for this window. A 1978 serial of the US Office of Health
Maintenance Organizations, matched at page `RA7`, prints, in the snippet held:

> "… Charter Med , Minneapolis , which manages ten IPAs around the coun- try . " Certainly lenders will
> look more fa- vorably on HMOs managed by us or some- one like us in terms of their willingness to put
> up money . ""

Three things are inside that window of text and each does different work. First, a **Minneapolis
firm called Charter Med existed in 1978 and was a manager of ten IPA-model groups** — it was not, in
this sentence, an HMO operator in one town, it was a multi-market management company. Second, the
speaker identifies the scarce good as **financeable management**: lenders' willingness to put up money
depended on *who managed the HMO*. Third, it is a quotation — and **the speaker is not named in the
held bytes**, so no attribution may be made (A2's conflict 5 is preserved here: do not put Burke's
name around this sentence).

The undated companion snippet in the same held feed lists Charter Med *of Minneapolis* alongside
*"Medserco of St. Louis, Hancock, Dikewood of Albuquerque, and Healthplans Corporation of Nashville"*
in a sentence about **for-profit HMO managers**, adding "46 for-profit plans are in operation, 25 of
which are federally…" — a named cohort of for-profit managers, of which Charter Med is one, and an
industry denominator. **The date of that item is UNKNOWN** and must not be pushed into the 1970s; the
volume and page (`ztWc6Ufs6qEC`, PA5) are recorded so the page can be pulled.

### K.4 What §K cannot answer, and why it is the largest hole in the volume

**Nothing in the held record states what the venture earned, raised, or lost in 1974-1989 other than
through the restated FY1990 column and the retrospective "since 1974".** Concretely:

* **No pre-1990 figure of any kind is held.** The FY1990 revenue line ($1,056,019 thousand) is the
  earliest number that exists anywhere in these bytes, and even it is printed under a pooling
  restatement. Twelve to sixteen years of operating and financing history sit to its left with no
  carrier at all.
* **The IPO is undocumented**: no registration statement, no price, no date, no proceeds. The one
  dated public-capital event is the March-1992 offering, and it is not the IPO.
* **The Warburg, Pincus placement is undocumented in amount.** The articles give the rights and the
  preferred's redemption ceiling; they do not give the subscription price, the number of shares, or
  the date the money moved. The 1998 S-4 prints "Convertible Preferred Stock Dividends … $(29)"
  million for FY1996 and FY1997 and `$--` for FY1993 and FY1994, which dates the **dividend
  obligation's appearance** to FY1995 and not to 1988 — so even the preferred's own economics in the
  1988-94 years remain unseen.
* **Burke's personal money at the founding is unknown.** His 1995 holding of 1,012,918 shares is the
  earliest personal-wealth datum in the corpus; how much of it, or of the company, he acquired in
  1977, and at what cost, is not stated anywhere. The Star Tribune's 2022 account that he "agreed to
  sell almost half his stock to … Warburg, Pincus Capital Co." when he stepped down as CEO is a
  **48-year-later** report of a private transaction, its percentage is not tied to any named share
  count, and the Form 4/5 series that could price it has not been reached.

A coda that meets §7's interpretive-coda duty: the shape of the money record is itself evidence about
the archive, not about the enterprise. What survives for 1974-1988 is a **charter instrument and a
newspaper's memory**; what survives for 1990-1994 is a complete audited series. That asymmetry exists
because a public company files, not because the later years were better managed — and reading the
onset of hard numbers as the onset of substance would import exactly the hindsight contamination §2
forbids.

### L.2 The strongest validation signal is the one that cuts against the story

column and positive in the owned column at the same time**. Between FY1992 and FY1994 the owned-plan

named health plan will expire in 1995", attached to four named managed plans, and with the separate
disclosure that "the change in the UHC-Allina management contract effective August 1994" distorted
1994 comparisons — the record shows a company whose *original* business (managing other people's
plans) was **shrinking as a share of revenue while its acquired-ownership business grew**. That is
validation of the owned-plan model, and simultaneously a warning that the model being validated was
not the model the company had started with — whatever the founding story turns out to be.

### M.2 The internal contradiction found inside a held primary document, and how it resolves

Schedule F of the 1995-02-14 8-K prints, on its TOTAL-Medicaid row: `285,000 · 284,000 · 332,000 ·
241,000`, with percentage-change columns of `16.5%` and `20.1%`. The first column checks

printed beside it.** The FY1994 10-K405, in the same accession, gives the January 31, 1995 Medicaid

owned and managed Medicaid cells for January 1995 are `291,000` and `50,000` = **341,000**. The
printed `241,000` is therefore contradicted by its own row, by its own percentage column, and by a
second document in the same filing; **341,000 is the best-supported January-1995 total Medicaid
figure**, and `284,000 → 341,000 = +20.1%` reproduces the printed percentage exactly. This is recorded
as `U.016` and the register row carries the printed value with the correction inside the same cell,
per §14 rule 8's retraction discipline — the value is not quietly rewritten.

**Lesson kept on the record:** the defect is in the **held bytes**, not in this volume's reading of
them, and it was only found because the same quantity appears twice in one accession on two bases.
A single-document citation of Schedule F would have transmitted a −15% movement as a +20% one.
