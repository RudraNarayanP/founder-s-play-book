April 16th, 1915. 



San Diego Traveler's Aid Society, whose directorate 
you know and with whose work of mora,l protection and assistance 
of* women and girls in the emergencies incident to travel you 
are familiar, will interest you. 

Cases of runaway girls and "boys, unscrvipulous and dan- 
gerous friendships and acquHintancJ^s, hunger, 'bewilderment, em- 
ploynent, lodgings, lost friends, money, wrong addresses or none 
at all, desertion, illness — these aro typical of the 618 per- 
sons, assisted during March, the majority women and girls travel- 
ing alone. The need during April is even greater. 

The Society through its trained women agents meeting all 
trains and "boats, working on the Exposition, in social service 
investigation and housing, protects and assists all travelers in 
need, without regard to race, class, creed or sex and without fee 
or gratuity. 

Endorsed "by Catholic, Jewish, Protestant, and many or- 
ganizations throughout the city, the work needs your support now, 
especially when so many thousands are coming to our city. 

Practical and efficient protection and aid in the emer- 
gency, prevention of error and crime, relief of suffering, safe- 
guarding the innocent, ignorant, inexperienced and helpless-- this 
is Travelers' Aid, It is your work--it urgently requires your 
interest and support. Will you help "by sending a contribution? 

Very sincerely yours. 



Executive Secretary

May 25th, 1915. 



Your knowledge of social conditions will interest you in 
the protection the Travelers* Aid Society provides at the station 
and docks as descrihed in the enclosed folder. The Society has 
trained workers on duty to give advice, guidance and protection, or 
other assistance to the inexperienced a>id bewildered, the stranger, 
and the foreigner who cannot speak our language. 

Wrong addresses, insufficient funds, the failure of 
friends to meet them, and a multiplicity of emergencies are every 
day occurences that "bring travelers under dangerous influences. 

The San Diego Society through its wide co-operation with 
other Travelers* Aid Societies and its relationship with other or- 
ganizations and individuals in cities and rural comfaiinities from 
coast to coast, is protecting hundreds of travelers from the con- 
ditions tloat would otherwise imperil thera. This service is abso- 
lutely free. Gratuities are not accepted from those assisted. 

The San Diego Society assisted 618 persons in March; 
973 in April; and in the present month -iTill assist approximately 
1200 persons. These figures can not adequately u-onvey any degree 
of the sufferiiTjg avoided or relieved. Without the timely assist- 
ance of the Travelers* Aid Society many of this number 7/ould have 
lost everything that makes life worth while. 

Because Travelers' Aid is giving protection and assist- 
ance to so many, it ne'-^ds the aid and financial backing of many 
friends. The character of the service rendered to individuals 
and to society at large justifies our asking for your support. 
Will you not help by giving $1, - $10., by giving $10. - $25. 
for the prevention of wrong and the relief of suffering? Checks 
should be made payable to Mr. John W. Snyder, Treasurer, and 
will be appreciated and promptly acknowledged. 



Very sincerely. 

Executive Secretary

Semi -Annual Statement 
(Active work Feb. -July inclusive) 

San Diego Travelers' Aid Society during the half year of 
active work has, with the splendid support of Dr. F. R. Burnham, 
President, the Executive Committee, and Directorate, assisted mort 
than 6,500 persons, with a present monthly average of 2200 per- 
sons helped. During this time the work has been fully organized 
and developed; a corps of workers trained to meet tHe need; daily 
individual and weekly group conferences held; cooperation widely 
and firmly established; system of case reports installed; a re- 
cord established and maintained for minimum per capita expense; 
extensive investigations made; an educational and publicity cam- 
paign carried on; station rest room furnished; and general en- 
dorsement and public recognition of the society obtained. 

The Secretary especially acknowledges the faithful and 
efficient work of the agents, and the cooperation of Travelers' 
Aid Societies of America. 

Respectfully submitted, 
Horace R. Baker, 

Executive Secretary.

During the month the Society confronted a financial crisis, 
and the Executive Committee voted to cut down the work one half, 
discharging two of the workers. As the work has no source of in- 
come, it is imperative that the directorate and friends assist in 
financing it. 

Two new directors were elected:- Mrs. Charles E. Spaulding and 
Miss Alice Lee. Mr. Milton Epstein was elected Acting Treasurer 
in the absence of Mr. Snyder, treasurer. 

The Secretary acknowledges the friendly spirit of cooperation 
existing between the Society and the social, humanitarian and 
charitable organizations of the city.
